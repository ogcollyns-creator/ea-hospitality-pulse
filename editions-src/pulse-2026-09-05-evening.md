## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**Kenya is proposing to move its tourism statistics inside its tourism marketing agency. The whole region should be watching.**

The Tourism (Amendment) Bill 2026 sits with the National Assembly's Departmental Committee on Tourism and Wildlife, in public participation. It would transfer the functions, assets, liabilities, pending legal proceedings and licences of the Tourism Research Institute to the Kenya Tourism Board, ending TRI as a separate institution, with its staff deployed to the Public Service Commission. The Tourism Finance Corporation is dissolved on the same terms. That is the reported detail from the committee sitting of 20 August 2026, at which Tourism CS Rebecca Miano and PS Julius Bitok appeared (The Kenya Times, 21 August 2026).

**THE SITUATION**

Three changes travel together in one Bill, and they are usually discussed apart.

Collection: the Tourism Fund keeps collecting hotel and restaurant levies, with the Cabinet Secretary overseeing management. Bitok's stated model was KRA collecting and the Central Bank holding, with allocation through Treasury.

Mandate: the Fund's remit widens to product development, events, safety, research, data collection, tourism intelligence, training, innovation, standards, and county co-financing through matching grants — plus "marketing and branding Kenya". It would also draw a new revenue line, proceeds from the Air Passenger Service Charge.

Statistics: TRI, which publishes Kenya's Annual Tourism Sector Performance Report, ceases to exist, and its work moves inside KTB.

The trade has pushed back. Fred Odek of the Kenya Association of Tour Operators warned of a "data vacuum" and wants a statutory research directorate plus explicit protection for TRI's databases through the transition. Barry Clemens of Hospitality EQ raised concentration of power, noting the Tourism Finance Corporation was itself folded into Kenya Development Corporation only five or six years ago (Tourism Update, 26 August 2026). Committee chair Kareke Mbiuki has said TRI is critical and should not be lost in the tidying-up.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

The public argument is about governance. The commercial exposure is narrower, and it is not confined to Kenya.

TRI's arrivals-by-source-market series is the most granular tourism dataset published free anywhere in East Africa. Its 2025 report put international arrivals at roughly 2.7 million, up about 9% on 2024 (reported 2 April 2026). Ugandan, Tanzanian and Rwandan operators use it — not out of loyalty, but because there is nothing better on their own side of the border, and commercial benchmarking coverage thins fast outside Nairobi. If Kenya's reference series moves inside a promotional agency, the region loses its yardstick, not just Kenya.

International practice points the other way. Under the UN's International Recommendations for Tourism Statistics, a country's system of tourism statistics sits inside the national statistical system. A marketing board is not that.

Then the mechanical problem, which nobody has raised. TRI's reports are hosted on TRI's own government domain. Dissolve the institution and the domain goes with it. Every feasibility study, credit paper, valuation and rate model in this region citing one of those links loses its source — quietly, and at the moment someone senior asks where the number came from.

An inference, labelled as one: none of this arrives as a headline. It arrives as a wider discount in a credit committee, from people who no longer treat the arrivals number as independent.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

**Everyone, this week.** Download every TRI publication your models, decks or lender packs rely on, and write the source and retrieval date into the document. Twenty minutes now; impossible retrospectively.

**Kenyan city properties.** You fund this. The levy is yours, the expanded mandate now includes branding, and spending priorities sit with the Cabinet Secretary. Public participation is the one moment your view is on the record. Ask for two things: continuity of the published arrivals series with its methodology intact, and a permanently accessible archive.

**Bush and beach.** Your exposure is the feasibility study and the lender. Anyone underwriting an expansion in the next eighteen months should build the citation trail now.

**The rest of the region.** Rwanda already publishes tourism revenue and arrivals through the Rwanda Development Board, the body that also markets the destination. If Kenya converges on that arrangement, the region's two most-quoted series both sit inside promotional agencies — and the private benchmarks that fill the gap are not free.

**THE WATCH**

Whether the enacted text carries a data-continuity clause. If it passes without one, the first practical signal will be mundane: a TRI link in an old deck that stops resolving.

Every operator here prices off a number somebody else publishes. It is worth knowing who that somebody is about to become.
