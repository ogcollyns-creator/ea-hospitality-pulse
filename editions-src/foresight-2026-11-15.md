Our inference, from one precedent: Gulf demand for Kenya returns in late Q1 2027, not at Christmas.

## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**Kenya's Ebola all-clear is due on 23 December. Uganda shows the markets reopen months later.**

If you run a hotel or lodge in Kenya, you now have a date. The last Ebola patient left hospital on 11 November, and if no new case appears the country can be declared Ebola-free on 23 December. The risk is that the date goes into your sales plan as the day demand returns. Uganda's record says it will not.

**THE SITUATION**

Kenya recorded nine Ebola cases in October; two patients died and seven recovered (Health Ministry, via Africanews, 11 November). The 42-day countdown began with the final discharge.

The restrictions it triggered run on their own terms. Saudi Arabia suspended all visas for travellers arriving from Kenya on 30 October and barred its citizens from travelling there, with no stated end condition (Saudi Press Agency, via Citizen Digital). The US State Department raised Kenya to Level 3, "reconsider travel", on 19 October.

Uganda went through the same sequence earlier. Its Health Ministry declared its outbreak over on 28 July. WHO confirmed in the last week of August. The UAE, which had suspended visas for Ugandans in June, eased its measures on 26 September and resumed new visas on 11 November, on condition that travellers have been out of Uganda for at least 21 days (The National, 11 November). On 9 November the State Department reissued Uganda's Level 4 "do not travel" rating, and on 10 November the CDC renewed its entry order covering Uganda to 10 December. Two days later a Ugandan minister told Parliament that talks with Washington had not changed the rating.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

The 42-day clock is the shortest of four. After it come WHO's confirmation, each government's own review, and the insurers and corporate travel policies that key off those reviews. Demand runs on the slowest of them.

The arithmetic from Uganda is plain. The UAE's first easing came 60 days after the national declaration; full visas came 106 days after it. Apply those gaps to 23 December and Kenya's first Gulf easing falls in late February, with full reopening in early April. That is our inference from a single precedent, not a forecast any government has made. It could be quicker, since Kenya's cases were imported and few.

The US clock is different again, and here Kenya may do better than Uganda. Uganda's Level 4 is held by the epidemic in neighbouring Congo, which passed 11,042 confirmed cases and 5,387 deaths on 11 November (Al Jazeera, citing government data). No Ugandan all-clear can fix that. Kenya does not border Congo. But its 19 October rise was also reported to cite protests as well as Ebola, so an Ebola-free declaration removes only one of the grounds.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

Coast and city hotels courting Gulf travellers: take Saudi and wider Gulf leisure out of the December to February forecast. Write 2027 offers that trigger on the lifting of the Saudi suspension, not on 23 December.

Safari lodges selling to the US: Level 3 is what insurers and corporate policies read. Sell free date changes, not discounts, and do not tell US agents the declaration changes the rating.

Uganda gorilla lodges: the CDC order runs to 10 December and Level 4 tracks Congo, not Uganda. Put marketing effort behind UK and EU agents. The UK advises against travel to no part of Uganda.

Everyone: keep a register of each restriction, its stated lifting condition and its date. Launch campaigns when a measure lifts, market by market.

**THE WATCH**

10 December, when the CDC order expires. 23 December, Kenya's earliest Ebola-free date. Any Saudi Press Agency notice on Kenya. The next State Department reissue for Kenya.

An all-clear ends an outbreak. It does not reopen a market, and the owners who price for the gap will not have to discount into it.
