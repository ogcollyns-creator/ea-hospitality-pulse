Uganda was declared Ebola-free on 28 July and completes its 42-day all-clear around 28 August. Operators waiting for "the ban to lift" to restart marketing are watching the wrong dial. The dial that matters won't move on 12 August, and it may not move for months.

So stop selling to the timeline of an all-clear that isn't coming. Move budget to the demand that no Western risk desk governs.

## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**Uganda is Ebola-free. It will still be "Do Not Travel" after 12 August — and that should change how you spend your marketing budget.**

If you run a lodge, camp or hotel that depends on Uganda — or on the region's reputation halo — this week handed you a strategy lesson disguised as a public-health headline.

**THE SITUATION**

Uganda was declared Ebola-free on 28 July 2026 and completes its 42-day all-clear around 28 August. Next door, DRC's Bundibugyo outbreak passed 4,000 confirmed cases this week — the largest in DRC's history and the second-largest ever recorded, spread across 49 health zones with a fatality rate near 44% and no approved vaccine for the strain (Al Jazeera / WHO, 7 August). The US CDC's Section 362 entry order, last renewed 13 July on a 30-day cycle, faces its next decision on or about 12 August. And Uganda, despite its clean bill, remains under a US Level 4 "Do Not Travel" advisory (ChimpReports / Uganda MoH, late July–August).

For six weeks our daily brief has reported the same line: the advisory board hasn't moved. Day-to-day, that reads as non-news. It isn't.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

Two instruments are routinely treated as one, and the distinction decides your quarter.

The CDC entry order suspends the entry of certain *foreign nationals into the United States* who were recently in DRC, Uganda or South Sudan. It explicitly exempts US citizens and nationals. Americans make up roughly 18–20% of Uganda's non-African visitors — and not one of them is legally barred from flying to Entebbe today. The instrument everyone is counting down to on 12 August was never the thing suppressing Uganda's most valuable source market.

The instrument that is suppressing it is the State Department Level 4 advisory. That is what triggers travel-insurance exclusions, corporate duty-of-care bans and agent risk thresholds. And it runs on a different clock entirely — coupled to DRC's epidemiology and to Uganda's broader crime and security factors, not to Uganda's own Ebola status. It will not flip on 12 August because Uganda's countdown ends on 28 August. A clean country is paying a penalty of proximity, and its recovery timeline is being set externally, by the dirtiest case on its border.

The connection the news cycle never draws: operators are pacing their marketing to the timeline of a policy all-clear that is not arriving on their schedule. They are watching the wrong dial.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

Uganda operators: stop gating your relaunch on the advisory. Redirect spend now toward demand that no Western risk desk designates — intra-African travel (a structural pillar, not a fallback, and riding visa-free momentum), the Gulf, and European markets whose advisories already diverge from Washington's. For the US market specifically, sell to the exempt reality: your American guest can travel, so the barrier is perception. Arm agents with dated specifics — Ebola-free since 28 July, gorilla parks hundreds of kilometres from any affected zone — because the blunt label cannot make those distinctions and you can.

Kenya, Tanzania, Zanzibar and Rwanda operators: the same regional halo that lifts you in good years drags on you in bad ones. Do not assume a US buyer separates "East Africa" cleanly. Lead with destination-specific facts and lean into the source markets that book on their own read of risk, not on a US advisory.

Everyone: build the "Ebola-free" collateral this week and date it, ready to fire the moment Uganda's 42-day countdown closes around 28 August. Have it queued, not drafted.

**THE WATCH**

Around 12 August the CDC renews or lifts its order — assume renewal with Uganda still listed, given DRC's trajectory. On 18 August the WHO IHR Emergency Committee reviews the PHEIC. On 15 August EPRA resets Kenyan fuel and transfer costs. Watch which advisory moves first — and note that the one that matters most for Uganda, the State Department's, is the one least likely to.
