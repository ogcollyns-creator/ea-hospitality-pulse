## LINKEDIN

In market intelligence the most expensive mistake isn't missing a story. It's republishing an old one as if it were new.

On 25 July we made a falsifiable call: that Kenya's domestic press would report the 29 July re-issue of the US travel advisory by 3 August. This morning we checked. It hasn't happened.

What's circulating instead is instructive. A Citizen Digital article warning about the Nairobi neighbourhoods of Eastleigh and Kibera — published 18 March 2025 — has been silently re-stamped "modified 2 August 2026" and is resurfacing in search results as current news. Its content is the previous advisory. We read the primary source ourselves: the US State Department page still carries a revision date of 17 March 2025 and still lists Eastleigh and Kibera as Reconsider Travel, not Do Not Travel.

For operators this matters concretely. When a guest or travel agent forwards a "new" Kenya security warning this week, the byline date is the whole story. The US position is unchanged — Level 2, with two Nairobi pockets at Reconsider Travel. There is no basis to discount rate or rewrite reassurance emails on security grounds this week.

The lesson holds well beyond Kenya: a modified timestamp is not a publication date, and a search engine ranking a page first is not the same as that page being new.
