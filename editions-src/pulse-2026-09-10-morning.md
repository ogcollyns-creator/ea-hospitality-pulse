## LINKEDIN

A schedule filing landed overnight that most East African operators will read as a route announcement. It isn't. It's a clock.

IndiGo will fly Mumbai–Dar es Salaam daily from 20 November on an A320neo. The inbound arrives at 06:05. The outbound leaves at 07:05.

Neither of those times works with a standard hotel day. An arrival before dawn, 365 days a year, is a guest standing in a lobby at 06:30 with nowhere to go. A 07:05 departure is a guest who must already be in a Dar bed the night before.

That is two bed-nights per rotation, generated by the timetable rather than by the destination — and today most properties in the city treat both as an inconvenience rather than a product. Guaranteed early check-in, priced and sold at the point of booking. A pre-departure night packaged with a 04:30 transfer. Neither requires capital. Both require deciding before the seats go on sale.

The wider point for Zanzibar and the northern circuit: this is Indian leisure and MICE demand routing through Dar on a narrowbody daily, not through a Gulf hub. Distribution relationships built for Gulf-fed traffic do not automatically capture it.

Timetables are strategy documents. They are just badly formatted.
