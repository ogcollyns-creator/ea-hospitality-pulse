1️⃣ Kenya's Ebola count stands at nine cases, two deaths and two patients still in treatment

**What happened.** Five men, two at Kenyatta National Hospital and three at The Nairobi Hospital, tested negative and went home on 3 November. Kenya has recorded nine confirmed cases and two deaths (22.2%), all in one cluster linked to the first imported case, with no new infection since 19 October. Another 46 contacts left quarantine today, making 66 released; 52 remain and are due out by 11 November *(KBC, 3 Nov; The Standard, 3 Nov; Reuters via TimesLIVE, 3 Nov)*.
The 42-day countdown starts only when the last patient has recovered or been buried; travel, trade and gatherings stay unrestricted *(KBC, 3 Nov)*.
Our inference: two patients remain in hospital, so the clock has not started. If both left by 11 November, the earliest end date would be about 23 December.

🎯 So what: keep "Ebola-free" out of your marketing until the Ministry declares it, and quote its figures to anxious bookers.

2️⃣ Kenya's Health Ministry confirmed Saudi Arabia and Kuwait have imposed travel measures on Kenya

**What happened.** The Ministry's 3 November statement says Saudi Arabia and Kuwait have announced measures on travel to and from Kenya and that it has asked for their justification *(KBC, 3 Nov)*. Saudi Arabia's order, announced on 30 October, bars Saudi citizens from travelling to Kenya, suspends every visa type for travellers from Kenya and refuses entry to anyone arriving from Kenya, including via a third country if they were in Kenya in the previous 21 days *(Khaleej Times, 30 Oct)*. We missed it on 31 October. Kuwait's terms and any effective date were not stated.

🎯 So what: if you hold Saudi or Kuwaiti bookings, ask each guest now whether they can still travel, and agree date changes rather than refunds where you can.
🏷 City, Beach | Kenya | Confirmed | impact:demand

3️⃣ Tanzania's Health Ministry and WHO assessed Ebola readiness in Ruvuma, with no case reported

**What happened.** The Ministry, TAMISEMI and WHO reviewed infection-control capacity at hospitals in Ruvuma Region on 3 November. The article reports no confirmed or suspected Ebola case in Tanzania. Teams will visit other regions near international borders *(Daily News Tanzania, 3 Nov)*. Ruvuma is far south of the northern safari circuit.
🎯 So what: no entry rule has changed; expect health-team visits in border regions.

## LINKEDIN

Kenya's Ebola outbreak reached a quieter stage today, and the commercial lesson is about wording, not optimism.

On 3 November the Health Ministry discharged five patients, released 46 more quarantined contacts and reported no new infection since 19 October. The total stays at nine cases and two deaths. Two patients are still in hospital, and the Ministry has said the 42-day countdown to declaring the outbreak over starts only when the last patient has recovered or been buried. That means the clock has not started.

The same statement confirmed that Saudi Arabia and Kuwait have imposed travel measures on Kenya. Saudi Arabia's order, announced on 30 October, bars its citizens from travelling to Kenya, suspends all visas for travellers from Kenya and refuses entry to arrivals from Kenya.

Our read: the health story is improving faster than the market's perception of it. Domestic travel and gatherings are unrestricted, yet two Gulf governments have moved the other way. For an operator, the practical steps are small. Quote Ministry figures rather than adjectives, ask any Gulf-origin guest what they can still do, and avoid "Ebola-free" until the Ministry says it. If both remaining patients were discharged by 11 November, the earliest end date would be about 23 December, which is our inference, not a Ministry date.
