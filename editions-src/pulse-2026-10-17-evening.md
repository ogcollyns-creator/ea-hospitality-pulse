1️⃣ Kenya confirmed a third Ebola case on 16 October, a 65-year-old woman already in quarantine since 6 October

She is a close relative of the first two patients and tested positive at the National Virology Reference Laboratory and KEMRI. All three cases sit in one family; one has died. Of 130 listed contacts, 127 (97.7%) are traced and in quarantine, three are still being traced. The Ministry sees no community transmission and warns more cases among quarantined contacts are possible *(Ministry of Health via The Star, 16 Oct; WHO AFRO, 16 Oct)*.
Our inference: cases are surfacing inside quarantine, which is the system working, but contacts are watched for 21 days from their own last exposure, so new exposures can push the end date into November.
🎯 So what: keep staff briefings to the Ministry's wording and hold cancellation policies steady; no restriction on travel is in force.

2️⃣ Kenya's National Assembly passed a bill on 14 October keeping 8% VAT on fuel for 90 days

The Tax Laws (Amendment) Bill 2026 lets the Treasury CS add three more months, up to six in total. This settles the question we left open on 14 Oct. Senate and presidential assent are not confirmed *(Kenya Times, 15 Oct, citing the National Assembly vote)*.
🎯 So what: budget diesel and transfers off the current EPRA ceilings, not a return to 16% VAT before mid-December.
🏷 City, Bush, Beach | Kenya | Reported | impact:margin

3️⃣ WHO advised on 16 October against closing borders or restricting trade over Kenya's cases

Kenya reports no restriction on travel, trade or gatherings, with health checks at points of entry. The Star (16 Oct) adds a digital form and QR code for travellers from 10 high-risk countries and enhanced screening of their aircraft *(WHO AFRO, The Star, 16 Oct)*. We saw no new advisory for Kenya after the third case.
🎯 So what: expect more questions at check-in from inbound agents on the 10-country form, not fewer bookings by order.

## LINKEDIN

Kenya confirmed its third Ebola case on 16 October, and the detail that matters for operators is where it was found.

The patient is a 65-year-old woman, a close relative of the first two cases, who had been in quarantine since 6 October. Kenya now has three cases, one death, all in one family. The Ministry reports 127 of 130 listed contacts traced and in quarantine, and sees no community transmission. WHO's regional office advises against closing borders or restricting trade.

Read it two ways. Cases surfacing inside quarantine is what monitoring is meant to catch. But each contact is watched for 21 days from their own last exposure, so a case in quarantine can extend the clock rather than end it.

For owners, the practical points are narrow. No restriction on travel, trade or gatherings is in force. Kenya asks travellers from 10 high-risk countries to complete a digital form before boarding. Keep staff briefings to the Ministry's wording, and keep cancellation policies steady rather than reacting to each headline.

Separately, Kenya's Assembly passed a bill on 14 October keeping fuel VAT at 8% for 90 days, which protects diesel budgets for now.
