## LINKEDIN

Kenya wants to more than double its Middle East arrivals. The number that decides whether it can is buried three paragraphs into the announcement.

Kenya Tourism Board is targeting 50,000 visitors from the Middle East, against 20,480 in the 2025/26 financial year (Capital FM, 16 September 2026). The strategy is connectivity-led: Emirates flies three times daily into Nairobi, flydubai four times weekly, and Qatar Airways feeds the Gulf hubs.

Here is the composition of the base being doubled. Israel, Yemen and Iran accounted for 48% of those 20,480 arrivals. The UAE contributed 10%. Saudi Arabia, 7%.

So the marketing is pointed at roughly a sixth of the market, and nearly half of it sits in countries at war — the same war whose fuel-price consequences pushed the US Federal Reserve into its first rate rise since July 2023 this week.

That is not an argument against the strategy. It is an argument for reading it correctly. A target built on GCC leisure, wellness and family travel is a genuine growth plan with real seat capacity behind it. A target that quietly assumes the Israeli, Iranian and Yemeni half recovers is a forecast resting on a ceasefire nobody has announced.

For operators, the practical move is unglamorous. Segment your Middle East pipeline by country of origin rather than by region, and price and contract the two halves differently. One is a market you can build against. The other is revenue you should be grateful for and never budget.
