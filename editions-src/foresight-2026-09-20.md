## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**Uganda cleared Ebola on 25 August. Its airport still lost 29% of a peak month. The risk label is now a line item — and Kenya just acquired one.**

If you run a lodge in western Uganda, you have spent the summer answering the same question from European and American agents: when does this end. In August you finally had the answer. The World Health Organization declared Uganda's outbreak over on 25 August, after 42 days with no confirmed case, against a total of twenty cases and two deaths. The epidemiological file was closed.

On Friday the Uganda Civil Aviation Authority published what that closure was worth. Entebbe International Airport handled 169,706 international passengers in August 2026. That is 8.5% better than July. It is also 29.1% below the 239,329 it handled in August 2025 — a shortfall of 69,623 passengers in the single strongest travel month of the year. The first half of 2026 was already down 10.8%. Nobody in the region has yet said the obvious thing about that number, so here it is: it is not the cost of an outbreak. It is the cost of a label.

**THE SITUATION**

Compare it with Zanzibar. The Office of the Chief Government Statistician recorded 124,481 international arrivals in August, an all-time monthly record, up 18% year on year, with 949,056 of 1,021,853 available bed spaces sold — 92.9% occupancy. Italy delivered 17,608 visitors, Britain 9,799, Germany 8,576, France 8,482. Same region. Same month. Same regional outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, which reached 7,475 confirmed cases and 3,605 deaths by 16 September, adding 71 cases in a single day. Neither Uganda nor Zanzibar recorded a case in August.

What separated them was not health. It was documentation. KLM has not resumed Entebbe despite the all-clear. Uganda Airlines' Kinshasa route is still suspended. Dubai still restricts Uganda-originating passengers. The United States order renewed on 16 September still names Uganda — and for the first time reaches certain American citizens and nationals, imposing a 21-day wait outside the country for those returning from DRC and routing arrivals from Uganda and South Sudan through four designated airports.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

Two things, and both are inside the numbers rather than the commentary.

First, the direction of the damage. Entebbe recorded 88,270 arrivals against 81,436 departures — arrivals exceeded departures by 6,834, which UCAA's reporting attributes to restrictions on Ugandans travelling out. The label is not only deterring foreign guests. It is involuntarily keeping Uganda's own outbound market at home. For a Kampala city hotel, an Entebbe property, a Jinja or Lake Victoria operator, that is a demand pool with zero acquisition cost sitting in the domestic market right now, and it is not being sold to.

Second, the transferability. On 15 September the French foreign ministry updated its Kenya security advice to describe Kenya as being in an active pre-electoral situation, eleven months before the 10 August 2027 vote, warning that political mobilisation can generate blockages, disruption and sometimes deadly violence. Kenya has not had an incident that moved a booking curve. It has had a sentence added to a page. Uganda's August figure is the price list for that sentence if nobody manages it.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

*Uganda, bush and city:* stop forecasting off the all-clear and forecast off the recovery curve. At 8.5% a month from a 29% deficit, August 2025 volumes are roughly four clean months away, and that assumes no further shock. Build one budget case on the label persisting through 2027. Separately, put a domestic and regional rate into market this week aimed at the outbound traveller who cannot leave.

*Kenya, all segments:* write an election-period rebooking clause into every 2027 group and FIT contract now, alongside the statutory-cost-variation clause the 14 October fuel-VAT and EPRA expiry already demands. Eleven months out it reads as governance. Three months out it reads as panic, and by then the trade has repriced you.

*Everyone selling into the United States:* your pre-departure pack should name the four-airport re-entry routing for any itinerary touching Uganda, including airside transit. Guests discover this at ticketing, and a surprise at ticketing is a cancellation.

**THE WATCH**

The order lapses or renews around 11 October. Tanzania's fuel cap review lands around 7 October. Kenya's 8% fuel VAT and the EPRA cycle both expire on 14 October, and Kenya Airways halves Nairobi–Dubai from 14 to 7 weekly on 25 October. None of these is an incident. All of them are dated.
