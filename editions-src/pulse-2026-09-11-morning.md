**1️⃣ FOUR STATISTICS OFFICES. ONE FINDING. YOUR BIGGEST INPUT IS INFLATING UP TO FIVE TIMES FASTER THAN YOUR ROOM RATE.**

All four August CPI releases are now out. Read side by side — which almost nobody does — they say the same thing. **Transport inflation, annual: Kenya 15.7% · Rwanda 24.2% · Tanzania 13.8% · Uganda 9.4%.** Restaurants-and-accommodation in the same releases: **Kenya 3.0% · Tanzania 3.0% · Uganda 2.5% · Rwanda 15.6%** *(KNBS 31 Aug; NISR 10 Sep, urban; NBS TZ 8 Sep; UBOS Aug 2026)*.

Kenyan transport is running **5.2× the hospitality line**. Tanzania 4.6×. Uganda 3.8×. That second line is effectively the official record of what the trade has managed to charge — so four governments have independently documented the same squeeze.

🎯 **So what:** stop burying transfers in the rate. If road transfers, game drives and airport runs sit inside an inclusive price, you are absorbing a 9–24% input rise inside a 2.5–3.0% price. Break them out as a quoted line with a fuel-adjustment clause — you are contracting 2027 rates *now*.

**2️⃣ AND KENYA IS ABSORBING THE MOST OF ANYONE.**

Subtract headline inflation from the hospitality line and you get the real price of a room: **Kenya −3.6pp · Uganda −1.6pp · Tanzania −1.3pp · Rwanda −0.1pp** *(our calculation from the four releases)*.

🎯 **So what:** if you held Nairobi or coast rate flat this year "to stay competitive", you did not hold it. You cut it about 3.6% in real terms while your transport bill rose 15.7%. Take that number into the October budget round instead of a gut feel.

**3️⃣ RWANDA HAS LEFT THE REGION'S PRICE BAND.**

Urban CPI **15.7%** y/y, from 14.5% in July and 9.2% in March — up every month since. Rural 16.0%. Housing and utilities **20.4%**, food 16.3%, core 11.5% *(NISR, 10 Sep 2026)*. Kigali's hospitality line is up **15.6%**: Rwandan operators have passed the shock through, Kenyan and Tanzanian ones have not.

🎯 **So what:** a Kigali conference gets dearer against Dar and Kampala every month, and RDB is chasing a US$156m MICE target into that. Selling Rwanda: quote in hard currency, shorten rate-validity windows. Selling Kampala or Dar against Kigali: your relative price has never been better — tell buyers now.

**4️⃣ UGANDA IS THE CHEAP SEAT — BUT NOT AT THE PUMP.**

Headline **4.1%**, the region's softest hospitality line at **2.5%** (from 3.2%). But petrol **+28.5%**, kerosene **+31.2%**, energy/fuel/utilities **+14.3%**. One easing: international flights **+17.0%**, from 24.1% *(UBOS, Aug 2026)*.

🎯 **So what:** Uganda is the value play and its airfare drag is loosening. Price Bwindi–Queen Elizabeth against Kenyan equivalents in the Q4 deck — but fuel-index vehicle costs.

## LINKEDIN

Four East African statistics offices have now published August inflation. Read individually, each is a domestic macro story. Read together, they are an operating instruction.

Annual transport inflation, August 2026: Kenya 15.7%, Rwanda 24.2%, Tanzania 13.8%, Uganda 9.4%.

The restaurants-and-accommodation line in the same four releases: Kenya 3.0%, Tanzania 3.0%, Uganda 2.5%, Rwanda 15.6%.

In Kenya, the cost of moving a guest is rising 5.2 times faster than the price of housing one. In Tanzania 4.6 times. In Uganda 3.8 times.

That accommodation line is, in practice, the official record of what our industry has actually been able to charge. So four governments have independently documented the same squeeze, and most operators have seen only their own country's headline number.

One consequence is worth sitting with. Subtract headline inflation from the accommodation line and you get the real price of a room: Kenya minus 3.6 points, Uganda minus 1.6, Tanzania minus 1.3, Rwanda minus 0.1. An operator who held rate flat to stay competitive did not hold rate. They cut it, in real terms, while their fuel bill rose by double digits.

The practical response is unglamorous and available now, in the middle of the 2027 contracting round: stop carrying transfers inside an inclusive rate, quote them as a separate line, and index them to fuel.
