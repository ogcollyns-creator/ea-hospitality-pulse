1️⃣ ETHIOPIAN GOES DAILY INTO KILIMANJARO AND ZANZIBAR — FROM 30 NOVEMBER
Ethiopian has filed a schedule increase on the Addis Ababa – Kilimanjaro – Zanzibar – Addis triangle: from the 3 weekly originally filed to 7 weekly, effective 30 November 2026 (AeroRoutes, published 04 September 2026). Overall Ethiopian service into those two airports rises from 17–18 weekly to 21. The rotation is ET855, a 737-800: ADD 23:00 → JRO 01:20 → ZNZ 03:30, back into ADD 07:00.
Read the clock before you read the capacity. Kilimanjaro receives at 01:20 and dispatches at 02:10; Zanzibar receives at 03:30 and dispatches at 04:20. That is a connecting bank feeding the Addis wave from Europe, Asia and the Gulf — not leisure timing. What arrives with it is night transfers and 06:00 arrivals at your gate, from guests on a through-fare who will not pay a supplement for an early room.
The demand side is where the caution sits. Zanzibar's arrivals growth has cooled: +19.2% year-on-year in January 2026, +4.9% in February, +3.1% in June, +9.6% in July (Office of the Chief Government Statistician, monthly releases). Seats are landing into the December–March peak faster than arrivals are currently growing.
🎯 So what: publish a priced 06:00 early-check-in policy before 30 November — you will be asked. And stress-test your peak rate strategy against arrivals growth of three per cent, not nineteen. New lift is not new demand.

2️⃣ 92% OF LUXURY HOTEL PHONE CALLS CAPTURE NOTHING
A Revinate mystery-shopper study of 308 calls to 135 luxury and upscale properties found 92.2% captured no guest contact detail, and staff asked zero discovery questions on more than half the calls (via Hospitality Net, 4 September 2026). The sample is North American. The failure is not.
Our transmission is direct. EA lodges and camps run far more enquiry by phone and WhatsApp than a European city hotel, and far more confirmed business through agents. The direct enquiry is the one channel where you own the guest record outright. Losing the name and email on nine calls in ten means paying commission twice — to whoever converts the guest this year, and again next year because you cannot reach them.
🎯 So what: mystery-shop your own reservations line this week. One call, at 19:00. If your team quotes a rate before asking for a name and an email, that is a free fix worth more than any channel-mix project you have.

## LINKEDIN

Ethiopian Airlines has filed to fly Addis Ababa – Kilimanjaro – Zanzibar daily from 30 November, up from the three weekly it originally filed. Overall service into those two airports rises from 17-18 weekly to 21. The filing went up on 4 September and, as far as we can see, has not yet been reported anywhere in the East African trade press.

Most operators will read that as good news. Read the timetable before you decide.

ET855 puts guests on the ground at Kilimanjaro at 01:20 and lifts again at 02:10. It reaches Zanzibar at 03:30 and departs at 04:20. That is a connecting bank built to feed Ethiopian's Addis wave from Europe, Asia and the Gulf — not a leisure schedule. What arrives with it is night transfers, 06:00 arrivals at your gate, and guests holding a through-fare who will not pay a supplement for an early room.

Then there is the demand side. Zanzibar's arrivals grew 19.2% year-on-year in January. By June that had fallen to 3.1%, and July came in at 9.6%. Those are the Office of the Chief Government Statistician's own monthly numbers, not a forecast. The island cleared 917,167 visitors in 2025 and is being handed more seats into the December-March peak than its current growth rate absorbs.

New lift is not new demand. It is a wider distribution of the same guest, and the property that has already published a priced early check-in policy will convert that guest at a better rate than the one improvising at the front desk on 1 December.

Two things worth doing before the end of September: put the 06:00 policy in writing, and stress-test your peak rate strategy against arrivals growth of three per cent rather than nineteen.
