## LINKEDIN

The US State Department renewed its Kenya travel advisory on 28 July. The country level did not move — still Level 2. Two Nairobi neighbourhoods did.

Checked today, the rendered advisory lists Eastleigh and Kibera under Level 4, Do Not Travel, for crime and kidnapping, and adds a sixth risk indicator, "Other". State's own summary of the action reads: "There was no change to the advisory level. The 'other' risk indicator was added. Advisory summary was updated." Easy to file as housekeeping. It wasn't.

None of that geography touches where guests actually stay — Westlands, Upper Hill, Gigiri, the CBD, Karen. But corporate travel-risk platforms and insurers parse advisory text as strings, not as maps. What reaches a duty-of-care officer is "Nairobi" and "Do Not Travel" in one paragraph, and the question lands on your reservations desk rather than at the State Department.

So this week's work is unglamorous and worth doing: rewrite pre-arrival and duty-of-care wording to name both neighbourhoods and state that no scheduled routing goes near them. Say it before a corporate account asks, not after.

And since we got it wrong ourselves: our own advisory board carried those neighbourhoods a level lower until this morning. Corrected.
