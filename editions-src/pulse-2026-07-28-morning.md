## LINKEDIN

A strike suspended is not a strike settled — and East African hoteliers keep making that mistake.

The Kenya Aviation Workers Union withdrew the nationwide strike it had scheduled for today, Tuesday 28 July, after signing a framework agreement on Monday with the Ministry of Roads and Transport, Kenya Airways, the Kenya Airports Authority, KCAA and Jambojet. Flights operate normally. Most properties will now quietly delete the contingency plan they built last week.

That is the wrong read. What was signed is an agreement to negotiate. The union's substantive demands — withdrawal of NYS and KDF personnel from the JKIA fire station, and reinstatement of four dismissed employees — remain live. Talks with the airlines open today; the harder round, KAWU with KAA, begins tomorrow, 29 July.

For a property, the cost asymmetry is stark. Holding a flexible-rebooking clause and a transfer buffer for another fortnight costs you almost nothing. Standing them down and being wrong during peak season costs you a night of rooms, a fleet of idle vehicles and a page of one-star reviews written by people who slept at the airport.

Three practical moves this week: keep the flexible wording in agent comms until a CBA is actually signed, not until a strike is called off; diarise 29 July as your real decision date; and tell your source-market partners precisely what happened rather than a reassuring summary — accuracy is what makes them believe you next time.
