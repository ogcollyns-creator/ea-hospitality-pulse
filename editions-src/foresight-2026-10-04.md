## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**An East African lodge's 2027 rate letter is an option contract, and most owners are writing it for free**

If you are drafting 2027 rates this month, you are probably asking how much of an increase the market will bear. September suggests that is the wrong question. The right one is what you have promised, and at what price, before the year starts.

**THE SITUATION**

A lodge signs a dollar rate card 12 to 18 months before the guest arrives. Safari bookings are made 186 to 190 days ahead, according to Rhino Africa's chief executive, David Ryan, speaking on 31 August. One Kenyan operator's published terms charge nothing for cancellation more than 60 days out. On those terms, 126 days of a 186-day booking cost the guest nothing to leave. That is our arithmetic on one operator's terms, not a market average, but it is a typical shape.

In the same month, the cost side repriced. On 30 September Uganda's statistics bureau reported annual diesel inflation of 42.5%, while headline inflation was 4.6%. Kenya's bureau reported headline inflation of 6.8%, transport at 15.6%, and core inflation up to 4.0% from 3.4%. The Bank of Tanzania recorded the shilling averaging 2,657 to the dollar in August, down 6.7% on a year earlier.

The revenue side did not follow. The Bank of Tanzania put travel receipts for the year to August at US$4.39bn, against US$4.31bn: growth of 1.7%. Zanzibar reported 124,481 arrivals in August, up 18%. Those two figures cover different places and periods, so they do not reconcile. They do say that volume and dollars are not moving together.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

Each of these has been filed as its own problem. Fuel costs call for a surcharge. Re-shopping calls for tighter terms. New taxes call for a lobbying submission.

Read together, they describe one position. The lodge has sold the guest a fixed price and a long free exit, and it carries costs that float and charges that do not. An option is worth more to its holder when prices swing and when the holder keeps looking for a better deal. Skift Research and McKinsey found on 22 September that 78% of travellers keep researching after they book, and that half have changed a confirmed accommodation. That figure is lifetime behaviour in a US sample, not a churn rate. Fuel and currency volatility rose in the same month.

So a flat 5% rate rise does not fix this. It raises the price of a contract the guest can still leave, and the owner keeps the fixed costs of the empty room. Those fixed costs are growing. Kenya deleted the VAT exemption on services for building tourism and conference facilities from 1 July. Nairobi's county schedule reportedly charges hotels KSh 2,000 per room a year, though only one outlet has reported it. Tanzania's US$44 insurance has applied to visitors since 1 October.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

For bush camps and lodges: the exposure is the fuel line. Put a review clause into 2028 contracts, tied to a published pump-price index, and shorten how long a rate stays fixed.

For beach properties, especially Zanzibar: demand is strongest here. Sell a non-refundable rate at a discount and a flexible rate at a premium instead of discounting for fill. State in writing whether your package includes the US$44 insurance.

For city hotels in Nairobi, Kampala and Kigali: your growth segment is short-lead regional and corporate demand, which sits almost entirely inside the free-cancellation window. Tier your cancellation terms. Add up every county and national charge per available room and show it as one annual figure.

**THE WATCH**

The Central Bank of Kenya decides rates on 7 October. Tanzania publishes September inflation on 8 October. The US entry order on DRC, Uganda and South Sudan lapses on 11 October unless renewed. Kenya's fuel VAT relief expires on 14 October.

The decision this week is not how much to raise rates. It is which promises in the 2027 contract you are still giving away.
