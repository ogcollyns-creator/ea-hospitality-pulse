## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**Uganda is Ebola-free and still banned. This week the reason changed — and the new one cannot be earned away.**

If you run a lodge in western Uganda, you have spent four months answering one question from European and American agents: when does this end? Until Friday you could give a defensible answer, because the restriction rested on a fact about your country. The disease was present; clear it, and the basis falls away. Uganda cleared it. WHO certified the 42-day countdown complete on 27 August.

On 11 September the Centers for Disease Control signed its fifth thirty-day entry order. Read the operative sentence: Ebola is "confirmed currently present in DRC and recently present in Uganda," and "there is a material risk that the outbreak will spread to South Sudan and again to Uganda." Uganda is retained not for what it has, but for what may return to it. That is a forecast about the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and no amount of Ugandan surveillance falsifies it.

**THE SITUATION**

The epidemiology behind the order is not in dispute. DRC stood at 6,779 confirmed cases and 3,267 deaths across 61 health zones on 9 September, against 4,318 and 2,011 across 41 zones a month earlier. Contact tracing has reached about 87% against the 95% threshold CDC considers operationally necessary, and the agency's own assessment is that true magnitude may run two to four times reported figures. Canada extended its 21-day quarantine on 28 August, to 28 September. Both governments still name Uganda, whose own CDC travel health notice sits at Level 2.

Read the order's factual basis and Kenya appears too. Jomo Kenyatta International is listed among the transit hubs — with Addis Ababa, Brussels, Doha, Dubai and Istanbul — whose connectivity to US gateways creates a pathway for an asymptomatic traveller. Kenya is not restricted. It is characterised, on the strength of its route map, in a document its tourism authorities have never commented on.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

The sector filed four unrelated items this week: a health renewal, a Kenya footnote, two inflation prints, a strong month in Zanzibar. They describe one mechanism, and it concerns the provenance of demand.

Demand that reaches you through a foreign government's risk framework is now priced on forecasts you cannot contest. Demand that reaches you from inside the region is priced on your own cost base — and there the numbers have come apart. Rwanda's August headline inflation printed 15.7%, with the restaurants-and-hotels line at 15.6% year-on-year and 2.0% in a single month. Tanzania printed 4.3%. Same region, same week, eleven points of separation. Kenya sat at 6.6%, Uganda at 4.1%.

Now look at which market set a record. Zanzibar took 124,481 arrivals in August, 18% above the same month last year, at 92.9% bed occupancy. Europe's share fell to 58.1% even as the total hit a high. And of 114,553 air arrivals, 39,339 — better than a third — stepped off a domestic flight from the Tanzanian mainland. The cheapest cost base in the region, growing through the shortest supply chain, on a channel no foreign ministry can designate.

The consequence nobody has costed is contractual. You are being asked this quarter to sign 2027 rates in dollars, against a designation with no forecastable expiry and a regional cost base moving at somewhere between 4.3% and 15.7% depending on which border you sit behind. That is not a rate negotiation. It is an indexation problem, and this region is not having that conversation.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

Uganda bush and city: stop quoting a recovery date. Rewrite Q4 and 2027 terms so re-book-not-refund language triggers on the designation itself rather than on outbreak status — as of Friday those are formally different things. Weight the next two quarters of pipeline toward source markets whose governments do not designate: regional, Gulf, India, intra-African.

Kenya city and coast: put JKIA's naming into your own risk annexe, with the order's date and its Level 2 context, before a corporate client's screening tool surfaces it unannotated. A conduit you disclose scores better than one they discover. Separately, France now treats Kenya's August 2027 election as a standing advisory condition rather than an event — get ground-movement protocols and cancellation terms into 2027 contracts while it is still a paragraph.

Rwanda: reopen every 2027 dollar rate agreed before the August print. A 15.6% move in your hotel cost line against a fixed contract is margin already spent. Build from the domestic cost curve, not the exchange rate.

Tanzania and Zanzibar: 92.9% occupancy is a capacity signal, not a pricing one. Sell length-of-stay into September–November and defend mainland air allocation before adding long-haul contracts. A third of your peak month came through that door.

**THE WATCH**

Around 11 October the CDC order lapses or renews, explicitly subject to an ongoing comprehensive risk assessment — that is the real date behind every Uganda recovery conversation, and its comment window closes about 26 September. Canada's measures expire 28 September. EPRA prices land 15 September against Brent's $101.21 close on 9 September. And Uganda's own free-entry days run 25–27 September with the scope of the waiver still not stated unambiguously by UWA.
