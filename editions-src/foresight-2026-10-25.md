## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**Kenya's Ebola cases may end before its Level 3 does. Uganda is the evidence.**

If you take bookings from the United States, the most important number in Kenya this week was not the nine cases. It was the date on a US government web page.

**THE SITUATION**

On 19 October the State Department raised Kenya from Level 2 to Level 3, "reconsider travel", citing Ebola alongside crime, terrorism and unrest. Three days earlier it had lowered Tanzania from Level 3 to Level 2. Rwanda has been at Level 3 and Uganda at Level 4 since early June, when those pages were last issued. Four neighbouring destinations now carry three different ratings.

They do not track the disease. Kenya's health ministry reports nine confirmed cases and two deaths, all in one cluster, with 135 contacts traced and no evidence of community spread. The first contacts are due out of quarantine today. Uganda says it has no active cases. Tanzania has none.

Uganda is the part to study. Its ministry declared the outbreak over on 28 July, and the World Health Organization dated the end to late August. On 25 October the US page still read Level 4, "do not travel", 89 days after Uganda's own declaration. Then on 14 October the Bank of Uganda held an emergency meeting and raised its policy rate from 9.75% to 12.0%, the largest move since 2011. It cited energy prices, a weak shilling and a current-account deficit that widened by US$1.4bn to US$4.6bn, partly because of lower tourism receipts. The shilling had lost more than 11% since January, according to the Daily Monitor. The Bank did not blame Ebola for any of this, and we are not saying Ebola caused the rate move. We are saying tourism receipts are now named in a central bank's reasoning.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

The Kenyan conversation is about whether the outbreak spreads. A stricter question is how long the rating lasts once it stops. Uganda's record suggests the answer is months, not weeks. A rating is set by a process that moves faster up than down, and a booking made today for a 2027 safari is made against the page as it reads today.

That is our inference, and it has limits. Kenya's cluster is smaller and its government has imposed no travel restrictions, while Uganda's outbreak started earlier and sat beside a far larger one in Congo. The WHO's weekly report put Congo at 9,066 confirmed cases by 11 October. Kenya's neighbour risk is real, and Nigeria's health minister has said its case is closely related to Kenya's index case.

The second thing missed is Tanzania. It is the obvious winner, at Level 2 with inflation of 4.3%. But on 17 October it began requiring every arrival to complete an online health form, wherever they fly from, and since 1 October visitors have needed insurance of about US$44. The US source said no link between the downgrade and bookings has yet been established. An advantage that has not yet produced a booking is not yet an advantage you can price.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

Kenya bush, Mara and Amboseli: assume the US page stays at Level 3 through your 2027 booking window and write to US guests now. One page: nine cases, one cluster, the screening at entry, your flexible rebooking terms. Offer the same safari to UK, European, Gulf, Indian and regional guests, whose governments have not matched the US. We have not confirmed the current UK, German, French and Canadian advice from primary pages, so check each before you rely on it.

Kenya city, Nairobi: the exposure is meetings. The Public Health PS memo of 7 October suspending government meetings in hotels has not, to our knowledge, been withdrawn. Do not budget for it.

Kenya coast: the Level 3 text names Lamu, Tana River and Kilifi north of highway C103 as "do not travel" for security reasons. Mombasa and Diani are not named. Say so.

Tanzania mainland and Zanzibar: hold rates. Put the health form and insurance in the pre-arrival email so the friction is yours to explain. Essence of Africa in Zanzibar was previewed with 178 buyers from 40 countries, 23% from the Americas. Follow up this week.

Uganda: if you borrow in shillings, ask your bank what the 225-point rise does to your repayment. If you earn in dollars, your local costs are lower in dollar terms, but the advisory is the larger constraint.

**THE WATCH**

The first Kenyan contacts leave quarantine on 25 October. Kenya's October inflation lands around 6 November. EPRA's fuel review is on 14 November. The US call on Kenya's level is ours to be tested by 30 November, and the EAC single-visa comment deadline is the same day.

An outbreak is counted in weeks. A rating is counted in seasons, and your rate card is set for the season.
