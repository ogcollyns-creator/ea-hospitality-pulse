1️⃣ TANZANIA IS STILL ON THE EU BAN LIST. THE TRADE THINKS IT ISN'T.
The Commission's 48th update of the EU Air Safety List (DG MOVE, 9 June 2026) still bans every Tanzanian-certified airline — one of 16 states listed for inadequate *regulator* oversight, not aircraft faults. Kyrgyzstan came off the same list entirely, so it is a two-way door. Yet ATTA's library still carries "Tanzanian Airlines to Resume Flights to Europe After EU Safety Ban Lifted", 15 September 2025, sourced to Tuko. Wrong then, still circulating. Separately, TCAA posted a notice headlined "TCAA and EU reaffirm commitment to strengthen aviation safety oversight" (tcaa.go.tz, 4 September 2026; seen 06:23 EAT, no press coverage found). The notice exists — the page would not render for us, so we make no claim on its contents.
🎯 So what: the ban is on the authority, not the fleet, and guests will not draw that line for you. Put one written sentence in your agent sheet saying domestic Tanzanian flights are unaffected — and stop the "ban lifted" story where you find it.

2️⃣ ARUSHA GOES 24 HOURS — THE COMPULSORY OVERNIGHT ENDS
ATCL chief executive Eng. Peter Ulanga says Arusha Airport begins round-the-clock operations within two weeks, after a lighting upgrade cleared night flights (Daily News Tanzania, 2 September 2026). Daily capacity rises from about 200 passengers to 300, on Dar es Salaam and Zanzibar routes. The stated purpose is evening safari arrivals: guests no longer need to sleep in Arusha before continuing. 3️⃣ TURKANA GETS A TEN-YEAR PLAN AND A FUNDED PARTNER
KWS formalised a management partnership with Archipelagus on 29 August 2026 covering Sibiloi and Central Island National Parks (KWS press release, Aug 2026; via ATTA, 3 September). It anchors the Lake Turkana National Parks Management Plan 2026–2036 across more than 6,000 km² of parks and community rangelands, currently on UNESCO's "In Danger" list. First moves: a Sibiloi headquarters, a joint operations centre, ranger outposts on real-time monitoring. Later: grassland restoration, phased reintroduction of reticulated giraffe, Grevy's zebra and black rhino, and nature-based tourism.
🎯 So what: Kenya's emptiest circuit just got a decade-long plan and a funded partner, with almost no bed stock in it. Read it if you are looking at concessions. Do not put Turkana in a brochure yet.

## LINKEDIN

A story that Tanzanian airlines were cleared to fly to Europe has been circulating in the African travel trade since September 2025. It is not true, and the paperwork says so plainly.

The European Commission published the 48th update of the EU Air Safety List on 9 June 2026. Every airline certified in Tanzania remains banned from EU skies — one of 16 states listed because the Commission judges the national authority's safety oversight inadequate. Not the aircraft. The regulator. In the same update, Kyrgyzstan was removed entirely after twenty years of work, which is the useful part: this is a two-way door, and states do get off it.

The commercial number sits in the Eurobarometer survey published alongside it. When an airline appears on that list, only 8% of Europeans say they would still book it. Seven in ten regard the list as effective. Just 12% ever check it — so the damage never arrives as a lost booking you can see. It arrives as a guest who read something once.

For anyone selling Tanzania into European source markets, the distinction between a grounded fleet and an unrated regulator is worth one written line in your agent sheet. Your guests will not draw it for you.
