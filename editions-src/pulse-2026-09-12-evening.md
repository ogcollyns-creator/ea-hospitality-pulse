## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**Your security plan protects the building. Tomorrow, the risk is the route.**

Nairobi Regional Police Commander Issa Mohamud announced this evening that large political rallies will not be permitted around the Central Business District. The Linda Mwananchi rally proceeds regardless tomorrow, Sunday 13 September, with a procession forming at All Saints Cathedral and moving to Jacaranda Grounds. Mohamud cited credible information that groups may be mobilised to confront rival crowds, and appealed directly to organisers: no political objective, he said, is worth the blood of a Kenyan.

Hours earlier, in Embu, one person was shot dead and six vehicles torched — one belonging to a journalist — when youths clashed with police outside a UDA meeting addressed by the Deputy President. That is the same Saturday. Kenya is eleven months from a general election, and France's foreign ministry has already put tomorrow's Nairobi rally in writing to its nationals.

Most Nairobi hotels are well prepared for the wrong thing.

Since 2019, the industry's security spending has gone into the perimeter: bollards, vehicle traps, scanners, trained guards at the gate. That architecture answers an armed assault on the property. It does not answer tomorrow, because tomorrow's risk is not at your gate. It is on Uhuru Highway at 11:00, on Kenyatta Avenue during a procession, and in the ninety minutes it takes a guest to get from a locked-down CBD to a terminal. All Saints sits on the CBD's western edge — the Serena, Norfolk and Upper Hill corridor, not Eastlands. A Sunday is a check-out day.

So the operational answer is unglamorous and it is all outside the wall. Reroute airport transfers tonight rather than improvising at 09:00. Give arriving guests a written route note instead of a lobby verbal, because a verbal does not survive a shift change. Move kitchen and housekeeping rotas earlier, since your staff cross the same city your guests do, on matatus, without a driver. And treat the weather as part of the plan: Kenya Met has rain over Nairobi from 13 to 17 September. Wet crowds move slowly and disperse badly.

**What everyone is missing**

The rally ban will lead every front page tomorrow. The number in the same briefing will not.

Mohamud disclosed that Central Police Station alone has recorded eighteen cases involving foreign nationals assaulted, robbed or threatened, with eleven already before the courts. The victims he named were Burundian, Chinese and Somali. Four suspects have been charged, and a separate Kilimani file concerns threats to Burundian nationals circulated on social media. He set this against what he described as heightened tensions around the presence of foreigners in some businesses — reporting that sits alongside a wider national story about a crackdown on foreign traders.

Note what the state did in a single press conference. It invoked the safety of tourists as grounds for restricting assembly, and it put a figure on foreign-national safety incidents at one station. Both things are true at once. Operators have spent a decade assuming that a foreign passport in Nairobi confers a kind of invisibility from domestic political friction. That assumption is the one under pressure, and the exposure it creates lands on staff before it lands on guests.

Central Police Station covers the CBD. Kilimani is serviced-apartment and boutique-hotel territory. Between them they describe a meaningful share of Nairobi's room stock, and a meaningful share of its payroll. If you employ non-Kenyan nationals — and in this market, at food-and-beverage, spa and back-of-house level, many properties do — this week is not the week for solo closing shifts, late cash runs or unaccompanied journeys home after dark. That costs nothing to fix and is difficult to explain having not fixed.

**The segmented read**

City properties carry all of tomorrow's operational risk and should be working the route tonight. Bush and beach properties carry almost none of it directly, but carry the reputational tail: a guest whose Nairobi transfer goes badly arrives at your camp having already decided what kind of trip this is. Brief your Nairobi handling agent tonight, not on Monday.

And a note against the gloom. Zanzibar reported a monthly record in August — 124,481 arrivals, up eighteen per cent year on year, with estimated bed occupancy at 92.9 per cent. Kenya was its fastest-growing African source market, up seventy-five per cent. We told operators on 5 September to stress-test peak rates against three per cent growth rather than nineteen. That call was wrong, and it is worth saying so plainly: the June slowdown was a trough, not a trend.
