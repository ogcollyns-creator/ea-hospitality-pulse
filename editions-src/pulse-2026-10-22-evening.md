1️⃣ Ghana tightened Ebola screening at Accra airport on 21 October, citing direct flights with Kenya

Ghana's Health Ministry announced enhanced screening at entry points, monitoring of travellers from affected areas, staff sensitisation and drills at Accra airport and the Infectious Disease Centre. Our inference: this is screening, not refusal, unlike El Salvador, but it invites copycat rules.
🎯 So what: if you hold Ghana or Nigeria bookings, send guests the arrival-screening note now and offer date changes before refunds.

2️⃣ WHO's latest situation report shows DRC's Ebola outbreak still rising, at 9,637 confirmed cases by 19 October

Confirmed cases rose 10.4% to 1,466 in the 21 days to 19 October against the prior 21 days; deaths rose 7.5% to 692. The DRC total is 9,637 cases and 4,646 deaths across 8 of 26 provinces. It lists Kenya at nine cases including one death (not independently confirmed by us) *(WHO situation report, via Xinhua/New Vision, 22 Oct)*.
Our inference: the source outbreak is not shrinking, so source-market caution will outlast Kenya's own cluster.

3️⃣ Ebola is adding cost and delay to the Northern Corridor, already strained by insecurity and peak-season imports

The EastAfrican reports four pressures at once: the outbreak, attacks on trucks in eastern DRC and South Sudan, seasonal import volume and Ebola-related border measures, raising transport costs, delays and extra charges. No figures given *(The EastAfrican, 22 Oct; article body not fully read)*.
Our inference: the corridor feeds Kampala and Kigali hotel supply chains; expect dearer imported fit-out, linen and food.
🎯 So what: place imported orders for Q4 now and ask suppliers for landed-cost quotes in writing.

## LINKEDIN

The Ebola story for East African hotels is shifting from Kenya's case count to what other countries do about it.

On 21 October Ghana's Health Ministry announced tighter screening at Accra airport and monitoring of travellers from affected areas, citing direct flights with Kenya (reported by Kenyans.co.ke on 22 October). It is screening rather than a ban, but El Salvador has already gone further, and each new rule adds friction to a booking.

The source outbreak is not helping. WHO's latest situation report, relayed by Xinhua on 22 October, puts DRC at 9,637 confirmed cases and 4,646 deaths by 19 October, with confirmed cases up 10.4% in the latest 21 days against the prior 21. The same report lists Kenya at nine cases.

There is a cost side as well: The EastAfrican reports that Ebola measures, insecurity and peak imports are slowing and raising the cost of the Northern Corridor.

Our inference: source-market caution will outlast Kenya's own cluster, so plan on flexible rebooking terms through December rather than a discount-led recovery. Check which of your source markets screen or restrict arrivals, and place imported orders early.
