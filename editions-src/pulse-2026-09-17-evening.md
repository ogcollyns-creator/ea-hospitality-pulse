**2️⃣ KILIMANJARO'S FIRE IS OUT. THE NUMBER IS 1,800 HECTARES**
The fire that burned on Mt Kilimanjaro for nearly two weeks has been contained, after consuming more than 18 square kilometres — 1,800 hectares — of vegetation *(The EastAfrican, 17 September 2026)*. It began on 3 September at Kikerelwa, near the Kenyan border. TANAPA kept designated climbing routes open throughout.
🎯 **So what:** That is about 2.4% of a 75,575-hectare park, and no route closed. Correct the client who has read a headline, with the figure rather than reassurance — Moshi and Arusha properties should add a one-line factual note to pre-arrival emails for October–December departures before an agent asks.

**3️⃣ WASHINGTON'S UGANDA ORDER IS SIX DAYS OLD AND STILL UNPUBLISHED**
Read directly on CDC's port-health page this afternoon: the Title 42 order of 11 September, signed by Assistant Secretary for Health Adm. Brian Christine, still carries the line "This Order **will** be published in the Federal Register." Every earlier order in the chain — 18 May, 22 May, 21 June, 13 July, 12 August — links to its publication within three to seven days. This one has not appeared in six. The order runs to about 11 October and carries a **15-day comment period** closing around 26 September.
🎯 **So what:** Nine days remain on a comment window attached to a document the public record does not yet carry. If you sell Uganda to American clients, that window is the formal channel for putting the 27 August all-clear on the US federal record before the renewal decision. Do not wait for the notice to appear.

## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**A trade fair is a pricing event. East Africa just fixed 2027 at 2026 costs.**

Somewhere at Paris Expo Porte de Versailles this week, a Ugandan operator shook hands on a 2028 gorilla itinerary. The rate in that handshake was built from a permit price, a fuel cost and a debt cost — and two of those three moved in the seven days before the fair opened.

**THE SITUATION**

IFTM Top Resa 2026 ran from 15 to 17 September: 32,000 tourism professionals, 1,650 companies and institutions, 177 destinations, according to Tanzania's Ministry of Foreign Affairs and East African Cooperation, reported by Daily News on 16 September.

East Africa showed up in force. Uganda brought eleven destination marketing companies to Stand T072 in the Africa and Indian Ocean Hall, more than double the five it sent in 2025. By the second day the Embassy of Uganda in Paris was reporting fully booked meeting schedules and, more to the point, a change in what was being discussed: from destination enquiries to contracts for 2027 and 2028, gorilla trekking permit allocations for Bwindi Impenetrable and Mgahinga, and multi-destination itineraries built around the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations, which Uganda co-hosts with Kenya and Tanzania. UG Standard and SoftPower News both carried the account on 16 September.

The market being contracted is real and growing. Uganda recorded 7,439 French visitors in 2025, more than double the 2024 figure, with average spend above US$2,025 and stays of eight to fifteen nights. France is also Uganda's largest single source of foreign direct investment — more than US$1.6 billion in 2023, over half of all inflows, per the Bank of Uganda's 2023 FDI report as cited by the embassy.

Tanzania worked the same hall with fifteen companies under the Zanzibar Commission for Tourism. At the fair's Club Afrique session, Ambassador Saidi Yakubu urged tourism ministers from DRC, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire and Uganda, alongside ambassadors from around seventeen African states, to build cross-border packages: African countries, he said, should complement rather than compete with one another. Daily News reported it on 17 September.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

The trade press will score this week on visibility — stand counts, meetings held, impressions bought. That is the wrong scoreboard. A trade fair is where two-year prices get fixed, and this one sat inside an unusually busy fortnight for costs.

On 16 September the US Federal Reserve raised to 3.75–4.00%, its first increase since July 2023, with most policymakers expecting a further rise before year-end. Today the Bank of England held at 3.75% — but by 6–3, with three members voting for 4% against UK CPI of 3.1% in August. Every dollar-denominated facility behind East Africa's new lodge supply just got more expensive, and the sterling side is one vote from following.

On 14 October two Kenyan cost events land on the same day: the reduced 8% VAT on petrol, diesel and kerosene expires, and EPRA's frozen pump cycle ends. And Uganda's gorilla habituation permit is reported to rise from US$1,500 to US$1,800 on 1 January 2027 — a US$300 per-person gap on precisely the product most likely to have been sold into a 2027 brochure this week. We have not sighted the UWA schedule itself, and say so.

So the exposure is not that European demand is soft. It is that European demand is being locked at a fixed price while the cost line underneath it has three dated moves already in the calendar and one central bank openly split.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

Write a tariff-variation clause into every 2027 and 2028 contract that is not yet counter-signed, naming park fees, permits and fuel as pass-through items. Vague force majeure language will not recover US$300 a head.

Audit every forward quotation carrying a gorilla habituation leg for the US$1,500 versus US$1,800 gap, and confirm the tariff in writing with UWA before it goes to an agent.

Segment your 2027 pipeline by government, not by continent. Europe is contracting Uganda now. The United States still lists Uganda in an entry suspension whose own text records that Uganda completed its 42-day Ebola countdown on 27 August. One buyer is ready and one is not — price and staff the mix accordingly.

For AFCON in June and July 2027: three co-hosts, no published room-block framework, and a tournament landing at the front of the high season. Decide your allocation split between block and transient before a buyer decides it for you.

**THE WATCH**

Around 26 September the comment window on the US Title 42 order closes — an order that, six days after signature, still had not appeared in the Federal Register when we read CDC's page this afternoon. On 14 October Kenya's fuel VAT and EPRA freeze expire together. Around 11 October the entry order lapses or renews.

The handshake in Paris was denominated in a currency you do not control, against a tariff you do not set. Price it that way.
