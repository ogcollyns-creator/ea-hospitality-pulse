## LINKEDIN

Kenya Airways got 30% of its flights out of Nairobi by 2pm on Sunday. Almost nothing was cancelled. That combination is what makes this week dangerous for lodges rather than for airlines.

Kenya Aviation Workers Union members downed tools at the Kenya Airports Authority, KCAA and Jambojet on 30 August, and the disruption ran into Monday morning. Departures are averaging two to three hours late, some beyond three (KQ customer update via KATA, 30 August).

Here is what most operators will miss. We are trained to watch cancellations, because cancellations generate a refund request and a phone call. A three-hour delay generates neither. It quietly destroys the same-day onward connection — the Wilson hop to the Mara, the leg to Kilimanjaro or Zanzibar — and the cost of that lands on a camp that has already provisioned the night.

It is also the third round of the same collective bargaining dispute this year. The February round closed JKIA for two days; Jambojet flew a quarter of its schedule and cancelled more than forty flights. A July round was enjoined, then suspended on 27 July under a deal the union now says was never honoured.

So the near-term call is straightforward: February settled on day two through ministerial mediation, and this will probably settle quickly too. Hold your rate. Compete on flexibility, not price.

The longer call is the one worth acting on. If a hub interrupts itself three times in seven months, a same-day bush connection is no longer a product you should be selling into the high season without a buffer night.

Kenya remains a US Level 2 advisory, unchanged since 28 July. This is an operations problem, not a country-risk problem, and the properties that keep those two straight will win the rebookings.
