## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**East African hospitality has started being priced on the quality of its records, not the quality of its assets**

Nothing in your building changed this week. Your rate did.

Five developments landed between 1 and 6 September 2026, and every operator I know filed them in five different folders. They belong in one.

**THE SITUATION**

Tanzania's airlines remain barred from European skies. The Commission's 48th update of the EU Air Safety List (DG MOVE, 9 June 2026) lists the country on the grounds of *regulator* oversight capacity — not on any finding about an aircraft or a crew. The asset is fine. The file is not.

Uganda's records diverged in public. On 2 September the FCDO updated its Uganda travel advice to record that WHO declared the country's Ebola outbreak over on 27 August; the page advises against travel to no part of Uganda and was still current at 6 September. The United States has not moved: Level 4, Do Not Travel, last re-issued 4 June 2026. Both governments are reading the same file. Congolese transmission is the binding variable and it is worse — 6,342 confirmed Bundibugyo cases and 3,072 deaths on data to 2 September, across 60 of 151 health zones in six provinces (ECDC, updated 4 September 2026). WHO advises against any travel or trade restriction on affected countries. ECDC rates the risk to EU/EEA residents very low. Same dataset, three institutions, three different prices on the same Ugandan bed.

On 27 September, Directive (EU) 2024/825 begins to apply. "Eco", "green" and "sustainable" stop being available without recognised certification. Offset-based "carbon neutral" claims stop being available at all.

A Revinate mystery-shop of 308 calls to 135 luxury and upscale properties found 92.2% captured no guest contact detail (via Hospitality Net, 4 September 2026).

And Kenya's Tourism (Amendment) Bill 2026 would dissolve the Tourism Research Institute — the source of the region's only free, granular arrivals series — into the Kenya Tourism Board, the agency paid to make the destination look good.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

The industry reflex is to call this unfair, and the reflex is correct and useless. Tanzanian aircraft are safe. Uganda has no Ebola. Most of these lodges really are low-impact, and have been for twenty years without a certificate to show for it.

None of that is the market's problem. The market's problem is verification cost, and it has quietly repriced accordingly. When a claim cannot be checked cheaply, the buyer does not argue with it — the buyer discounts it, or drops it. A European tour operator carrying exposure under the amended unfair commercial practices regime will not litigate your eco copy. It will delete the sentence, and if the sentence was the reason you were in the brochure, it will delete you. That is why the loss arrives as an absence: no email, no negotiation, just a smaller allocation next season.

Which inverts the usual complaint. Verification is not a compliance tax. In a region where airlift, currency and foreign advisory decisions all sit outside our control, it is one of the very few levers entirely inside it — and, at the price of a certification audit, the cheapest yield instrument on the table.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

*Bush and beach properties selling through European operators:* audit every environmental claim on your website, rate sheet and agent pack before 26 September. Evidence it, replace it with a measured fact, or delete it. Then send your top five EU partners a one-page substantiation sheet unprompted — the first supplier to arrive with evidence sets the template everyone else is measured against.

*City hotels:* your exposure is the advisory spread, not the certificate. Segment pre-arrival communication by source market. British and European guests are working from a formally updated, calmer record than American ones; a single generic reassurance letter serves neither.

*Every property, regardless of segment:* mystery-shop your own reservations line this week, once, at 19:00. If your team quotes a rate before taking a name and an email, you are funding someone else's database.

*Owners with debt or a refurbishment case:* download the TRI arrivals reports your model cites and write the retrieval date into the document. Institutions get restructured; their domains go with them.

**THE WATCH**

14 September, EPRA's fuel review. 16 September, the hearing on Kenya's US$50,000 travel-health insurance gazette notice. 26 September, the KAWU arbitration window closes. 27 September, EmpCo applies. 14 October, Kenya's 8% fuel VAT rate expires. 30 November, Ethiopian goes daily into Kilimanjaro and Zanzibar.

For twenty years the honest operator and the loud one competed on equal terms, because a claim cost nothing to make. From 27 September, only one of them can keep talking.
