**1️⃣ 🇹🇿 CASHLESS OR NO LICENCE — TANZANIA'S DIGITAL-PAYMENT RULE NOW GATES YOUR RENEWAL**

Under amendments to the Electronic Transactions Act (effective 1 July 2026) and the Finance Act 2026, hotels, restaurants and safari operators must run a compliant digital-payment facility — **Lipa Namba or TANQR** — to obtain or renew a business licence. Existing operators get a **six-month grace window** (bite ~Jan 2027); after that, no digital rail means no renewal. _(The Citizen / Daily News, effective 1 Jul 2026.)_
🎯 **So what:** Audit your checkout this week. A licence tied to a payment terminal isn't a back-office detail — it's your permission to trade.
🏷 TZ/ZNZ · All · Confirmed

**2️⃣ 🇰🇪 KENYA GAZETTES A US$50,000 TRAVEL-INSURANCE MANDATE — AND IT'S ALREADY IN COURT**

A Kenya Gazette notice (30 July 2026) sets out mandatory travel-health insurance for most international arrivals — minimum cover **US$50,000** (medical, evacuation, repatriation). It is **not yet operational** — the Kenya Tourism Federation is still seeking clarity on approved insurers and whether existing foreign policies count — and it now faces a **High Court challenge, mention 29 September**. _(Tourism Update, 7 Aug; Kenya Gazette, 30 Jul.)_
🎯 **So what:** Don't rewrite pre-arrival comms yet, but brief agents — this stacks on the visa, park and levy costs a multi-country guest already carries.
🏷 KE · All · Reported

**3️⃣ 🇺🇬 KAMPALA'S MARRIOTT FLAGSHIP IS IN FINAL PREP — OPENING IMMINENT, NOT LIVE**

The full Marriott-brand flagship (**181 rooms + 96 executive apartments**, Nsambya) is in final preparations, per the Ministry of Finance and Nile Post (June 2026) — a first for the brand in Uganda beyond its Protea and Four Points properties. No confirmed open date yet.
🎯 **So what:** Kampala corporate-rate compression is coming — independents, defend your accounts now; confirm the open date before quoting it as live.

## LINKEDIN

East Africa keeps announcing record arrivals. Quietly, it is also raising the cost and friction of getting in — and operators, not governments, absorb the first complaint.

Three moves in the space of days. Tanzania now requires a compliant digital-payment facility — Lipa Namba or TANQR — to obtain or renew a hospitality business licence, with a six-month grace window before the rule bites (Electronic Transactions Act amendments, effective 1 July 2026). Kenya has gazetted a mandatory US$50,000 travel-health insurance requirement for most arrivals (30 July) — not yet operational, and already in the High Court, mention 29 September. And Kenya's Parliament has opened public participation on folding the Tourism Research Institute and Tourism Finance Corporation into the Kenya Tourism Board.

Each measure is defensible on its own. The cumulative effect is what a multi-country safari guest actually feels: visa, park fee, conservation levy, bed-night tax — and now insurance and payment-compliance friction stacked on top. That is a competitiveness question, not a compliance footnote.

The operators who win read these early, brief their agents, and write them into net-rate contracts — rather than discovering them at a guest's checkout desk.
