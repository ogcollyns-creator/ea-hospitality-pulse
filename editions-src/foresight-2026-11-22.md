## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**In September the region flew more people into Nairobi than Europe did. Most lodges are still built to sell to Europe.**

In September, 80,763 international passengers landed at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport from East and Central Africa. Europe supplied 61,893 and the Middle East 54,246. Europe accounted for about one in five of those arrivals. If your lodge or resort still plans its year around a European tour-operator contract, it is chasing the smaller share.

**THE SITUATION**

The figures come from the Kenya National Bureau of Statistics' Leading Economic Indicators for September, reported on 18 November. Total international arrivals at JKIA fell from 338,157 in August to 295,381. Europe fell 15.4 per cent and the Middle East 22 per cent. September always follows the August peak, and the press report gives no year-on-year comparison, so this is a snapshot of the mix, not proof of a trend.

The week's airline news pointed the same way. Kenya Airways will add a tenth weekly Nairobi–Mumbai flight from 20 January. Eswatini Air named Nairobi as its next destination. Uganda Airlines began flying Entebbe–Kigali daily on 18 November. At a ministerial aviation session in Windhoek on 17 November, delegates noted that only one in five African city pairs has a direct daily flight. That leaves plenty of room to grow.

The long-haul news was about 2027. On 18 November Tanzania's tourism minister, Ashatu Kijaji, told the London trade that park fees will be waived for approved UK agent and media familiarisation trips next year. That is a sensible move, and a slow one. Meanwhile Kenya has had no new Ebola case and can be declared free of the disease on 23 December, but WHO still rates its risk "high". Last week we showed that the markets reopen months after an all-clear.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

The regional demand is real, but it arrives in a shape that most of the region's leisure inventory cannot sell to.

A delegate from Kampala or Kigali flies in on a daily schedule for a two-day meeting. They book late and direct, and they pay with a regional card. A Mara lodge or a Diani resort is usually sold as part of a multi-night itinerary through an overseas operator, months ahead, in dollars. The demand is landing in the country and the product is in the country, but the two never meet. City hotels capture most of the first group, and bush and beach properties keep waiting for the second group to come back.

This is our inference. No one publishes guest origin by property. But it explains why a quarter in which Europe softens feels worse in the bush than the arrivals data suggests.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

City hotels: you are already selling to this guest, so sell the extension. Partner with a lodge or a coast property and put a two-night add-on on the confirmation email for every regional conference booking.

Bush lodges: build a two- or three-night product at a resident rate, with a road or scheduled-air transfer, bookable on your own site. Don't cut the long-haul rack rate to make it fit.

Beach resorts: weekend packages timed to Friday arrivals on the new daily regional schedules, priced in shillings for East African passport holders.

Tanzanian properties: apply now for the 2027 UK familiarisation programme and offer the trips in shoulder months, when the beds are empty.

**THE WATCH**

KNBS's October indicators, which will show whether East and Central Africa stays on top once the European winter season starts. Kenya's Ebola-free date on 23 December. Kenya Airways' tenth Mumbai frequency on 20 January.

The guests who are already flying in were not in last year's sales plan. They should be in next year's.
