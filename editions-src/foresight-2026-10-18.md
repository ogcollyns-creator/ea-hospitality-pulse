_🏨 EA Hospitality Pulse · Sunday Foresight · Sun 18 Oct 2026_

This week the region sold the next decade. Formula One signed Rwanda for ten years, circuit due early next decade *(13 Oct)*. EAC ministers adopted a 2027–2036 marketing strategy *(15 Oct)*. The same week's statistics describe 2027: urban inflation of 17.2% in Rwanda *(NISR, September)*, 6.8% in Kenya *(KNBS, 30 Sep)* and 4.3% in Tanzania *(NBS, 8 Oct)*.

**What everyone is missing:** nobody sets the cost bases side by side. Rates are quoted in dollars; payroll, power, fuel and food are paid in local currency. A 13-point inflation gap is a margin gap on the same US$ rate, unless the exchange rate offsets it. We hold no Rwandan franc data to say it does *(our inference)*.

## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**East Africa sold the 2030s this week. Your 2027 costs are being set at three different speeds.**

If you contract rates in dollars and pay your people, power and fuel in shillings or francs, the most important number this week was not a race or a strategy. It was the gap between three inflation prints.

**THE SITUATION**

On 13 October, Formula One announced a ten-year African Grand Prix at a purpose-built circuit in Bugesera, about 40 km from Kigali, with the track due early next decade. On 15 October, EAC tourism ministers in Arusha adopted a marketing strategy for 2027 to 2036. In November, Marriott's Autograph Collection opens an 18-suite tented camp in the Serengeti.

The statistics offices published something less glamorous. Rwanda's urban consumer prices were 17.2% higher in September than a year earlier, up from 15.7% in August; the national figure was 20.0%. Within that, the restaurants and hotels group rose 18.9% and the energy index 45%, according to the National Institute of Statistics' September report. Kenya's inflation was 6.8%, with transport at 15.6% (KNBS, 30 September). Tanzania held at 4.3%, transport 14.0% (NBS, 8 October). The latest Uganda figure we could find is 4.1% for August.

Kenya's own costs moved in two directions. EPRA's diesel cap rose KSh9.90 to KSh227.76 a litre from 15 October, while the October electricity add-ons came to about KSh4.10 a unit, from KSh4.16 in September. The Assembly kept fuel VAT at 8% for 90 days.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

Each announcement was read as a story about the future. Each print was read as a story about one country. Together they say something else. The ministers and the race promoters are dating their benefits to the 2030s, while costs are being set now, and they are being set at very different speeds across a region that sells its rooms at one dollar rate.

Rwanda is the sharp case. It has the biggest long-dated promise of the week and the fastest-rising cost base, four times Tanzania's. That does not mean Rwandan margins are collapsing. If the franc weakened in step, the dollar cost of a Kigali payroll may barely have moved, and we hold no exchange-rate data this week to say either way. That is our inference, and it is the honest limit of the argument. But an owner who has not checked is guessing.

The second misread is the visa. The single-visa study is due 20 October, with comments by 30 November. That is a process, not a product. Nothing agreed in Arusha makes a 2027 itinerary cheaper to sell.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

Rwanda, city and gorilla lodges: add a dated cost-review clause to 2027 dollar contracts, and list which of your costs are paid in francs. Do not underwrite anything on Formula One.

Kenya, all segments: budget transfers and generators on KSh227.76 diesel until the 14 November review. Do not budget a power windfall from one month's easing.

Tanzania mainland and Zanzibar: your cost base is the lowest of the markets we can measure, and the US lowered its advisory to Level 2 on 16 October. Reopen US quotes. Zanzibar took 124,481 visitors in August, up 18%, with estimated bed occupancy of 92.9% (OCGS, 8 September, an older release), so peak rates have room to hold.

Uganda: we could not find a September print. Ask your accountant for your own input-cost change before you reprice.

**THE WATCH**

Tanzania's October inflation on 9 November. Rwanda's October figure around 10 November, going by the release pattern. The EPRA review on 14 November. The EAC visa comment deadline on 30 November.

The region announced a decade. The rate card is for 2027, and the costs behind it are not moving together.
