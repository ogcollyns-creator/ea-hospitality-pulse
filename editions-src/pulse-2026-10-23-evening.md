1️⃣ Kenya recorded no new Ebola cases in the 24 hours to 22 October, holding at nine

Ministry of Health figures: all nine cases sit in one cluster. The index case died on 5 October; eight patients (four relatives, four health workers) are in isolation and reported stable. All 14 samples tested in the day were negative, all 135 contacts of the first case are traced, and the first group finishes 21-day monitoring on Sunday 25 October. 709,093 travellers have been screened at entry points, 3,107 in the latest 24 hours. Government says travel, trade and public gatherings stay unrestricted *(Ministry statement 22 Oct, via The Standard 22 Oct 16:53 and The Star 23 Oct 06:52)*.
Our inference: one quiet day is not a clearance. The Standard notes anyone exposed to a later case is monitored from that later date, and the ninth case tested positive on 19 October after falling ill in quarantine *(Citizen Digital, 20 Oct)*, so some clocks may have restarted.
🎯 So what: do not reprice or relaunch on a plateau. Keep flexible-rebooking terms and brief reception staff on the screening questions guests will meet at the airport.

2️⃣ Foreign government decisions, not Kenya's case count, now decide how many guests can travel

## LINKEDIN

Kenya's Ebola count went flat this week, and hotel owners should resist reading that as good news for bookings.

The Ministry of Health reported no new cases in the 24 hours to 22 October, leaving the total at nine, all in one cluster. All 135 contacts of the first case are traced and the first group leaves quarantine on 25 October. Screening has covered 709,093 travellers, and the government says travel and gatherings remain unrestricted (The Standard, 22 October; The Star, 23 October).

The booking problem sits elsewhere. In three days the US raised Kenya to Level 3, El Salvador refused entry to travellers from or through Kenya, Uganda, Rwanda and Ethiopia, and Ghana tightened screening at Accra. Those are decisions by foreign governments, and we have seen no published criteria for lifting any of them.

Our inference: a flat case count will not reopen those markets on its own, and one quiet day is not a clearance because contact monitoring clocks restart after each new exposure.

For operators, the practical moves are to keep flexible-rebooking terms, ask each source-market agent which rule applies to their guests, and write refund and date-change terms into contracts before peak-season deposits arrive.
