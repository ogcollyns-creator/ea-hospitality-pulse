## LINKEDIN
Uganda's inflation rose to 4.6% in September, while Kenya's hit 6.8%. For anyone running properties across the region, that gap matters more than either number.

The Uganda Bureau of Statistics reported prices up 0.9% in the month, led by food after prolonged dry spells, with hospitality and transport prices also higher and fuel above UGX 6,600 a litre in some areas. The statistician expects pressure to ease from November if the rains improve. Kenya's figure was 6.8%. Tanzania's latest published rate is 4.3% for August, so September is not yet comparable.

The inference is ours: the three markets are repricing at different speeds, and Kenyan cost lines are moving fastest. A group that budgets one regional cost uplift will overshoot in Uganda and undershoot in Kenya.

Three steps. Write an escalator into 2027 Kenyan supplier contracts now. Hold Ugandan food contracts for the November window. Wait for Tanzania's September figure before fixing any cross-border rate.
