## LINKEDIN

Most East African operators are briefing their agents on "the US Ebola restrictions." That phrase is now wrong in a way that costs bookings.

The CDC renewed its entry order on 12 August, under 42 CFR 71.40, continuing restrictions for travellers recently in the DRC, Uganda or South Sudan. For Uganda the rule is proportionate: US citizens and nationals aren't barred, they're rerouted through Dulles, Atlanta, Houston or JFK for public health screening. An inconvenience, not a deal-breaker.

Canada is a different instrument. Since 20 July, under the Quarantine Act, anyone who has been in Uganda or South Sudan within 21 days may enter Canada — then must quarantine 21 days on arrival unless exempt. For a Canadian guest on a Bwindi gorilla circuit, that is three weeks of annual leave spent at home after the holiday. The measures run to 29 August 2026.

Same outbreak, two governments, two rules that land completely differently on a guest's calendar. A single "North America" paragraph in your pre-arrival comms under-warns Canadians and over-warns Americans at once.

Split the comms this week, then diarise 29 August — Canada's measures carry a stated expiry rather than a rolling renewal, and Uganda's own 42-day all-clear completes within days of it.
