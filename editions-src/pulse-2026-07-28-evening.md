1️⃣ THE COMMENT WINDOW ON THE UGANDA ENTRY BAN CLOSES FRIDAY

The CDC order suspending entry to the US of anyone present in DRC, Uganda or South Sudan in the previous 21 days was published in the Federal Register on 16 July (91 FR 43636, docket CDC-2026-0892). It carries a 15-day public comment period that closes **31 July**. In the same document CDC answers the comments it received last round — and two of them asked for exactly what Ugandan tourism needs: that Uganda be assessed independently of DRC. CDC refused, in writing: "the outbreak remains interconnected across national borders." We have not seen a single East African tourism body, airline or destination marketing organisation on that docket.
🎯 So what: If you sell Uganda to Americans, file a comment at regulations.gov under docket CDC-2026-0892 before Friday, with your own cancellation and rebooking numbers in it. Evidence beats sentiment, and this is the only formal channel that exists.

2️⃣ THE BAN NOW CATCHES US GREEN CARD HOLDERS

Quietly, the exemption that let US lawful permanent residents through was deleted. 42 CFR 71.40(f) was amended on 27 May (91 FR 31362) and the 13 July order applies "including lawful permanent residents of the United States." A green card holder who treks Bwindi cannot go home for 21 days. That is a distinct, high-value segment — diaspora, US-based professionals, repeat gorilla clients — and most of them do not know.
🎯 So what: Put a one-line non-US-citizen warning into every American-market quote and pre-arrival email tonight. Offer a free date change rather than lose the booking to a surprise at the airport.

## LINKEDIN BIG READ

East Africa is losing an argument in Washington it never turned up to make

There is a US federal docket open right now that will help decide whether your American guests can fly home after a Ugandan safari. It closes on Friday. As far as we can tell, not one East African hotel group, tour operator, airline or tourism board has filed a word on it.

**THE SITUATION**

On 13 July the US Assistant Secretary for Health signed an order under sections 362 and 365 of the Public Health Service Act continuing the suspension of the right to introduce into the United States anyone who has been in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Uganda or South Sudan in the preceding 21 days. It was published in the Federal Register on 16 July at 91 FR 43636, under docket CDC-2026-0892. It runs to 4:59pm Eastern on 12 August. And it was issued with a 15-day comment period that closes on 31 July.

Read the response-to-comments section and the picture sharpens considerably. CDC received five comments on the previous order. Two of them asked that Uganda be evaluated independently and removed from the designated list, citing Uganda's cross-border travel restrictions, enhanced border screening and public health controls. CDC declined. Its reasoning, verbatim: "the outbreak remains interconnected across national borders" and regional epidemiology must govern.

So the argument East African tourism most needs to win has already been put — by someone else, unnamed, in five paragraphs — and lost.

Meanwhile the facts on the Ugandan side have moved. Uganda's Ministry of Health dashboard, updated today, shows 20 confirmed cases, two deaths, zero current admissions, and — the number that matters — zero active contacts under follow-up, with 821 of 831 listed contacts having completed their 21 days. The 42-day countdown began on 16 July. Today is day 12.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

Three things.

First, the calendar does not work in Uganda's favour and no amount of good epidemiology fixes it. The US order expires on 12 August. Uganda's 42-day all-clear lands around 27 August. Whoever signs the next 30-day order in early August will be signing it a fortnight before Uganda can legally be declared free of Ebola — and on a risk assessment dominated by DRC, where CDC's own document records cases more than doubling from 837 to 1,873 between 15 June and 12 July, with an estimated 80 percent of new cases in the epicentre not linked to any known contact. If that order renews on pattern, restrictions run into mid-September. Plan for that, not for a late-August reprieve.

Second, the ban has quietly widened. The exemption for US lawful permanent residents was removed when 42 CFR 71.40(f) was amended on 27 May, and the July order applies expressly to green card holders. A US permanent resident who treks Bwindi cannot return home for 21 days. That is a distinct and lucrative segment and almost none of them have been told.

Third — and Kenyan operators should read this twice — the order names Jomo Kenyatta International Airport in Nairobi, alongside Addis Ababa, Doha, Dubai, Brussels and Istanbul, as a transit hub through which outbreak-region travellers reach the United States. Kenya is not restricted. But being named in a US federal instrument as a vector corridor is a reputational asset that decays quietly, and it is the sort of sentence that gets quoted back at you in three years.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

File. Docket CDC-2026-0892 at regulations.gov, before Friday. Not a plea — a submission with your own numbers: cancellations, rebookings, screening protocols at your properties, the health infrastructure between your lodge and the nearest treatment centre. CDC's stated reason for the comment period is to inform the forthcoming risk assessment. It is asking for exactly the evidence you have and nobody is sending.

Rewrite your American-market terms tonight. Every quote to a US-domiciled guest who is not a US citizen needs a plain-English line about the 21-day rule. Free date changes are cheaper than chargebacks and far cheaper than the review.

Price September, not August. If you have been holding inventory for a post-all-clear surge, move that assumption out by four to six weeks and discount August rather than defend it.

Sell the divergence. The UK, Germany and most European source markets have not matched the US restriction. Your US pipeline is impaired; your European one largely is not. Shift marketing spend accordingly this week, not next quarter.

**THE WATCH**

31 July — comment period closes on docket CDC-2026-0892.
Around 10-12 August — the next CDC order signs or lapses; 12 August is the hard expiry.
Around 27 August — Uganda's 42-day countdown completes, absent a new case.

The rules that will govern your American bookings in September are being written this week, in public, by people who have invited you to comment. Silence is also a submission.
