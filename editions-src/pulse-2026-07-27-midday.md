## LINKEDIN

Nanyuki did not lose a booking last week. It lost a business model.

The Standard reports today that Britain has moved Exercise Haraka Storm — due to run in Laikipia from September to November — to Tanzania, after Kenya withheld the training licences BATUK needs. Friday's story was a delay. Today's is a relocation.

The number that matters to operators: British military activity injects roughly £58m a year, about Sh10bn, into the Nanyuki-area economy, and BATUK rotates up to 10,000 soldiers through Kenya annually.

Here is what makes this different from an ordinary demand shock. BATUK room-nights were counter-seasonal. They filled Laikipia when the safari calendar didn't, they paid in sterling, they booked long and they didn't haggle. Nothing in the leisure market replaces that profile — you can replace the volume, but not the shape.

So the honest question for a Laikipia owner this week isn't whether the licences get renewed. It's what your P&L looks like if they don't, for three years. That is a different capital plan, a different staffing model, and probably a different lender conversation.

And for northern Tanzania: a September-to-November accommodation and logistics requirement has just landed in your market. Someone will win that supply contract. It should be someone who reads their market this week.
