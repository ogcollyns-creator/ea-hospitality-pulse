**1️⃣ KENYA IS ABOUT TO PRICE A LICENCE OFF YOUR TURNOVER**

The Senate Committee on Delegated Legislation took evidence on Thursday 17 September from nine industry bodies on the Tourism (Tourism Enterprises) Regulations 2026 — **Legal Notice No. 127 of 2026**, published 10 July and tabled in the Senate on 17 July *(People Daily, 18 September 2026)*. The regime licences, classifies and grades hotels, restaurants, tour operators, travel agents, tourist transport, air charter and nature-based enterprises under the Tourism Regulatory Authority. Licence fees are banded on **annual gross revenue** — reported from KSh 16,000 for a new or sub-KSh 2m restaurant up to **KSh 250,000** *(The Kenya Times, 18 September 2026)*. Specified offences carry up to KSh 1m, six months, or both.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**
Kenya already charges 2% of gross sales on accommodation, food and drink as the Tourism Levy *(Tourism Fund)*. Band a licence on gross revenue too and you have a second turnover charge wearing a licence's clothes. But the fee is not the winnable argument. The associations also told the committee they were **not consulted before the regulations were drafted** — a public-participation defect. That is the ground on which Kenyan instruments actually get annulled, and it is on a clock: under the **Statutory Instruments Act No. 23 of 2013, s.15**, the committee must report within 28 sitting days of referral. Chair Sen. Mwenda Gataya (Tharaka Nithi) put it plainly: the committee will decide "whether to annul or accede to the Regulations."

🎯 **So what:** Count your licensable entities before you budget one fee — a lodge with a transport arm and a tour-operating arm is three applications, not one. Then get a written submission to the committee naming the public-participation defect specifically. Verbal evidence on Thursday is not a filing.

## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**Kenya is about to price a tourism licence off your turnover. The industry is arguing about the wrong thing.**

On Thursday, nine of Kenya's tourism associations sat in front of the Senate Committee on Delegated Legislation and told it that the proposed licence fees were too high. They are probably right. It is also the argument least likely to work.

**THE SITUATION**

The Tourism (Tourism Enterprises) Regulations 2026 — Legal Notice No. 127 of 2026 — were published on 10 July and tabled in the Senate on 17 July. They establish licensing, classification and grading for a wide slice of the sector: hotels, restaurants, tour operators, travel agencies, tourist transport, air charter operators and nature-based enterprises, all administered by the Tourism Regulatory Authority. Licence fees are banded on annual gross revenue, reported as running from KSh 16,000 for a new or sub-KSh 2 million restaurant up to KSh 250,000. Specified offences attract up to KSh 1 million, six months' imprisonment, or both.

The associations that appeared — among them the Kenya Association of Hotelkeepers and Caterers, KATO, the Tour Operators Society of Kenya, the Kenya Coast Tourism Association and Ecotourism Kenya — argued that the cost would deter investment and push guests toward rival destinations. Committee chair Senator Mwenda Gataya said the committee would engage the regulator before deciding "whether to annul or accede to the Regulations."

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

Two things.

First, the pricing mechanism, not the price. Kenya already charges 2 per cent of gross sales on accommodation, food and drink through the Tourism Levy, remitted monthly to the Tourism Fund. Band a licence on annual gross revenue as well and you have not created a fee — you have created a second turnover charge with a licence's name on it. A turnover charge is indifferent to whether you made money. It lands on the low-margin coast operator and the full-occupancy city hotel in the same proportion, and it is the one cost line a bad season cannot reduce. Compare the regional practice: Tanzania's TALA runs on flat rates — TZS 200,000 a year for a locally owned tour operator, USD 1,000 for a foreign-owned one — and Rwanda introduced its tourism operating licence at a flat RWF 80,000 application fee. Flat fees are regressive on small operators; revenue-banded fees are a tax. Kenya is moving in a direction its neighbours have not.

Second, and more urgent: there is a clock, and the industry has been arguing the wrong case against it. Under the Statutory Instruments Act No. 23 of 2013, the committee must report within 28 sitting days of referral. A committee cannot easily redraft a fee schedule; that is the regulator's business. What a committee — and a court — can act on is procedure. Almost in passing on Thursday, the associations said they had not been engaged before the regulations were drafted, raising the constitutional public participation requirement. That is the ground on which Kenyan subsidiary legislation actually falls. It is also the ground that needs to be on the record in writing, with dates and correspondence, before the committee reports. Oral evidence at a sitting is not a filing.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

City hotels: a full-service property with meaningful F&B lands at or near the top band. Carry KSh 250,000 in the 2027 budget as a provisional line and check whether classification and grading renewal falls in the same cycle, because two compliance events in one quarter is a cash-flow problem, not just a cost.

Bush lodges and camps: count entities, not properties. A camp that also holds a tourist transport licence and runs its own tour-operating arm is looking at banded fees applied separately. Most owners will under-budget this by a factor of two or three.

Beach resorts: your exposure is largely indirect. The coast's restaurant, watersports and ground-handling supply chain sits in the lower bands, where a fee is a bigger share of margin. Their compliance cost arrives in your rates within a season.

Everyone: KATO has already proposed the constructive version — digital, risk-based licensing on 12-month rolling validity rather than fixed calendar years. That is the amendment worth backing, because it gives the committee something to accede to rather than a binary between annulment and waving it through.

**THE WATCH**

Whether the committee reports recommending amendment or annulment, rather than unqualified accession, before the year ends. Watch the Senate Hansard, not the trade press.
