## LINKEDIN

Something changed in East Africa this week that most operators haven't registered, because it looks like more of the same story.

It isn't. Until now, the Ebola situation was an advisory story — governments telling citizens not to travel. That affects whether guests *want* to come.

It is now an entry-rules story. Rwanda is refusing entry to any foreign national who has travelled to, or transited through, the DRC in the previous 30 days. Kenya and Tanzania have introduced surveillance forms and screening at all points of entry.

The distinction matters enormously. Advisories suppress demand. Entry rules remove the ability to travel at all — and no marketing, and no discount, solves a closed border.

Three practical consequences.

First: the test is transit, not destination. A guest connecting through Kinshasa or Goma is caught by Rwanda's rule even if the DRC was never their destination. Audit your bookings for connections, not just endpoints.

Second: Kenya and Tanzania have zero cases and precautionary screening only, yet are routinely described as restricted. If you operate there, correct that framing — repeatedly.

Third, and least noticed: Uganda's outbreak may be declared over around 27 August. That will not lift these entry rules, because they are keyed to the DRC outbreak, which continues. Two separate clocks. Anyone building a single recovery plan on the Uganda date is planning against the wrong calendar.
