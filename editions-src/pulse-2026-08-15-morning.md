**1️⃣ 🇰🇪 YOU GOT SH5 OFF DIESEL. THE MARKET GAVE SH19.28**

EPRA's schedule takes effect **today**, to 14 September: Nairobi diesel **Sh217.86/L** (–Sh5), petrol held at **Sh214.03**, kerosene **Sh191.38**.

But landed diesel fell **13.08%** last month — US$855.59/m³ from US$984.37 — while landed petrol **rose 6.9%**. On the regulator's own disclosures diesel should have dropped **Sh19.28, to Sh203.58**. The State used diesel to cross-subsidise petrol, plus **Sh938m** of stabilisation (Business Daily, 14 Aug, quoting acting EPRA DG Joseph Oketch).

## LINKEDIN

Kenyan operators woke up to a Sh5 cut in diesel and most will file it under good news. The regulator's own disclosures say it should have been Sh19.28.

EPRA's schedule effective 15 August prices Nairobi diesel at Sh217.86 a litre, with petrol held at Sh214.03. Landed diesel costs fell 13.08% last month, to US$855.59 per cubic metre from US$984.37, while landed petrol rose 6.9%. In line with global prices, diesel should have fallen to Sh203.58.

The difference is a cross-subsidy — diesel users funding petrol users, plus Sh938m of stabilisation support — per Business Daily on 14 August, quoting acting EPRA Director General Joseph Oketch.

For a lodge or a resort this is not an abstraction. Diesel runs the game drives, the transfers, the generators and the laundry. Hospitality is carrying roughly Sh14.28 a litre above market so Nairobi's private motorists are insulated from the Iran-war spike.

Two things follow. Refuse any supplier fuel surcharge this cycle: global diesel fell 13.08% and you can now cite the number. And do not budget the Sh5 forward — the Petroleum Development Levy kitty is nearly depleted, the mechanism is being challenged in court, and the 8% fuel-VAT concession expires on 14 October.

The relief you were given is smaller than it looks and shorter than it sounds.
