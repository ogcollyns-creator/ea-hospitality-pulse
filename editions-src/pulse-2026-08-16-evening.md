**1️⃣ 🌍 THE VACCINE LINE IS NO LONGER SUPPORTABLE — AND THE RELEASE NUMBER ROSE**

At midday we said the condition freeing Uganda from the US entry order is a DRC number. Tonight we have it. Africa CDC's Emergency Consultative Group, chaired by Prof. Salim Abdool Karim, reports **4,449 confirmed cases, 2,061 deaths, 46.3% fatality** in DRC, **~100 new cases a day**, **R(t) consistently above 1** (Africa CDC statement, **14 August**). Its words: the epidemic is "still growing rapidly, and faster than previous Ebola epidemics." Against the US order's 4,318 and WHO AFRO's 4,381 for 9 August, the curve has not turned.


**What everyone is missing: the vaccine sentence.** On Ervebo against Bundibugyo the ECG is blunt — **"no reliable evidence of its clinical efficacy"**, and "a reasonable chance the vaccine is not effective." It recommends use under **trial protocol**, not rollout.

Since 2019 the reassurance script has been "Ebola is vaccine-preventable." For this strain, Africa's own experts say that is not established. And **63% of deaths in the last fortnight occurred in the community**, not treatment centres — detection, not capacity, is the constraint.

🎯 **So what:** Strike the vaccine line from every agent script and insurer letter tonight. Use transmission facts instead — no Ugandan case since 21 June, none outside Kampala, no community spread. Brief 11 September as continuation, not release.

**2️⃣ 🌍 TWO CDC PAGES, TWO DIFFERENT MAPS**

We checked both tonight; we haven't seen this reported anywhere.

CDC's **Travel Health Notices index** (read 16 Aug, ~19:00 EAT) states under Level 4: _"Currently there are no Travel Health Notices at this level."_ Its Ebola notices — Level 3 for **Ituri, North and South Kivu**, Level 2 for **DRC and Uganda** — are dated **15 June 2026**.

CDC's **Ebola situation summary** (page dated **updated 12 August 2026**) instead _"recommends avoiding all travel to Ituri and Nord-Kivu... and avoiding nonessential travel to Haut-Uele and Tshopo."_

Not the same map. Risk vendors and insurers ingest the index because it is machine-readable — so a client's system sees June while their risk officer reads August.

## LINKEDIN BIG READ

For seven years, everyone selling East Africa has had one sentence to fall back on when a client raises Ebola. There is a vaccine.

On 14 August, Africa's own scientists took that sentence away.

Africa CDC's Emergency Consultative Group — chaired by Prof. Salim Abdool Karim and drawn from the continent's most senior virologists and epidemiologists — published its assessment of Ervebo against Bundibugyo virus, the strain driving the current outbreak. The language is unusually direct for a scientific advisory body: there is "no reliable evidence of its clinical efficacy." Immunological data are difficult to interpret without a validated correlate of protection. The animal efficacy studies rest on very small numbers. The group states plainly that there remains "a reasonable chance the vaccine is not effective."

Its recommendation is not a rollout. It is a ring-vaccination clinical trial modelled on Ebola Ça Suffit!, plus compassionate provision to frontline workers under a study protocol — explicitly to generate the evidence that does not yet exist.

This matters to hoteliers, not just epidemiologists, because the reassurance economy of a health shock runs on exactly this kind of sentence. It appears in agent briefings, duty-of-care packs, and the reply a general manager sends a corporate account at eleven at night. Ervebo's licensure was for Zaire ebolavirus. Bundibugyo is a different species. Much of the industry has elided that distinction for three months, in good faith, because nobody told us not to. Now someone has.

The epidemiology is no kinder. DRC stands at 4,449 confirmed cases and 2,061 deaths, a case fatality rate of 46.3%, roughly 100 new cases a day, R(t) consistently above 1. The group's own words: the epidemic is "still growing rapidly, and faster than previous Ebola epidemics."

Read that against the sequence. The US entry order signed on 12 August cited 4,318 cases as of 9 August. WHO AFRO's Situation Report 13, same date, said 4,381. Five days on, the ECG says 4,449. Three official numbers, one direction of travel.

That direction should reset September planning. The US order restricting entry from DRC, Uganda and South Sudan expires on 11 September. Uganda's 42-day all-clear completes around 28 August, and much of the East African trade has been selling that date as the turning point. It is not. Washington's stated ground for keeping Uganda listed is cross-border risk from DRC, not Ugandan epidemiology. The governing number is the one Africa CDC just published, and it is rising.

A second finding deserves more attention than it will get. Sixty-three per cent of Ebola deaths in the past fortnight occurred in the community, only about a third in treatment centres. Deaths outside the system mean cases never detected and contacts never traced. Meanwhile DRC's laboratory capacity has gone from two testing sites to nineteen, plus two mobile units, above 3,000 samples a day. The constraint is no longer the ability to test; it is people reaching the system at all. Capacity metrics will keep improving while the curve refuses to bend — and that gap is where premature optimism gets manufactured.

So what should an operator do this week?

Remove the vaccine line. Not soften it — remove it. Replace it with facts that are both true and stronger: Uganda has recorded no confirmed case since 21 June, every Ugandan case was in Kampala, none arose from community spread, and no CDC travel health notice names Kenya, Tanzania, Zanzibar or Rwanda.

Then check which document your counterparty is reading. CDC's travel health notices index — the machine-readable page corporate risk vendors ingest — carries no Level 4 notice anywhere in the world tonight and dates its Ebola notices 15 June. CDC's Ebola situation summary, updated 12 August, advises avoiding all travel to Ituri and North Kivu and nonessential travel to Haut-Uélé and Tshopo. Same government, same week, two maps. When an account cites "CDC guidance", the useful question is which page, dated when. Put both in your response pack.

And drop the assumption that a calendar date releases anything. The all-clear is a Ugandan milestone; the restriction is a Congolese arithmetic problem. Until roughly 100 cases a day becomes a materially smaller number, plan the American segment on continuation.

The honest version of this story is more persuasive than the reassuring one, and it survives contact with a risk officer who has read the source. That was always true. It is now unavoidable.
