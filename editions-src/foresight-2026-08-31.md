## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**East Africa Has Built Africa's Least Cancellable Hotel Pipeline — Just as Its Yield Per Guest Started Falling**

Kenya has 6,190 hotel rooms in its development pipeline, and 4,922 of them — 79.5% — are already under construction. Ethiopia sits at 79.9%, Tanzania at 77.5%. Egypt, which owns more than a third of Africa's entire pipeline, is at 51.4%. Nigeria is at 39.2%. Cape Verde is at 8.6%. Those figures come from W Hospitality Group's 2026 Hotel Chain Development Pipelines in Africa report, published on 10 March 2026, and the trade read them as a vote of confidence in East Africa.

They are not. They are a measure of how little room the region has left to change its mind.

**THE SITUATION**

Start with the week just gone, because it makes the point better than any forecast.

Kenya Airways released its half-year on 25 August. Revenue of KSh81.2bn, up 9.1% on H1 2025 and the second-highest first half in the airline's history. Net loss of KSh16.08bn, against KSh12.15bn a year earlier — 32% worse. Operating costs up 13.8% to KSh91.9bn; the operating loss widening from KSh6.2bn to KSh10.6bn. Fuel rose 32% to KSh29bn, roughly 52% of direct operating costs, with jet fuel averaging US$142 a barrel over the period. Five days later, aviation workers downed tools at the Kenya Airports Authority, KCAA and Jambojet, and by 14:00 on 30 August only 30% of Kenya Airways' scheduled flights had departed — the third round of the same collective bargaining dispute this year.

On 28 August in Bwejuu, Tanzania collected Africa's Leading Destination and Africa's Leading Tourist Board at the World Travel Awards Africa Gala; Zanzibar took Africa's Leading Beach Destination; TANAPA won six park titles, Serengeti among them. Also true this year: mainland Tanzania has overtaken Zanzibar on visitor spend per person per night.

And Kenya's own 2025 tourism performance report records 2,652,540 international arrivals and KSh501.34bn in earnings — both records — while earnings per arrival rose 3.13% against the report's own 4.075% inflation assumption. In real terms, Kenya sold more nights for less money per guest.

That is three record top lines and three deteriorating unit economics, inside one week's reading.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

A development pipeline is not one thing. It is two, and they behave in opposite ways when demand disappoints.

A room in planning is an option. It has a site, a brand and a spreadsheet, and it can be quietly allowed to expire at almost no cost. A room under construction is a commitment: contractors engaged, drawdowns scheduled, an opening date the brand has already put in a press release. It arrives whether or not the market wants it.

So the honest way to read W Hospitality's construction-ratio table is as a measure of stored flexibility. Egypt can defer 22,362 rooms. Nigeria can defer 5,152. Cape Verde, at 8.6% under construction, has 3,954 rooms of pure optionality. Kenya can defer 1,268. Tanzania can defer 937.

When African demand softens — and Kenya's arrivals growth already halved, from 15.71% in 2024 to 7.19% in 2025 — every other major market on the continent can adjust through supply. East Africa can only adjust through price.

That distinction decides who absorbs the pain. Deferred supply costs a developer an option premium. Delivered supply costs an incumbent operator rate. Roughly 2,000 branded rooms opened in Nairobi between 2023 and 2025, and the market recorded occupancy declines of close to ten percentage points alongside pressure on rates; around 1,500 more rooms across ten hotels are expected in 2026, with the international-chain pipeline for the city up 58.3% to 3,650 rooms. A new branded hotel opens with debt service, an empty guest history and no legacy rate to protect. It will buy occupancy. It will buy it from you.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

**City hotels, Nairobi, Kampala, Kigali, Dar.** Budget 2027 on rate defence, not occupancy growth. Find out which specific properties in your competitive set have topped out and when they open — construction status is public, and an opening date twelve months out is a pricing event you can plan for rather than discover. Lock multi-year corporate and embassy accounts now, before a new opening undercuts you into them.

**Bush and safari.** Your protection is that a lodge is a location, not a box, and locations are finite. Sell the concession, the traversing rights and the guide, not the suite. Serengeti's luxury pipeline is only 129 rooms across three projects (W Hospitality via Nairametrics, 27 July 2026) — scarcity remains your asset, so behave like it and stop discounting into shoulder season.

**Beach and island.** Zanzibar has the largest luxury hotel pipeline in Sub-Saharan Africa — five projects, 593 rooms, including one of 323 keys — arriving into an island whose per-night spend has just been overtaken by the mainland. If you sell undifferentiated package beach, you are the segment that new supply is aimed at. Move now to something a 323-key resort cannot replicate, or accept that you compete on price from 2027.

**Everyone.** Write down the one thing you sell that a newly built branded property down the road cannot copy in its first three years. If the honest answer is "nothing", that is your 2027 capital plan.
