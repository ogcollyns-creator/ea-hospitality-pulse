## LINKEDIN

The most useful thing a market-intelligence brief can do on a quiet news day is audit its own forecasts in public — so here is ours, four days out from the call that matters.

On Tuesday 12 August, the US CDC's Section 362 Ebola entry suspension covering the DR Congo, Uganda and South Sudan reaches its expiry. Most coverage frames this as "renew or lift." That misreads the decision.

The DRC's outbreak is still accelerating — 4,053 confirmed cases and 1,850 deaths to 5 August, up from 3,874 a week earlier. On those numbers alone, renewal is the base case.

But Uganda is a different country on a different curve. It declared itself Ebola-free on 28 July, its last case was 21 June, and it is now 48 days clear. The live question for anyone running rooms in Uganda is narrower and more consequential: does CDC decouple Uganda from the DRC and drop it from the list?

For an Entebbe city hotel or a western-Uganda lodge, that single administrative choice is the difference between another month frozen out of the US source market and the first green light for recovery. Our logged call says renewal with DRC still named; Uganda's decoupling is the one we're watching hardest.
