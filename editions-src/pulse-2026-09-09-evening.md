## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**Kenya's disease map and its hotel map are now the same map**

At 11:30 Eastern on 8 September, Canada quietly changed one line on its Kenya travel advice. Not the risk level — that still reads "exercise a high degree of caution." The change was a health notice: Kenya has been added to Ottawa's dengue watchlist, a list of eleven destinations "reporting higher than expected numbers of dengue cases."

It is the third such flag from a G7 government in five weeks. The US Embassy in Nairobi issued a dengue health alert naming Kenya's coast, Wajir and Garissa on 3 August; Germany logged the same CDC notice that day. None of the three changed a travel warning. All three changed what a corporate duty-of-care officer, or a travel insurer's claims handler, sees when they search.

One detail worth having to hand while the searching happens: Canada's avoid-all-travel zone for the Somalia border counties explicitly excludes Lamu Island and Manda Island, where the advice is the national "exercise a high degree of caution". Lamu operators fielding a nervous agent should send the carve-out, not the county name.

The instinct in the trade is to shrug. Level 1 is the mildest category Canada publishes. But the shrug misses where the notice points.

The outbreak Kenya's Ministry of Health is fighting sits in the north-east. It activated its Public Health Emergency Operations Centre for 1,678 cases and five suspected deaths across Garissa and Wajir as at 26 August, and 1,276 of those are in Dadaab. That is a refugee-complex emergency. It is nowhere near a hotel.

The advisories do not name Dadaab. They name the coast, and they are right to. Between January and May, Mombasa County recorded 3,989 dengue cases — more than double the entire north-eastern outbreak that triggered the emergency centre — with Lamu on 445 and Kilifi on 339. Nationally, Kenya averages some 16,675 cases a year. The disease has been sitting in the coastal counties all along. It simply never produced a single dramatic cluster, so it never got an emergency operations centre attached to it.

Now put the second outbreak on the same sheet. On Monday, Public Health PS Mary Muthoni reported 1,298 confirmed mpox cases and 19 deaths across 40 of Kenya's 47 counties, and named seven as highest-risk on transport-corridor grounds: Mombasa, Nairobi, Busia, Makueni, Kiambu, Kilifi and Nakuru. Mombasa leads on 452. Nairobi is second on 320.

Two unrelated pathogens, two independent surveillance systems, one geography. The counties carrying Kenya's highest disease burden are the counties carrying Kenya's rooms.

That is uncomfortable, and it is also the least interesting part. The interesting part is operational, and almost nobody has acted on it.

Every serious property in East Africa runs a vector-control protocol. Nets over beds. Fogging at dusk. Repellent at turndown. Screens on the maintenance round. That protocol was designed for *Anopheles*, the malaria mosquito, which feeds at night. It is close to worthless against *Aedes aegypti*, which carries dengue, bites in broad daylight, and breeds not in swamps but in small volumes of clean standing water — a plant saucer, a blocked gutter, a boat cover, an ornamental water feature, the drainage channel around a pool deck.

The features a resort spends capital on are the features that breed the vector, and the hours a resort protects are the wrong hours. The Ministry's own guidance is explicit, asking institutions to prevent *daytime* bites and to empty, scrub and tightly cover water-storage containers weekly. Read as an operating instruction rather than a public-health leaflet, that is a housekeeping schedule change, and it costs almost nothing.

The timing is not neutral. The Central Bank of Kenya's market perceptions survey put average forward hotel bookings for August to November at 56.25 per cent against 49.5 per cent a year earlier, with September the weakest of the four at 53 per cent — a recovering pipeline meeting a rising advisory profile in the same quarter. The Ministry convened an El Niño stakeholder meeting today, with around eighteen counties flagged highest-risk for the coming rains. Standing water connects all of it.

There is no crisis here, and operators who manufacture one will do themselves damage. Level 1 notices do not stop bookings. What they create is a written record that a well-run business was told. The properties that look competent in ninety days will be the ones that changed a cleaning schedule in September, wrote two lines for their agents, and briefed a front desk on fever-and-rash escalation — before anyone asked them what they had done.
