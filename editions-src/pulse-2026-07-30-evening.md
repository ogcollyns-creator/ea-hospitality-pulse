## LINKEDIN BIG READ

Uganda is Ebola-free. That won't reopen the American market.

If you run a gorilla lodge in Bwindi or a city hotel in Kampala, Tuesday's declaration felt like the all-clear. It is real, and it is earned. But it is not the news that reopens your most valuable long-haul market — and confusing the two will cost you.

**THE SITUATION**

On 28 July, Uganda's Ministry of Health declared the 2026 Bundibugyo outbreak over: 20 confirmed cases, 18 recoveries, two deaths, every transmission chain reconstructed and closed after a full 42-day surveillance window. Textbook containment of an imported outbreak.

Across the border, WHO's Weekly External Situation Report 11 (data to 26 July) describes a different reality. The Democratic Republic of Congo has recorded 3,262 confirmed cases and 1,437 deaths — a case-fatality rate near 44%. Ituri province alone accounts for 2,901 cases across 28 of its 36 health zones, and fewer than 80% of identified contacts are under follow-up, meaning transmission is slipping past the traced chains.

The instrument that matters to American demand is the US CDC's Section 362 entry suspension. Renewed on 13 July for a further 30 days, it runs to roughly 12 August and names Uganda alongside DRC and South Sudan.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

The declaration and the ban answer different questions. Uganda's declaration certifies that Uganda is clean. The American ban asks whether the region is — and it is built around the DRC caseload, which is still rising and now spreading beyond traced contacts. A ban designed on regional risk does not lift because one country in the region recovered; it lifts when the source stops burning. On Congo's current trajectory, that is not 12 August.

So "Ebola-free Uganda" carries real reassurance value for a British or German traveller who reads a headline and books. Its market-access value for a US-origin guest — or their insurer, or their employer's travel policy — is close to nil until CDC actually republishes its list.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

Segment your reassurance by source market. For UK and European FITs, lead with the declaration; it clears the psychological barrier and those governments move faster than Washington. For US-origin guests and corporate accounts, say Uganda is safe but be precise that US federal entry rules have not yet changed — over-promising a "lifted ban" invites a cancellation and a bad review when a traveller is turned back.

Bush operators: keep low-season gorilla-permit packaging (US$600 in April, May and November) aimed at European and intra-African demand for Q3–Q4, not US groups you cannot yet guarantee.

City and MICE: for any US-linked conference enquiry, quote with a documented advisory caveat and a flexible-cancellation clause dated past 12 August.

**THE WATCH**

CDC port-health order page — whether the mid-August renewal keeps or finally drops Uganda from the list. WHO AFRO External Situation Report 12 (~2 August) — does DRC's confirmed count cross 3,600? US State Department and UK FCDO Uganda advisory levels through 11 August.

Uganda earned its clean bill. But the market it most wants back is priced off a number in another country — and that number is still going up.
