## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**Uganda finally got its certificate. Washington was never reading it.**

Uganda has spent four months proving it can stop Ebola. Twenty confirmed cases. Two deaths. Every transmission chain reconstructed, every contact closed. This week the World Health Organization made it official, certifying that Uganda had cleared the 42-day monitoring period with no new transmission (Al Jazeera, 26 August 2026).

The reward, so far, is nothing. Uganda remains at Level 4 — Do Not Travel — on the US State Department's advisory board. We read the page this afternoon: date issued 4 June 2026, unchanged for 84 days.

Owners in Kampala, Bwindi and Murchison have been treating that advisory as a countdown. It is not one. It is a fixed planning assumption, and the sooner it is written into the 2027 season as such, the better the season will be.

**THE SITUATION**

Uganda's outbreak ended cleanly. The last confirmed case was recorded on 21 June; the last patient was discharged from Mulago on 16 July. Kampala declared the outbreak over on 28 July, counting from its own earlier discharge date — which is why the national and WHO declarations arrived a month apart, and why the gap was procedural rather than epidemiological.

Uganda's Permanent Secretary for Health, Diana Atwine, put the ask plainly: "Unless they have other reasons, they should definitely lift the restrictions off the country."

They do have other reasons, and they wrote them down.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

Read the State Department's own advisory history rather than the advisory itself. Uganda was raised from Level 3 to Level 4 on 17 May 2026 "due to the Ebola Bundibugyo Virus Disease outbreak in Ituri Province in the DRC." Not in Uganda. In Ituri.

That single clause changes the entire commercial calculation. The condition Uganda must satisfy to be downgraded is not a condition Uganda controls. Ituri is now the epicentre of the largest Ebola outbreak ever recorded — 5,656 confirmed cases and 2,715 deaths as at 25 August, more than 4,700 of them in Ituri, per DRC government data cited by the ECDC (Al Jazeera, 26 August 2026). Cases are still rising. Uganda shares 877km of open border with it.

There is a harder irony underneath. Uganda's response to the all-clear has been to build outward: taking delivery of the MBP134 antibody treatment at Kajjansi on 26 August, commissioning a Shs 1.87bn mobile laboratory to move testing closer to outbreak zones (Daily Monitor, 27 August 2026), and opening two further Ebola treatment centres inside the DRC to bring its total there to four, alongside laboratory support at Aru and Kasenyi in Ituri.

Every one of those decisions is correct public health. It is also why Uganda's outbreak stopped at 20 cases while its neighbour's passed five thousand. But each one deepens the documentary association between the word "Uganda" and the word "Ebola" in the files that risk desks and underwriters read. Competence and contamination look identical from a desk in Washington.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

*Contract 2027 as if the Level 4 holds.* Not as a forecast — as a base case. If it lifts, you have upside. If you plan for a lift that does not come, you will be repricing in January with no inventory left to protect.

*Reweight the source mix now.* The UK's guidance treats western Uganda as a regional carve-out rather than a country-wide prohibition (advisory board, verified 12 August 2026). The Gulf, the EU and EAC regional trade do not read State Department levels at all. US-dependent inventory is the exposed inventory; move the allocation, not the rate.

*Ask your US agents the insurance question this week.* Not whether their clients feel safe — whether their trip-cancellation and medical policies treat a Level 4 destination differently. That answer, not the sentiment, is what actually kills a booking.

*Sell the arithmetic, not the adjective.* Twenty cases against 5,656. Two deaths against 2,715. Bwindi and Murchison never closed. An agent can repeat a number to a nervous client; they cannot repeat "we are safe."

**THE WATCH**

The next US advisory re-issue for Uganda, and whether the "health" indicator survives it. WHO AFRO and ECDC weekly bulletins for the first sustained week-on-week decline in Ituri — that, not anything in Kampala, is the leading indicator for a downgrade. And Uganda's own arrivals prints from UBOS through Q4, which will show whether the certificate moved anything at all.

Uganda did not fail an epidemiological test. It failed a geography test set by other people, and it cannot resit it alone.
