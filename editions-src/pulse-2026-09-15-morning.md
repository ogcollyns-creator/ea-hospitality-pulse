## LINKEDIN

**Three chokepoints, one invoice**

Kenyan operators read yesterday's news as "fuel prices unchanged". The more useful reading is what happened to the routes.

Saudi Arabia has shut its 1,200km East-West pipeline after drone attacks near Riyadh and Medina. That line exists to bypass the Strait of Hormuz, and it is the reason Saudi Aramco displaced the UAE as Kenya's largest fuel supplier this year — KSh 99.78bn of imports in the quarter to May against KSh 42.10bn (KNBS, via Business Daily, 14 September 2026). Hormuz is already running at roughly 6–9m barrels a day against more than 20m pre-war. And this week the Houthis took Yemen's Red Sea coast, Perim Island and the Hanish Islands, tightening control of Bab al-Mandeb — through which about 12% of world goods normally passes.

These are being filed as three Middle East stories. For a hotel in Nairobi or a lodge in the Mara they are one story with one output: the landed cost of everything arriving by sea, fuel included.

EPRA's frozen cycle and Kenya's reduced 8% fuel VAT both end on 14 October. That is the horizon on which your Q4 pricing is actually fixed.
