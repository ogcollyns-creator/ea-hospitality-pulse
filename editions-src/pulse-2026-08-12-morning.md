**1️⃣ 🇺🇸→🌍 THE US EBOLA ENTRY ORDER EXPIRES TODAY — RENEW OR LAPSE BY 4:59PM EDT**

The CDC's 30-day Title 42 order covering travellers recently in the DRC, Uganda and South Sudan expires at 4:59pm EDT today (Federal Register 2026-14365, order signed 13 July). As of this morning no renewal has been published — we haven't seen the decision reported anywhere yet. The pattern says it renews (it did on 21 June and 13 July), but the question that matters for us is whether Uganda stays coupled to the DRC or gets decoupled ahead of its 42-day Ebola all-clear (~28 Aug).

Where it stands now (CDC returning-travellers page, 7 Aug): the DRC is a hard block — nobody, US citizens included, boards a US-bound flight within 21 days of leaving. Uganda and South Sudan are lighter: US citizens and nationals aren't restricted, but permitted travellers are rerouted to four US airports (Dulles, Atlanta, Houston, JFK) for screening. Uganda remains US **Level 4**.

## LINKEDIN

The US CDC's 30-day Ebola entry order expires today at 4:59pm EDT. For East African operators, the detail that matters isn't whether it renews — the pattern says it will — but whether Uganda is finally decoupled from the DRC.

As it stands, the two are treated very differently. Anyone recently in the DRC, US citizens included, cannot board a US-bound flight for 21 days. Uganda and South Sudan are lighter: US citizens and nationals aren't restricted at all, and permitted travellers are simply rerouted to four US airports for screening. Uganda, though, remains at US Level 4 — a blunt signal that lags the epidemiology, with its 42-day Ebola all-clear due around 28 August.

The operator takeaway: if you sell to US guests on Uganda or Rwanda gorilla circuits, today's decision sets your pre-arrival messaging for the next month. Any itinerary that so much as transits eastern DRC still triggers the 21-day block. Brief agents on the distinction now, rather than fielding it as a cancellation later.
