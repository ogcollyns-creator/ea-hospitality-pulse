**THE THEME: YOUR NEW GROWTH ENGINE HAS A HEALTH PROBLEM**

Read the daily briefs back and the week looks like good news. Uganda counting down to an Ebola all-clear. Uganda Airlines committing to eight Boeing jets. Emirates going triple-daily into Nairobi. Kampala winning FHS Africa 2027. Mombasa clearing 20,000 visitors in seven days.

Now put one more number beside them. Rwanda's largest single source market in the first half of 2026 was not Europe, not the United States, not Kenya — it was the Democratic Republic of Congo, with 185,420 visitors, 12.5% of all arrivals (reported 18 July). Add Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania and roughly 42% of Rwanda's arrivals now come from four neighbours. That is the shape of this cycle's growth right across the region: regional, repeat, cheap to acquire, weakly branded.

And on 24 July the ECDC's weekly threats report put DRC's Bundibugyo outbreak at 2,473 confirmed cases and 999 deaths, spread across 47 of 140 health zones in five provinces. The region has been watching Uganda's 42-day clock. The clock that matters is the one nobody is timing — in the country that is now Rwanda's biggest customer. European leisure demand takes months to leave and months to return. Regional demand can be switched off in an afternoon by a border notice, and Rwanda has already done exactly that with its 30-day DRC travel-history rule. Cheap demand is fragile demand. Price it that way.

2️⃣ **UGANDA AIRLINES BUYS FOR A DECADE IT HASN'T UNDERWRITTEN**
At Farnborough on 21 July, Uganda Airlines ordered four Boeing 737-8 MAX and four 787-9 Dreamliners — its first direct purchase from Boeing (New Vision, 22 July). The 787-9s are for Europe, Asia and the Middle East; the MAXs for Africa, the Middle East and India. The carrier currently serves 17 destinations in 13 countries from Entebbe.
🎯 **So what:** This is a long-haul bet placed by a country sitting at US Level 4 with Q1 Entebbe arrivals down 7.9%. Seats, not brand, will set 2027–28 Kampala rates. If you own Kampala rooms, model a scenario where lift arrives twelve months before the advisory lifts: high volume, low rate, heavy regional and Indian mix. Start building corporate and MICE contracts now while you still have pricing power.

🎯 **So what:** Coast demand is not the problem this quarter; conversion is. Hold rate through August rather than discounting into a full house, and shift any spend into September–November, where the coast is thinner than the bush.

## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**East Africa's fastest-growing source market is a country with 999 Ebola deaths. Price your 2027 forecast accordingly.**

If you own a lodge in Rwanda, a city hotel in Kampala or a camp on the northern circuit, here is a question worth an hour of your Monday: how much of next year's forecast depends on guests who drove or flew in from a neighbouring country? Most owners cannot answer it, because regional demand arrives quietly, books late, pays in cash or local card, and never shows up in the tour-operator conversation. It is now the growth engine of this cycle. It is also the most fragile revenue line on your P&L, and this week showed exactly why.

**THE SITUATION**

Rwanda's largest single tourism source market in the first half of 2026 was the Democratic Republic of Congo: 185,420 visitors, 12.5% of all international arrivals, reported on 18 July. Uganda followed with 162,890, Kenya with 148,320, Tanzania with 133,650. Four neighbours, roughly 42% of arrivals. That mix is why Rwanda's numbers keep growing while European long-haul demand across the region flattens — Zanzibar's June arrivals grew just 3.1% year-on-year, against 16.2% growth a year earlier.

Then read the health file. The ECDC's Communicable Disease Threats Report for week 30, published 24 July, and its outbreak page updated 22 July, cite DRC's national institute of public health situation report of 21 July: 2,473 confirmed Bundibugyo cases and 999 related deaths, across 47 of 140 health zones in five provinces, with 50 new confirmed cases and 32 deaths recorded in a single day. Ituri alone carries 2,202 of those cases. Uganda's own outbreak — 20 cases, two deaths, 15 of them imported from DRC — has had no new case since 21 June, and the country began its 42-day countdown on 16 July.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

The regional conversation has been about Uganda's countdown. It has been the wrong clock. Uganda's declaration, expected on or about 27 August, closes a small outbreak that was mostly imported. It does not touch the source. The advisory desks that set the US Level 4 and the UK's regional warnings are not modelling Uganda's 42 days; they are modelling a Congolese epidemic that is still adding fifty confirmed cases a day.

The commercial consequence runs deeper than advisories. Regional demand and long-haul demand fail in completely different ways. European leisure demand degrades slowly — bookings soften over a season, and it takes eighteen months to rebuild. Regional demand fails instantly and administratively: one border notice, one entry rule, and 12.5% of your arrivals stop at the frontier. Rwanda has already imposed a 30-day DRC travel-history restriction. It is, in effect, suppressing its own largest source market for public health reasons — and it is right to. But that means the growth number and the risk number are the same number.

Meanwhile capital is being committed against the optimistic reading. Uganda Airlines ordered four 737-8 MAX and four 787-9 Dreamliners at Farnborough on 21 July, its first direct Boeing purchase, aimed at long-haul Europe and Asia and at regional Africa. That is a fleet plan for a Uganda whose advisory has lifted and whose Entebbe arrivals have stopped falling. Neither has happened yet.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

**Split the line.** Report regional and long-haul demand as two separate revenue lines with separate risk weightings, in every budget and every board pack. If you cannot separate them today, your reporting is not fit for this cycle.

**City hotels (Kampala, Kigali, Nairobi):** assume seats arrive before advisories lift. Sign corporate and MICE contracts through Q1 2027 now, while capacity is still tight enough to give you pricing power. When Uganda Airlines' order and Emirates' third daily Nairobi rotation both land, rate discipline will be someone else's problem to hold.

**Bush and gorilla operators:** build the evidence pack before you need it — current WHO and ministry status, distance from affected health zones in kilometres, screening protocol, and your cancellation terms. Have it drafted by 20 August, not written on the day Uganda is declared clear.

**Beach and coast:** you are the hedge. Kenya's coast cleared 20,000 arrivals through Mombasa in a week to 24 July. Hold August rate rather than discounting into a full house, and move discretionary spend into the September–November trough.

**All segments:** stress-test one scenario — a neighbouring border tightens for 60 days in Q1 2027. If that removes more than 8% of your forecast room nights, you have a concentration problem, not a growth story.

**THE WATCH**

**~27 August** — Uganda's 42-day countdown ends; earliest possible end-of-outbreak declaration. **Every Friday** — the ECDC threats report, the cleanest public read on DRC's trajectory; next on 31 July. **~13 August** — Zanzibar's July arrivals from the Commission for Tourism; a second consecutive print under 10% growth confirms the long-haul flattening.

Cheap demand is fragile demand. This region has spent two years congratulating itself on regional growth without ever asking what it would take to lose it. This week supplied the answer.
