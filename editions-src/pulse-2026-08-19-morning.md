The regional guest is the base load now, not the overflow.

**What most operators miss:** the guest base changed but the playbook didn't. Regional demand shrugs off advisories but is exquisitely sensitive to a $20 levy.

## LINKEDIN

The most important number in East African hospitality this year isn't a long-haul figure. It's 234,556 — the Ugandans who visited Kenya in 2025, 31% of all African arrivals and up 8.7%. Tanzania added another 209,536, up 7.7%. In Rwanda, the DRC is now the single largest source market: 185,420 visitors in the first half of 2026, 12.5% of all international arrivals.

The regional guest has become the base load, not the overflow. Yet most properties still run a long-haul-dollar operation on top of it — USD rack rates, GDS and OTA distribution, advisory-driven risk comms aimed squarely at Europe and North America. The marginal booking now comes from a traveller who ignores Western Level-3 advisories, books short, drives or short-hauls in, and pays in a regional currency.

Here's the second-order read few are pricing in: the same treasuries celebrating this demand are taxing its most price-sensitive flyer. Rising passenger levies, short-term-rental charges and proposed insurance mandates land hardest on the regional traveller — the one who is immune to a security headline but exquisitely sensitive to a $20 fee. Distribution, currency and total trip cost — not another advisory line — are where this base load is won or lost.
