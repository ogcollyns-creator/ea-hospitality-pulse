1️⃣ Uganda's gorilla-tourism pledge evaluation says only 33% of operators brief tourists, and lodges need NEMA impact certificates before December

An IGCP-commissioned evaluation of the Gorilla Friendly Pledge was validated in Kampala on 28 October. Of 250 respondents (Dec 2025–Feb 2026), 95.2% had taken the pledge and 73.6% had training, but only 33.2% had briefed tourists. It cites weak enforcement, unregulated tipping and reflective glass near lodges, urges UWA to run a carrying-capacity assessment, and tells lodges to hold NEMA environmental and social impact certificates before December 2026. UWA's Charles Tumwesigye said certification should follow. No permit or tariff change was announced *(The Independent Uganda, 29 Oct)*.
Our inference: certification plus a capacity study is the likely route to tighter rules; the NEMA deadline is the only dated item.
🎯 So what: check your NEMA certificate status this week and put the pledge in the pre-arrival brief.

## LINKEDIN

Uganda's gorilla-tourism pledge has a follow-through problem. An evaluation commissioned by IGCP and validated in Kampala on 28 October found that 95.2% of 250 respondents had taken the Gorilla Friendly Pledge, but only 33.2% had told tourists about it.

The report also lists weak enforcement of visitation rules, unregulated tipping, attempts to herd gorillas, and reflective glass near lodges. It asks UWA for a science-based carrying-capacity assessment and a review of revenue sharing, and tells hotels and lodges to hold NEMA environmental and social impact certificates before December 2026. UWA says certification should follow. No permit or tariff change has been announced.

Our inference: certification plus a capacity study is the likely route to tighter rules on the gorilla circuit. Only the NEMA date is firm.

For a lodge owner the cheap move is operational: check certificate status now and put the pledge in the pre-arrival brief, so the 33% becomes 100% before anyone audits it.
