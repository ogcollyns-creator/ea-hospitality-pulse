## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**An Ebola patient travelled through Rwanda and Uganda before falling ill. That sentence is about to be misread across East Africa — and the misreading will cost more than the virus.**

On Friday, two things happened that belong in the same paragraph and have not yet been put there.

**THE SITUATION**

In Washington, the CDC signed a further 30-day order continuing the suspension of entry for people recently present in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Uganda and South Sudan. Uganda has had no confirmed Ebola case since 21 June and completed its 42-day monitoring period on 27 August. It stayed on the list anyway, on a determination that there is "a material risk that the outbreak will spread to South Sudan and again to Uganda".

In Congo, the acting governor of South-Ubangi confirmed Ebola in a seventh province — the first in the west of the country. According to his statement, reported by AFP, AP and Reuters, the patient had travelled through Rwanda, Uganda and Ituri province, developed symptoms in Kisangani, then continued by boat down the Congo River before dying. Thirty-eight contacts are in quarantine. Local transmission has not been confirmed, so South-Ubangi has not been added to the official list of affected provinces.

So the abstract risk named in an American federal document on Friday had, on the same day, a concrete itinerary attached to it. Rwanda. Uganda. Ituri.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

That itinerary will be read across the region as an exposure event. It is not one, and the reason sits in the American order itself: infected people "do not spread the virus until symptoms begin."

Symptom onset was in Kisangani — after the Rwandan and Ugandan legs. On the published evidence, nobody in Kigali or Entebbe was exposed by this patient. The journey happened during incubation, which is precisely why border screening did not catch it and precisely why it did not seed anything behind him.

Hold both halves of that, because they point in opposite directions. Epidemiologically, this case is close to a non-event for East African properties. Regulatorily, it is the strongest evidence yet for the American position — it demonstrates, in one traceable journey, that a person can carry Bundibugyo across three borders without being detectable. Every future renewal of that order now has a worked example to cite.

Which means the damage to your business this quarter arrives through paperwork, not pathology. Operators will spend the week on sanitiser and screening theatre for a virus that is not coming, and leave an American agent unable to explain to a client whether her green-card-holding mother can fly home after a gorilla trek. One of those costs nothing. The other quietly sells Botswana.

There is a second-order point about competence. When a story like this breaks, guests and agents do not test whether you are safe. They test whether you know. A property that can explain the incubation sequence in two sentences reads as one that has thought about the risk; a property that sends a warm, general reassurance reads as one that is hoping. In a market where the shortlist is already made on trust, that distinction is worth more than the rate.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

Write the two sentences. The patient's symptoms began in Kisangani, after the Rwandan and Ugandan legs; Ebola is not transmissible before symptoms; therefore no exposure occurred in either country. Put it in front of reservations before Tuesday, dated and sourced.

Check transit history at the point of booking for anything touching Rwanda. Rwanda still denies entry outright to any foreign national who has been in or transited the DRC within the previous 30 days — a hard bar, not a delay. A guest routing through Goma or Bukavu before Kigali is refused, not rescheduled.

Separate your American guests by status, not by nationality. The entry order covers non-citizens including lawful permanent residents; US citizens are exempt. A single "North America" line in your pre-arrival comms is wrong for half the people reading it.

And re-read your own cancellation triggers. If they fire on "an outbreak in the country of travel", they will never fire — because the outbreak is not in your country and never was. The thing that moves your bookings is a designation, and designations do not expire on a 42-day countdown.

**THE WATCH**

Whether WHO adds South-Ubangi to the official affected-province list, which requires confirmed local transmission and has not happened. The Federal Register publication of the 11 September order and the comment period that opens with it. And roughly 11 October, when the order lapses or renews again.

The virus is unlikely to reach your property. The paperwork already has.
