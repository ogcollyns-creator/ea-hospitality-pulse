## LINKEDIN BIG READ

Uganda will be declared clear on 28 August. Your American guests still will not be.

Around 28 August, Uganda completes the 42-day count that formally closes its Bundibugyo chapter. The press release writes itself, and a wave of "Uganda is open" messaging will follow it. Almost none of it will be true in the only sense that matters to a booking.

THE SITUATION

Today the Democratic Republic of the Congo's outbreak became the deadliest in that country's history: 2,325 deaths from 4,945 confirmed cases, passing the 2018–20 epidemic, with 101 new cases in twenty-four hours (CNN, 16 August; Al Jazeera and Bloomberg, 17 August). The case fatality ratio has roughly doubled since early June, from about 20% to 46%. The outbreak spans 55 health zones across six provinces, up from the 53 zones the United States cited in its 12 August entry order.

Uganda's own epidemiology looks nothing like this. Twenty confirmed cases, two deaths, all confined to Kampala and Wakiso, outbreak declared over on 28 July. The WHO IHR Emergency Committee meets tomorrow.

WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING

The industry keeps reading these as one story. They are two, and only one of them governs entry.

Read the American order and the reason Uganda is on it is stated plainly: not Ugandan case counts, but continued overland travel from DRC. The order's own release condition is DRC-side — it cites contact tracing running near 78% against a 95% operational threshold. Today's numbers moved that indicator in the wrong direction. A wider outbreak across more health zones does not make 95% more reachable by 11 September; it makes it less.

So the 28 August all-clear is an epidemiological milestone with no regulatory consequence. Uganda will be clean and still covered. Canada's rule is harsher still and expires on its own schedule.

The commercial risk is not the outbreak. It is the messaging gap that opens on 28 August, when your agents read "Uganda declared Ebola-free", quote it to a client, and the client is then rerouted through Dulles or told to quarantine on arrival home. That is a refund conversation, and it is entirely avoidable.

WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT

Write the 28 August language now, before the news breaks. Two sentences: Uganda's outbreak is over, and separately, the United States and Canada maintain entry measures that have not changed. Send it to your agents in advance, not in response.

City hotels in Kampala and Entebbe: your corporate accounts are the ones with US-bound travellers. Brief them directly rather than waiting for the question.

Bush operators on the gorilla circuit: this is the quarter your Rwandan competitor's US$1,500 permit stops looking expensive if your guest faces a rerouting. Sell the certainty, and be precise about which nationalities are actually affected.

Kenya, Tanzania, Zanzibar and Rwanda: none of you is restricted. Nairobi appears in the American order only as a named transit corridor. Do not import a Ugandan problem into your own pre-arrival comms — over-explaining a rule that does not apply to you creates the doubt you were trying to remove.

THE WATCH

Tomorrow, 18 August: the IHR Emergency Committee sits. Around 1 September: the American comment window on docket CDC-2026-0892 closes, having attracted almost no submissions from this industry in any previous round. 11 September: the decision point.

The outbreak is a tragedy several hundred kilometres away. The thing that will actually cost you money is a fortnight of confident, well-meant, incorrect messaging by people who sell your rooms.
