## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**Your Ebola risk is not epidemiological. It is cartographic.**

Uganda has not recorded a confirmed Ebola case since 21 June. WHO declared the outbreak over on 25 August, after the full 42-day countdown, with 20 cases and a 10% case fatality ratio. That is 86 days clean. Uganda is still named on foreign entry restrictions written in May.

Meanwhile the epidemic that actually sets those restrictions is next door and getting worse. On data to 12 September, DRC stood at 7,200 confirmed cases and 3,475 deaths, with 923 patients hospitalised in isolation — an increase of 258 cases and 126 deaths in the two days since the previous report (DRC Institut National de Santé Publique, SitRep N°122/MVEBDB/13/09/2026, carried by ECDC, page updated 14 September 2026). Sixty-two of 167 health zones are affected, across seven provinces. WHO's Disease Outbreak News of 10 September, working from 7 September data, had 61 health zones across six.

**THE SITUATION**

The geography matters more than the totals. Ituri remains the epicentre with 5,406 cumulative confirmed cases. But North Kivu is the province to watch: 1,066 cumulative cases, 453 of them in the preceding 21 days, and the outbreak's highest case fatality ratio at 65.4%, high enough that WHO says investigations into the cause are ongoing (DON617, 10 September 2026). North Kivu is the province that borders Uganda and Rwanda.

WHO's own advice, restated in that same document, is against any restriction of travel to or trade with affected countries. Almost nobody is following it.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

Operators are tracking the wrong variable. Every general manager in Kampala and Kigali can tell you their country's case count. Almost none can tell you the drafting convention of the restrictions that price their rooms — and that is the variable that binds.

Every measure written in this outbreak was written at country resolution. Rwanda denies entry to any foreign national who has travelled to or transited through the DRC in the preceding 30 days (Ministry of Health, Kigali, 22 May 2026). Bahrain, Jordan, Kuwait, Lebanon, Mauritius, Taiwan, Canada and the UAE all drew their lines around DRC, Uganda and South Sudan as whole countries (Fragomen, 9 June 2026). Not one of them distinguishes Ituri from Kinshasa, which are roughly 1,600km apart — further than London to Warsaw.

At that resolution, an epidemic concentrated in the far north-east of a country the size of Western Europe prices risk into room nights in Kigali and Entebbe. And it cuts the other way too: Rwanda's notice has not been publicly revised since 22 May. It was not tightened as DRC grew from hundreds of cases to 7,200, and it was not loosened when Uganda cleared. The restriction map is frozen at a May picture of a September epidemic.

That is the second-order read. Restrictions go on at the speed of news and come off at the speed of bureaucracy. The demand hit lands in days; the recovery takes quarters. Any operator modelling a recovery off the epidemic curve will be out by a quarter, possibly two.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

City properties in Nairobi, Kigali and Kampala: your corporate and MICE exposure is to the transit clause, not the virus. Rwanda's 30-day rule catches a delegate who spent one day in Kinshasa. Collect DRC-transit history at contracting, not at check-in, and put it in the group agreement.

Bush properties on the Uganda and Rwanda gorilla circuit: Uganda is clean and still listed. Put the 25 August declaration and the 42-day countdown in the first paragraph of every agent communication, with the source link. Trade partners are still quoting numbers they read in May.

Beach and coast: the FCDO's Tanzania advice carries heightened health screening for arrivals from Uganda and DRC (gov.uk, updated 31 August 2026). That is a connection-time problem, not a health one. Rebuild transfer buffers for Zanzibar arrivals routing through Entebbe before someone misses a boat.

And for everyone: borrow Rwanda's drafting. The same 22 May notice that closed the airport door states plainly that "all activities within Rwanda continue as usual, including conferences, events, tourism." A government can restrict entry and defend its meetings business in one document. Most cannot be bothered to try.

**THE WATCH**

WHO's next biweekly Bundibugyo Disease Outbreak News, due on or about 24 September — whether the affected-province count prints at seven. Rwanda's Ministry of Health EVD updates page, where the most recent entry is dated 16 June. And any ministry, anywhere, that swaps a country name for a province name.

Stop counting cases. Start reading maps.
