## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**The Kenya–Tanzania safari now crosses two paid borders, and nobody is pricing the crossing**

If you sell a Nairobi–Kilimanjaro or Mara–Serengeti package for 2027, your guest's bill changed this week, and your quote probably did not.

**THE SITUATION**

On 7 October, Justice Olel at the High Court in Marsabit dismissed the petition against Kenya's US$44 visitor health insurance, holding Gazette Notice 11492 valid. The UK Foreign Office now tells eTA applicants to buy cover from 5 October. Tanzania's mainland has required its own policy since 1 October. For a non-exempt guest on a Kenya–Tanzania trip, that is two charges: US$88 a person, US$176 for a couple. That is our arithmetic. Some regional citizens are exempt, and it is still unclear whether the exemption covers regional residents.

The airline map moved in the same days. Jambojet began daily Nairobi–Entebbe flights on 1 October and stopped Mombasa–Zanzibar, citing fuel and the Tanzanian insurance. Its dates do not fit: AeroRoutes logged the last Zanzibar flight in April, before the insurance existed. On 3 October a Kenyan man who had lived in Congo for seven years flew the Entebbe route and landed in Nairobi. He died of Bundibugyo Ebola in Nairobi within days. Reuters reports he passed temperature checks at Entebbe and Nairobi and at least two digital health forms. Kenya now has a dedicated gate at Nairobi for arrivals from Congo and linked countries. Tanzania, meanwhile, lifted its extra screening for Uganda arrivals, according to UK Foreign Office advice reported on 1 October. The WHO's Tedros advised against closing borders.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

Each item has been filed as its own story: an insurance rule, an airline decision, a screening failure. They are one story. Governments are adding cost and friction where one country ends and the next begins, and they are not doing it together. The lodge sells a destination. The guest buys a journey with a border in the middle.

That matters because of how guests decide. Skift and McKinsey found in September that 78% of US travellers keep researching after they book. On a two-country trip, the decision to keep it is set by the most awkward border on the route. A rule change in a country where you have no property can cancel the stay in the one where you do. Your cancellation terms cover your own leg. The risk covers all of them. We cannot size this: no one publishes booking pace for twin-centre trips, and we will not guess. But the direction is clear, and the seam where an airline just cut costs is the seam the health authorities are now watching.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

For bush lodges selling circuits: quote each border as a separate line, and state who buys the insurance. Offer a defined "border event" clause: if a government changes an entry rule or advisory level before arrival, the guest gets a free date change or credit, not a refund and not a rate cut.

For beach properties in Zanzibar and the coast: assume no low-cost Mombasa–Zanzibar link, and price the connection through Nairobi or Dar. Say plainly which package includes the Tanzanian cover.

For city hotels in Nairobi, Kampala and Kigali: the Kenyan Public Health PS suspended government meetings in hotels from 7 October. Get any government block's status in writing and check your force-majeure wording before the next block date.

**THE WATCH**

The US entry order on DRC, Uganda and South Sudan lapses on 11 October, with no renewal listed. Kenya's fuel bill closes comments on 13 October and EPRA reviews prices on 14 October. Tanzania publishes October inflation on 9 November, with transport already at 14.0%.

The question for your 2027 rate card is not what the destination costs. It is who pays when the border changes.
