Zanzibar was promised a port today. Check the arithmetic before you budget on it.
━━━━━━━━━
1️⃣ ZANZIBAR PORT PLEDGE ARRIVES WITHOUT A DATE
TRA Commissioner General Yusuph Mwenda toured Fumba Port in Unguja on Saturday 15 August and pledged a modern cargo scanner to speed clearance (The Citizen, 17 August). In the same report, Zanzibar Ports Corporation director general Akif Ali Khamis said the completed port would handle "approximately 40 containers per hour, equivalent to between 250,000 and 400,000 containers per month", with 350-metre quays on each side by February 2027.

Those two figures do not reconcile. Forty an hour run continuously is about 28,800 a month. The monthly claim implies roughly 350 an hour — eight to fourteen times the stated rate.
🎯 So what: The operator gain is real and separate from the throughput claim. Khamis says Zanzibar cargo has been routed through neighbouring ports first; removing that leg is the saving worth pricing. Do not model 2027 landed costs on either container figure until TRA publishes a contract award and ZPC restates throughput.

2️⃣ TANZANIA SWITCHES ON ITS BIGGEST POWER ASSET
President Samia Suluhu Hassan inaugurates the 2,115 MW Julius Nyerere Hydropower Project at Rufiji on 22 August (The Citizen, 16 August). JNHPP cost TSh 7.45 trillion, was funded domestically, and supplied 44.9% of all electricity fed to the national grid in the year to 31 May 2026. National installed capacity now stands at about 4,646 MW; The Citizen reports a 2,636 MW surplus.
🎯 So what: Mainland lodges and Dar city hotels should revisit generator run-hour assumptions and any diesel contract priced off the rationing era. Zanzibar operators should not assume the same relief — the island's binding constraint is interconnector capacity, not mainland generation. Ask your supplier which one actually binds you before you cut the genset line.

3️⃣ ZANZIBAR BUYS CYBER SKILLS, NOT YET GUEST-FACING ONES
Zanzibar's Ministry of Communications, IT and Innovation signed a three-year MoU with the Emerson Education Institute on Sunday 16 August to train an initial 600 public servants in AI and cybersecurity, in classes of 50 (The Citizen, 17 August).
🎯 So what: No guest-facing effect yet, and we won't pretend otherwise. It matters only if it reaches the licensing, permit and tourism-levy systems you actually queue in. Watch, don't plan.

## LINKEDIN

A port authority quoted two throughput figures on Saturday. They are eight to fourteen times apart, and one of them will end up in somebody's 2027 budget.

Tanzania Revenue Authority Commissioner General Yusuph Mwenda toured Fumba Port in Unguja on 15 August and pledged a modern cargo scanner to speed clearance. In the same report, Zanzibar Ports Corporation director general Akif Ali Khamis said the completed port would handle "approximately 40 containers per hour, equivalent to between 250,000 and 400,000 containers per month" (The Citizen, 17 August).

Forty containers an hour, run continuously, is about 28,800 a month. The monthly figure implies roughly 350 an hour.

Both cannot be right. And that matters, because the real prize here is not throughput at all. Khamis noted that Zanzibar-bound cargo has been routed through neighbouring ports first, then shipped on. Removing a transhipment leg is a genuine, quantifiable saving on F&B, fuel and refurbishment inputs for island resorts — the kind of saving you can actually put in a model.

It just isn't the number being quoted.

Price the removed leg, not the headline capacity. And wait for a contract award before either figure goes anywhere near a budget.
