## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**The week that sets East Africa's risk picture starts with an empty page**

Four days out from the 12 August decision that shapes the region's travel-risk story, the most useful thing an operator can look at is a page with nothing new on it.

We checked the US CDC's port-health order page tonight. The latest entry order suspending arrivals of foreign nationals recently in the DRC, Uganda or South Sudan is still the one signed on 13 July — in force for 30 days, so it lapses on or about 12 August. No renewed order has been posted. That emptiness is not a lull; it is the pattern. Each of the previous renewals — May, June, July — landed at or near the expiry, not before it. The base case for next week is therefore a renewal, not a lapse.

The reason sits across the border in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and it hardened this week. The Bundibugyo outbreak has now passed 4,000 confirmed cases for the first time: 4,053 confirmed and 1,850 deaths in the DRC's 6 August situation update, using data to 5 August. That is up from 3,605 confirmed and 1,587 deaths at the World Health Organization's 30 July cut-off — a case-fatality ratio near 44%, and no sign of a plateau. Nothing in that trajectory gives a US decision-maker a reason to lift a regional entry restriction on 12 August.

For property owners, the operational read is about what does *not* change. Uganda's US travel advisory remains at Level 4, Do Not Travel, on Ebola grounds — even though Uganda's own Ministry of Health declared its outbreak over on 28 July, with the last confirmed case back on 21 June. The gap between Uganda's clean epidemiological picture and its unchanged advisory level is the single most important thing a Ugandan lodge or a Kampala hotel needs to price into the autumn. The advisory is anchored to the regional order, not to Uganda's own numbers, and it will not move on the country's timeline alone.

So the honest planning guidance is this. Do not build a September recovery in the US market on the assumption of a downgrade next week. The realistic earliest window for Uganda relief tracks the 42-day all-clear that completes around 27 August, and even then a US advisory revision typically lags the epidemiology by weeks. The next hard date beyond 12 August is 18 August, when the WHO's International Health Regulations Emergency Committee meets; the reasonable expectation is that it keeps the Public Health Emergency of International Concern in force while DRC case counts are still climbing.

The rest of the board is quieter, and quiet is worth stating plainly. Kenya holds at US Level 2, Tanzania and Zanzibar at Level 3, Rwanda at Level 3 — unchanged since our last verification on 4 August. For the Kenyan, Tanzanian, Zanzibari and Rwandan tourist cores, that means no fresh advisory drag into the September–October peak. A confirmed "no change" is an argument to hold rate, not to discount against a downgrade that has not happened.

And the cost side has a diarised date of its own. EPRA's next fuel review takes effect on 15 August. Nairobi diesel — the number that drives game drives, transfers, generators and supply runs — is held at KSh222.86 a litre this cycle, but only because the state is propping it with the petroleum levy and an 8% VAT rate now extended to 14 October. Both are discretionary. The planning window closes on 14 August. The move that pays for itself is unglamorous: lock diesel-linked transfer and generator contracts this week, before the review and before the October VAT cliff.

None of tonight's picture is a headline. It is a set of dates and one rising number, read together. But that is usually where the edge is — not in the story everyone runs the morning after, but in knowing, four days early, which way the week is likely to break.
