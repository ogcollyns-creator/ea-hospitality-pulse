## LINKEDIN

Kenya's visitor insurance rule is no longer in limbo, and we got that wrong for three days.

On 7 October, Justice Olel at the High Court in Marsabit dismissed the petition against Gazette Notice 11492, holding it valid under the Social Health Insurance Act. The UK FCDO updated its Kenya advice on 9 October: from 5 October, anyone applying for a Kenyan eTA must buy travel health insurance. The portal sells a US$44 policy, and a VisasNews test checkout came to US$52 with fees.

Our rules board showed the rule as suspended, and we told operators they could quote packages without it. That was right until 7 October and wrong after it.

Three questions remain open: whether foreign-issued policies are accepted, how older ETAs are treated, and whether the petitioners appeal.

The inference is ours: a Kenya–Tanzania itinerary now carries two US$44 charges, because Tanzania mainland has required its own policy since 1 October.

For operators, the practical step is to add a per-guest line to every Kenya quote, have agents prompt guests to buy cover before they fly, and price the cost into 2027 net rates rather than absorbing it.
