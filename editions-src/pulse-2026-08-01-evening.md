## LINKEDIN BIG READ

The virus is in Congo. The cancellation is in your inbox.

If you run a beach resort in Zanzibar, a camp in the Mara or a lodge in Nyungwe, you have zero Ebola cases within a thousand kilometres of your gate. That will not stop the word "Ebola" arriving in your guest's inbox this weekend — and the property that manages the headline, not the virus, keeps its Q4.

**THE SITUATION**

On 31 July, DR Congo's Ministry of Communications put the Bundibugyo outbreak at 3,532 confirmed cases and 1,556 deaths (reported by Al Jazeera, CNN and Xinhua, 31 July). That total passed the 3,481 recorded in the 2018–20 eastern DRC outbreak, making this the second-largest Ebola outbreak in history — behind only West Africa in 2014–16. The 2018–20 outbreak took nearly two years to reach that number. This one did it in roughly eleven weeks. Africa CDC has described the epidemic as "uncontrolled," with contact tracing running at 78.3% against the 90–95% the WHO says containment requires — meaning transmission is slipping past the traced chains.

None of those cases are in the five markets this brief covers. Uganda declared its own imported outbreak over on 28 July. Kenya, Tanzania and Zanzibar have recorded nothing.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

Operators are watching the wrong threat. The virus is a Congolese public-health emergency; it is not, for a Kenyan or Tanzanian property, an epidemiological risk. The risk is narrative. A milestone headline — "second-largest Ebola outbreak ever" — does not distinguish between Ituri province and a Diani beach. It travels through the same source-market news feeds that carry your inbound demand, and it does its damage silently: not through the cancellations you can see, but through the new bookings that quietly stop arriving. By the time an empty Q4 is visible, the headline is three weeks old and the pace is already lost.

This is a reputational-contagion event dressed as a health story. The correct response is not medical reassurance about a disease you never had. It is demand defence.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

Beach and bush: publish a single, factual line on your site and in pre-arrival comms stating the distance in kilometres between your property and the nearest confirmed case, and that your country has zero cases. Precision is your most valuable asset — "1,500km away, zero cases in Tanzania" beats any amount of soothing.

City and MICE: for US-linked corporate and conference enquiries, be precise that the US federal entry ban runs to 12 August and has not lifted. Quote with a documented advisory caveat and flexible cancellation dated past that. Over-promising a "lifted ban" invites a turned-back traveller and a bad review.

All segments: measure pace weekly — new bookings this week against the same week last month and last year, split by source market. That number, not your cancellation log, tells you whether the headline is biting. Hold rate; compete on flexibility and value-add, not discount. A traveller frightened by a headline is not tempted by 20% off — you would hand margin to people who were coming anyway.

**THE WATCH**

WHO AFRO External Situation Report 12 (~2 August) — does DRC's confirmed count cross 3,600? US CDC port-health order — whether the 12 August renewal keeps DRC, Uganda and South Sudan on the list. Your own booking pace by source market, checked every Friday through August.

The camp is clean. The market is nervous. Whoever explains the difference first, in kilometres, keeps the booking.
