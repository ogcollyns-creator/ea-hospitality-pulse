## LINKEDIN

**Six days out: the East African travel-risk decision nobody's pricing in**

On or around 12 August, the US CDC decides whether to renew the Section 362 order that suspends entry of travellers recently in the DR Congo, Uganda or South Sudan. The current order was issued on 13 July and runs for 30 days — so the clock is real, and it's short.

Two things matter for hotel and lodge operators.

First, the epidemiology gives no room to lift. WHO's latest figures put the DRC Bundibugyo outbreak at 3,605 confirmed cases and 1,587 deaths as at 30 July — a 44% fatality rate and the worst single week on record. A renewal, with DRC still listed, is the base case.

Second, Uganda is the genuine open question. Kampala declared its own outbreak over on 28 July. Whether Washington decouples Uganda from its neighbour — or keeps it listed until the 42-day all-clear around 27 August — decides when Ugandan inbound from the US can realistically recover.

The operator takeaway: don't model a September US-market bounce on a decoupling that hasn't happened. Hold rate, brief your source markets, and watch the 12th.
