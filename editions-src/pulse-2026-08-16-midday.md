**1️⃣ 🇺🇬 WASHINGTON ALREADY WRITES UGANDA IN THE PAST TENSE — AND KEEPS IT RESTRICTED ANYWAY**

The full text of the US entry order signed on **12 August** went on public inspection at the Federal Register on **14 August, 08:45** and publishes **17 August** (FR Doc **2026-16706**, docket **CDC-2026-0892**). We read it this morning on public inspection; we haven't seen it reported anywhere yet.

Two things in it matter more than the renewal we covered on 13 August.

**First, the operative finding.** The determination reads that Ebola is _"confirmed currently present in DRC and **recently present in Uganda**."_ Past tense — in a document that keeps Uganda on the restricted list. The order records Uganda's last confirmed case as **21 June**, 20 confirmed cases and two deaths in total, and the Ministry of Health declaration of **28 July**. It then keeps Uganda in on a stated ground that has nothing to do with Uganda's epidemiology: _"continued overland travel from DRC poses an ongoing risk of cross-border transmission, particularly among healthcare workers and in western Ugandan districts."_

**What everyone is missing:** the release condition is a DRC number, not a Ugandan one. The order names contact tracing at **~78% nationwide against a 95% operational threshold**, says the true outbreak size may be _"two to four times greater"_ than reported, and puts DRC at **4,318 confirmed cases and 2,011 deaths across 53 health zones** as of 9 August — against **1,873 and 672 across 41 zones** when the July order was signed. Uganda's 42-day clock completing around **28 August** does not move any of those. Expiry is **4:59pm EDT, Friday 11 September**.

**Second, there is a door, and nobody has walked through it.** Publication on 17 August opens a **15-day comment period** on docket CDC-2026-0892 — closing on or about **1 September**, ahead of the 11 September call. The order records the comments received so far: **three** on the original, **five** in June, and on the July renewal, _none_. The East African trade has never filed.

🎯 **So what:** Stop selling 28 August as the turning point — brief agents that the US rule is priced to run to 11 September, and rebook US arrivals accordingly. If you want Uganda decoupled, the comment docket is the only formal channel into the risk assessment that decides it, it costs nothing, and it closes ~1 September.

**2️⃣ 🌍 TWO OFFICIAL CASE COUNTS FOR THE SAME DAY — 63 APART**

Worth knowing before you quote a number to an agent or an insurer. For **9 August**, the US order states DRC at **4,318 confirmed cases** across **53 health zones**. WHO AFRO's Weekly External Situation Report 13, same reporting date, states **4,381 confirmed cases and 2,011 deaths, a case fatality ratio of 45.9%**, with Ituri accounting for 85.8% of cumulative cases (afro.who.int, posted 11 Aug).

Deaths agree exactly. Cases differ by 63. Neither is wrong — the US figure is a snapshot taken for an American risk assessment; WHO AFRO's is the regional surveillance line. But they will be quoted against each other in the coming fortnight, and being caught with the lower one looks like minimising.

🏷 Regional · Bush/City · Confirmed (both documents, dated)
🎯 **So what:** Standardise on WHO AFRO for any client-facing figure, cite it with the report number and date, and never quote an outbreak number without one.

## LINKEDIN

The full text of the 12 August entry order went on public inspection at the Federal Register on 14 August and publishes tomorrow (FR Doc 2026-16706). Its operative determination reads that Ebola is "confirmed currently present in DRC and recently present in Uganda" — past tense for Uganda, whose last confirmed case was 21 June and whose outbreak the Ministry of Health declared over on 28 July.

The ground for keeping Uganda in is not Ugandan epidemiology. It is, in the order's own words, "continued overland travel from DRC." Which means the release condition sits on the other side of the border: contact tracing at roughly 78% against a 95% operational threshold, and 4,318 confirmed cases across 53 health zones as of 9 August, up from 1,873 across 41 zones a month earlier.
