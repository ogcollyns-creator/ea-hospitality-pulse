**1️⃣ 🇹🇿 ZANZIBAR'S RECORD JULY WIDENED THE GAP IT SHOULD HAVE CLOSED**

**107,801** visitors in July — an all-time monthly record, **+9.6%** on July 2025's 98,370 (OCGS, via The Citizen, 14 Aug). It was also **+54.9%** on June's 69,605, and **2.68× May's 40,151**.

A year earlier that same step was +45.7%. The swing didn't narrow. It widened by nine points. The Maldives, on months published so far in 2026, ran 247,722 in February to 123,552 in June — **2.01×** (Maldives Monetary Authority). Definitions differ, but the direction isn't in doubt: Zanzibar is getting better at selling ten weeks and no better at selling the other forty-two.

**What everyone is missing:** the record was disproportionately *not* European. Europe supplied 60,754, yet its share fell from **61.9%** in June to **56.4%** in July. Back out the residual and non-European arrivals grew **+77.5%** month-on-month, against Europe's +40.9%. The marginal guest is increasingly African, Gulf and Asian — booking shorter, later, outside European tour-operator rails. That is the only demand that doesn't obey a European school calendar, and the only demand that can flatten this curve.

## LINKEDIN

Zanzibar recorded its best month in history in July — 107,801 arrivals. The more useful number is May's: 40,151.

That is a 2.68× swing between trough and peak inside one season (Office of the Chief Government Statistician, released 14 August). A year earlier the July-over-June step was +45.7%; this year, +54.9%. The gap widened. The Maldives, by comparison, ran 247,722 in February against 123,552 in June — a 2.01× swing on months published so far in 2026 (Maldives Monetary Authority).

Records are being read as validation. They are better read as compression. An island getting better at selling ten weeks and no better at selling the other forty-two isn't growing its business — it is concentrating it, with all the working-capital and staffing cost that implies.

What the coverage missed sits in the same release. Europe supplied 60,754 of July's arrivals, but its share fell from 61.9% in June to 56.4%. Non-European arrivals grew 77.5% month-on-month against Europe's 40.9%. The marginal guest is increasingly African, Gulf and Asian — booking shorter, later, outside European tour-operator rails.

That is the demand that doesn't follow a European school calendar, and the only demand that can flatten this curve. Operators who build a shoulder product for it now will own April and May while everyone else closes.
