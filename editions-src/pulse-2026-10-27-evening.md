1️⃣ The US will require a signed no-DRC declaration from every passenger flying to America from 3 November, including from Nairobi, Entebbe and Kigali

CDC's Order, signed 22 Oct and published in the Federal Register on 27 Oct (91 FR 67582), bars airlines from boarding anyone to the US without a signed statement that they were not in the Democratic Republic of the Congo in the previous 21 days. It applies from 11:59pm EST on 3 Nov, covers US connections, and airlines must keep the forms 42 days. The Order names no East African country. The text gives two end dates, 17 and 18 Dec *(Federal Register, 27 Oct; CDC, undated page)*.
Our inference: this is a paperwork cost, not a ban, but it bites gorilla-trekking itineraries that cross into the DRC.
🎯 So what: tell US-bound guests and agents now, and add the form to check-in for departures after 3 Nov.

## LINKEDIN

From 3 November, every passenger flying to the United States will have to sign a statement that they have not been in the Democratic Republic of the Congo in the past 21 days.

CDC signed the Order on 22 October and it was published in the Federal Register today. It applies to all airlines, all departure countries and US connections. Airlines must refuse boarding without the form and keep copies for 42 days. It names no East African country. Nairobi, Entebbe and Kigali departures are covered only because they fly to the US.

Our inference: for most lodges this is a paperwork step, not a ban. It matters where guests combine Rwanda or Uganda gorilla trekking with a DRC leg, because those guests cannot truthfully sign. The Order also gives two end dates, 17 and 18 December, so expect a clarification.

The same week, Uganda barred travellers with DRC history from transiting and brought back locator forms, after a Nigerian Ebola patient was said to have holidayed there. Kenya released its first 20 quarantined contacts, with 98 to follow from 3 November.
