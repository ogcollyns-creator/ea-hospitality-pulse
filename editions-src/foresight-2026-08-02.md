## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**Your biggest 2027 risk isn't the next travel advisory. It's a cost base quietly diverging from your competitor's.**

If you own a lodge, camp or hotel in East Africa, this week handed you a P&L lesson disguised as a health story.

**THE SITUATION**

The headlines were about disease and security. DRC's Bundibugyo outbreak reached 3,605 confirmed cases and 1,587 deaths — a 44% fatality rate across 49 health zones (WHO AFRO Situation Report 11, data to 26 July). The US kept its entry ban on DRC, Uganda and South Sudan, with the next renewal due around 12 August. A re-issued US advisory on Kenya rattled agents.

Yet the genuinely new, durable development sat in a statistics release almost nobody in hospitality read closely. Kenya's inflation rose to 6.5% in July — its highest in nearly two years — driven by electricity, transport up 15.6%, and food up 9.0% (KNBS, 31 July). Meanwhile Uganda was declared Ebola-free on 28 July, and Tanzania's cost base stayed benign.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

The scary stories are the transient ones. Uganda's outbreak is over. Advisories move on classifiers and get corrected. Even the US ban is a blunt regional instrument that says nothing about any single property's safety.

The cost divergence is the story that compounds. Kenya's inflation is not a spike — it's a trajectory toward the Central Bank's 7.5% ceiling, and it is pulling Kenyan operating costs *away* from Tanzanian and Ugandan ones. That matters because East Africa is not sold in isolation. With the rand weak, a comparable Big Five safari currently runs 30–60% cheaper in South Africa than in Kenya or Tanzania for the same lodge tier; Kruger's daily park fee sits near US$33. Every point of Kenyan cost inflation, passed into rate, widens a price gap your guest can already see on a competitor's quote.

So the connection the daily news cycle never draws is this: the demand-side risks that dominated the week are receding, while the supply-side cost risk — the one that actually decides whether your 2027 rate card survives contact with a South African or Mauritian alternative — is the one that firmed up.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

City hotels (Nairobi especially): you face the cost squeeze *and* a supply wave as branded rooms open through 2026. Compete on corporate contracts, F&B and loyalty, not on a rack rate that new supply will undercut. Fix your energy line — the electricity pass-through moves every month and is bigger than the fuel review most owners watch.

Safari camps: stop benchmarking only against the camp next door. Your real competitor is a currency. Sell what South Africa cannot replicate — private access, guiding depth, the crossing itself — and hold rate discipline through the Aug–Oct peak. Discounting into peak season funds your rival's marketing.

Beach resorts (Zanzibar & the coast): watch the Indian Ocean price signal as closely as your own occupancy. When Mauritius discounts into shoulder season, your European volume moves first. Anchor demand to fixed dates — the 25 October Zanzibar Marathon is a 10,000-runner booking window; price it now.

Everyone: build your 2027 budget off the cost-gap number, not last year's. KNBS and UBOS release August CPI in early September — that print is your negotiating evidence.

**THE WATCH**

Two dates decide the near term. Around 12 August the US renews or lifts its Ebola order — given DRC's numbers, assume renewal and brief your agents accordingly. On 15 August EPRA's fuel review resets Kenyan transfer and generator costs. Lock your quotes before then.
