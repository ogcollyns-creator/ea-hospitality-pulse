## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**In 26 days, "eco-lodge" stops being a marketing word and becomes a legal claim**

On 27 September 2026 the European Union's Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition Directive — Directive (EU) 2024/825, EmpCo — begins to apply. Most East African owners have never heard of it. Almost all of them are covered by it.

**THE SITUATION**

EmpCo amends EU consumer-protection law to ban a list of environmental marketing practices. Generic claims go first: "green", "eco-friendly", "sustainable", "responsible" cannot be used unless a recognised certification directly covers the claim. Carbon-neutrality claims resting on offsets bought outside the value chain are prohibited outright, so "carbon-neutral safari" becomes a dead phrase rather than a risky one. Self-created labels are banned — a leaf logo and the words "committed to conservation", designed in-house, is now a prohibited badge. Forward-looking pledges require a published, independently reviewed plan.

The European Commission's 2020 study found 53% of environmental claims vague, misleading or unfounded, and puts the share of green labels with weak or no verification at 50%. That is the problem the directive was written to fix. Geography offers no protection: per Sustainable Travel International's guide, published 5 August 2026, the rules bind any business marketing to consumers in the EU regardless of where it sits.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

Enforcement will not come from Brussels. It will come from your agent in Frankfurt.

A regulator in Düsseldorf is not going to pursue a camp in the Serengeti. The European tour operator selling that camp is a far easier target, is unambiguously in scope, and reuses supplier copy in its own brochure. The Unfair Commercial Practices Directive that EmpCo amends carries maximum fines of at least 4% of annual turnover in the member states concerned. Buyers facing that risk do what buyers always do: push it down the supply chain. Our expectation — inference, not fact — is that the Q4 2026 contracting round brings substantiation questionnaires to East African suppliers for the first time. Answer within days and you keep your 2027 brochure page. Take a quarter and you are quietly not renewed, and never told why.

The second miss matters more. This is not a compliance cost. It is a repricing of an asset East Africa already owns.

Conservation credentials have been commercially worthless here for twenty years, because they were free to claim. A conservancy paying real lease fees to named group ranches, employing real rangers, competed for the same German guest against a lodge whose entire sustainability programme was the word "eco" in its name. The market could not tell them apart, so it never paid for the difference.

From 27 September, only one of them can keep saying it. Scarce claims have value. This is the first regulation in memory that shifts advantage toward operators who actually do the work — and East African bush product does more of it, per bed, than almost anywhere. The catch: fewer than 3.4% of African hotels hold any sustainability certification, per the most recent continent-wide count in a GSTC-published review from 2018. The advantage goes to whoever closes that gap first, not to whoever deserves it most.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

**Bush — largest exposure, largest prize.** Replace adjectives with arithmetic: hectares under conservancy lease, fees paid to named group ranches in 2025, staff recruited within 20km, diesel litres displaced by solar. That copy is compliant, and it sells better than "eco-lodge" ever did. Then apply for certification — Travelife, Green Key, Green Globe, EarthCheck and Green Destinations all meet the third-party bar. Applications take months. September is late.

**Beach — Zanzibar, Diani, the north coast.** Heaviest European package exposure, so the questionnaire reaches you first; package organisers carry the strictest liability under EU consumer law. One trap: do not advertise "plastic-free" as a benefit. Kenya and Zanzibar both have statutory plastic restrictions, and EmpCo prohibits marketing legally required practices as achievements.

**City — smallest exposure, longest fuse.** Nairobi and Kigali properties chasing European corporate accounts will meet the same substantiation language in 2027 RFPs, from procurement teams with no discretion to accept an adjective.

**Everyone.** Stop describing offsets as compensation for your footprint. You may describe them as investment in climate action beyond your operations. That distinction is now legal, not semantic.

**THE WATCH**

27 September 2026 — EmpCo applies. Transposition was due 27 March 2026; check whether Germany, the Netherlands and France have published national guidance. Then watch your inbox from October: the first supplier questionnaire citing this directive is the signal that theory has become contracting.

Every lodge in East Africa has called itself eco for a decade. In 26 days most of them must stop — and the few who can prove it will finally be paid for the difference.
