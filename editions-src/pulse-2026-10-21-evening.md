3️⃣ Nigeria confirmed an imported Ebola case on 20 October in a woman who had travelled to Uganda, which says it has no active cases

WHO's Africa office says lab testing confirmed Bundibugyo virus on 20 October. Symptoms began 7 October, four days after arrival; 44 contacts are monitored and her source is unresolved. WHO again advises against travel or trade restrictions *(WHO AFRO, 20 Oct)*. Uganda's Director General of Health Services, Charles Olaro, said exposure may not have happened in Uganda and that it has "no confirmed active Ebola cases" *(Daily Monitor, 20 Oct)*.
Our inference: a Uganda link risks pulling gorilla-circuit bookings into the scare.
🎯 So what: if you sell Uganda, put Olaro's statement in your agent briefings.

## LINKEDIN

Kenya's Ebola count has moved to nine, and the detail matters more than the number.

On 20 October the Ministry of Health confirmed a 24-year-old relative of the first patient. He was already in quarantine when he fell ill. Director-General Patrick Amoth says all nine cases belong to one cluster, eight patients are stable, and 134 of 135 contacts are traced, with most due out of quarantine from 25 October. Kenya has restricted no travel, trade or gatherings.

Elsewhere the restrictions are widening. Kenyans.co.ke reports that El Salvador has ordered entry refused to people arriving from or through Kenya, Uganda, Rwanda, Ethiopia and four other countries, some with no cases. Nigeria also confirmed an imported case on 20 October in a woman who had travelled to Uganda; Uganda says it has no active cases. WHO advises against travel and trade restrictions.

Our inference: the risk to bookings is now less about Kenya's case count and more about blanket rules from source markets. El Salvador is small, but the pattern is the point.

For owners: find out which source markets restrict entry or apply advisory-level rules, offer date changes before discounts, and give agents one sentence on the traced cluster. Do not call yourself Ebola-free.
