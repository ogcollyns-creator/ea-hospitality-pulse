## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**Your central bank publishes hotel booking data. Almost nobody in the trade reads it.**

Two things landed in East African hospitality today, and only one of them will be reported.

The reportable one: Jambojet is returning to Nairobi–Entebbe after six years away, with daily passenger flights and cargo from 1 October 2026 and one-way fares starting near KSh 22,950 (Bizna Kenya, 21 August 2026). The airline is openly positioning the route as a feeder to Kenya's coast, naming Mombasa, Diani, Malindi and Lamu, and projects roughly 300,000 passengers moving between the two countries this year. New cheap daily capacity is arriving in a corridor that matters.

The unreported one is more useful. The Central Bank of Kenya published its July 2026 Market Perceptions, CEOs and Agriculture surveys today. The headline is unremarkable — inflation expected to hold within the target band despite fuel pressure, optimism sustained over twelve months. What matters sits further down, and it has been sitting there for years.

CBK's survey panel includes 96 hotels. The bank asks them for monthly bed occupancy, restaurant and conferencing activity, and forward bookings — and then publishes the result, by city, free.

Look at what the May 2026 edition actually showed. Nairobi's forward bookings for June read 71, against 45 for the same month a year earlier. Mombasa read 60 against 56. The national average moved 51 to 62. Nairobi is being carried by business events; the coast is drifting along behind it. In the same series, Mombasa's March, April and May 2026 readings all sat *below* their 2025 equivalents — 54 against 58, 44 against 67, 40 against 61 — before June recovered. That is a coast with a soft shoulder and a city with a MICE engine, described in official numbers, published by the central bank, and quoted by essentially no one in the trade press.

I want to be honest about why this matters to us specifically. We have told readers repeatedly that forward bookings and occupancy are figures this brief will not print, because no credible public source produces them for East Africa. That was half right. It is true of the region. It is not true of Kenya, and we should have found this sooner.

The limitations are real and worth stating. It is a diffusion-style index from a panel with a 62% response rate, not an STR-grade occupancy series. It cannot tell you your ADR. It moves on a monetary-policy calendar, not a commercial one. But it is official, it is free, it is city-level, and it is directionally honest — which is more than can be said for the anecdote most rate decisions in this region actually rest on.

Now put the two stories together. Capacity into Uganda–Kenya is being repriced by an airline. Cost is being repriced by governments — Thailand endorsed a THB 450 arrival fee today, its sixth attempt in six years (Skift, 24 August 2026), while Kenya sits on a US0,000 mandatory travel-health insurance rule gazetted on 30 July and still not operational, now under a High Court challenge. Decisions are being made about your demand and your cost base by people who are not you.

The response is not louder commentary. It is measurement. If you cannot say whether your booking pace is running ahead of or behind Nairobi's, you are not negotiating with tour operators in October — you are accepting their number. The data to answer that question was published this afternoon, by the Central Bank of Kenya, and it cost nothing.
