## LINKEDIN

Governments signal intent with org charts before they do it with ribbon-cuttings.

On 30 July, Rwanda's Office of the Prime Minister appointed a dedicated CEO — Jules Muheto Ndenga — for "Airport City," the planned hotel, logistics and commercial district around the new Kigali International Airport at Bugesera. The airport itself is only 25–30% built, with phase one targeted for 2027. Yet the commercial district around it now has its own chief executive, distinct from the aviation holding company that builds the runway.

That sequencing matters for anyone in East African hospitality. You don't give a masterplan its own CEO. You give a project one. Separating the commercial-district leadership from the airport build is how a government moves the hotel and mixed-use component from concept into procurement — while the concrete is still curing airside.

For Kigali operators, this reframes Bugesera from a 2027–28 supply threat into a live counterparty question today: who is the office you'd approach about a plot, a management contract, a serviced-apartment block? As of this week, it has a name.

The read-across for the region: Rwanda continues to treat aviation and tourism infrastructure as a single, sequenced state project — airport, national carrier, MICE, and now an airport city — rather than a series of unrelated tenders. Whether the underwriting keeps pace with the ambition is the open question we keep returning to. But the intent is now staffed, not just drawn.

Watching for the first hotel or commercial tender under the new office over the next 90 days.
