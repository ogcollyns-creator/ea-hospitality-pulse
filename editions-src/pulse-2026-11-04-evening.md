━━━━━━━━━
1️⃣ Uganda has been Ebola-free since 27 August, yet the US order covering it runs out on 10 November

**What is on the record.** CDC's Title 42 order bars non-US nationals who were in the DRC, Uganda or South Sudan in the previous 21 days from entering the United States. The current term took effect on 11 October and runs "through 10 November unless modified or rescinded earlier". Kenya, Rwanda and Tanzania are not covered *(Erickson Immigration Group, 14 Oct)*. Earlier terms ended on 11 September and 11 October, and the October lapse was renewed before it bit *(our 12 Oct edition)*. The DRC had 10,060 cases and 4,887 deaths at 25 October *(our 28 Oct edition)*. Entebbe's international passengers fell 29.1% year on year in August *(UCAA, Aug 2026)*.

**What most readers miss.** The order governs entry to the US, not travel to Uganda. It hits foreign guests with a US onward leg inside 21 days, not Americans arriving at Entebbe. Our inference: renewal is likelier than not while DRC cases keep rising, and Uganda's own clean record does not enter the test. We could not open the Federal Register filing the radar flagged on 3 November, so we do not know whether a renewal is already filed.

🎯 So what: before 10 November, ask Uganda-bound guests with US connections whether their return dates still work, and quote the CDC text, not the headlines.

## LINKEDIN

Uganda has been clear of Ebola since 27 August. The United States still treats it like an active outbreak, and the date that matters is 10 November.

CDC's Title 42 order bars non-US nationals who were in the DRC, Uganda or South Sudan in the previous 21 days from entering the US. The current term runs through 10 November unless modified. Kenya, Rwanda and Tanzania are not covered.

Two points that get lost. First, the order governs entry to the US, not travel to Uganda, so the guests it touches are foreign visitors with a US onward leg inside 21 days. Second, the test appears to follow the DRC, which had 10,060 cases at 25 October, not Uganda's own record. Our inference is that renewal is likelier than not, but we have not seen a notice, and it is a call we have logged.

For operators the move is practical. Ask guests with US connections whether their return dates still work, quote the CDC text rather than headlines, and do not read Entebbe's 29.1% fall in August international passengers as caused by this order; it is consistent with the wider shock, not proof of it.
