## LINKEDIN

Kenya's July inflation just hit a two-year high of 6.5%, and for hotel and lodge operators the interesting part isn't the headline — it's where the pressure sits.

KNBS reported on 31 July that transport prices are up 15.6% year-on-year and food 9.0%, the two lines that flow straight into safari transfers and F&B. The shilling was broadly flat, so this is a domestic cost story, not a currency one.

Set that 6.5% against Uganda and Tanzania, both around 4.0%, and Kenya is now the clear regional cost outlier by roughly two and a half points.

For operators running multi-country itineraries, that gap is no longer academic — it is a margin decision. The move is to re-price the Kenya leg on margin rather than volume, lock transfer and food-supply contracts before they reset again, and read the per-kWh electricity pass-throughs on the July bill, which now add more than KSh 5 per unit and rarely make the headlines.
