## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**Europe's biggest tour operator is down 6%. Zanzibar just had its best month on record. Your pace report can only see one of them.**

On 12 August, TUI reported Summer 2026 booked revenue down 6% season-to-date, with the UK down 8% and Germany down 3%. Two days later, Zanzibar published July arrivals of 107,801 — an all-time monthly record, up 9.6% year on year. If you run a resort on that island, or a lodge that sells alongside it, those two facts arrived in the same week and pointed in opposite directions. Only one of them is visible in your forecast.

**THE SITUATION**

TUI's Q3 was weak on the surface: revenue down 5.6% to €5.8bn, underlying operating profit down 27.1% to €234m, €81m of geopolitical costs absorbed. Management reaffirmed full-year EBIT guidance of €1.1–1.4bn, and the last four weeks of bookings ran 7% ahead. So the European package channel is soft, not collapsing.

Zanzibar, meanwhile, set a record — and the composition matters more than the total. Europe supplied 60,754 of July's arrivals, but its share fell from 61.9% in June to 56.4% in July (OCGS, via The Citizen, 14 August). On our calculation from those figures, non-European arrivals grew roughly 77% month on month against Europe's 41%. The growth was disproportionately African, Gulf and Asian.

Those guests do not book the way a European package guest books. They book later, stay shorter, and arrive through direct and OTA channels rather than through allocation contracted six to nine months out.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

This is a measurement problem before it is a demand problem.

Pace, pick-up, pace-versus-last-year — the instruments most owners actually run rate decisions off — were designed around the tour-operator and allocation book. That book is contracting. The book that is growing is largely invisible to those instruments until roughly three weeks out.

So the pace report shows a hole. The hole is not real. But the discount taken to fill it is entirely real, and it is taken at exactly the moment the property has the least information.

The channel shift makes the instrument worse in a second way. Cloudbeds' 2026 State of Independent Hotels report, published 25 March 2026 from 90 million bookings across 180 countries, put OTA share of independent bookings at 63.4%, up from 61%, with OTA cancellation running at 21.8% against 10.6% for direct. Gross pick-up on the growing channel overstates by more than a fifth. Owners are therefore reading a signal that is simultaneously too quiet early and too loud late.

And there is a trap at the end of it. TUI is not retreating from Zanzibar. Its Winter 2027 programme puts the island at the centre, with the first-ever UK direct package flights from London Gatwick, twice weekly from 3 November 2027 through 22 March 2028. The tour-operator channel returns to this destination at scale in roughly fifteen months. Any owner who signs long allocation now, on terms negotiated in a panic about a phantom hole, will have locked a low net rate into precisely the season when pricing power comes back.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

**Split the pace report, this week.** Two lines, two lead times: allocation and tour-operator on one, direct and OTA on the other. Compare each against its own prior year. A single blended pace line is now measuring a shrinking share of the book and reporting it as the whole book.

**Move the discount trigger off the calendar.** A gap at sixty days out means something different when a growing share of arrivals books inside twenty-one days. Set the trigger on channel-adjusted pace, and net of cancellation, not on days-to-arrival.

**Beach and coast:** aim shoulder inventory — April and May, the 2.68× trough behind July's record — at the segment that actually grew: regional, Gulf and African demand, sold direct, short-stay, on your own channel. That is the only demand that does not obey a European school calendar.

**Bush and safari:** the same distortion applies with a longer lag. Regional and intra-African safari demand is not in your allocation book at all. Price the June–October core off allocation; price everything else off a shorter window.

**City:** hold contract discipline through the October compression — MKTE Nairobi lands 6–8 October with 10,000-plus delegates — and refuse multi-year allocation at 2026 terms.

**THE WATCH**

**Mid-September** — OCGS publishes Zanzibar's August arrivals. Europe below 60% again confirms the dilution is structural rather than a one-month artefact. **11 September** — the US entry order covering Uganda expires; its comment window closes on or about 1 September. **December 2026** — TUI's FY2026 results against the guidance reaffirmed on 12 August; a downgrade would say the softness is demand, not channel.

The demand did not disappear. It changed address, and nobody updated the forecast.
