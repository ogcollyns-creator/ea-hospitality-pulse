Kenya's annual inflation rose to 7.3% from 6.8%, the highest since July 2023, with transport up 17.1% and diesel up 32.5% on a year earlier *(KNBS, 30 Oct)*. Core inflation is only 4.3%. **What everyone is missing:** that split matters more than the headline. The shilling was KSh129.91 to the dollar on 22 October, against 129.81 a week earlier *(CBK, 23 Oct)*. With the currency still, local inflation is dollar cost for anything bought in shillings, so 2027 dollar rates set on spring costs are quietly marked down. That is our inference.

## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**Kenya's biggest 2027 risk is on your cost line, not in the Ebola count**

If you run a lodge or hotel in Kenya, the number that mattered most this week was not a case count. It was 17.1%, the annual rise in transport costs that Kenya's statistics bureau published on 30 October.

**THE SITUATION**

Kenya's annual inflation rose to 7.3% in October from 6.8%, the highest since July 2023. Diesel is up 32.5% on a year earlier and petrol 22.8%. Food is up 10.2%. Core inflation, which strips out food and fuel, is 4.3%, while the non-core measure is 15.1%.

Fuel is the pressure point. EPRA's prices for 15 October to 14 November put diesel at KSh227.76 a litre in Nairobi, up KSh9.90, and petrol at KSh226.96, up KSh12.93. Both carry VAT at 8%. The Tax Laws (Amendment) Act signed on 16 October extends that reduced rate for 90 days from 14 October, which by our arithmetic is about 12 January 2027, with a further extension at the Treasury's discretion.

Meanwhile the shilling is flat. The Central Bank's bulletin showed KSh129.91 to the dollar on 22 October, 10 cents from a week earlier. The same bulletin shows growth in accommodation and food services of 7.4% in the second quarter, down from 14.7% in the first.

**WHAT EVERYONE IS MISSING**

The conversation is about demand: the US Level 3 for Kenya on 19 October, nine Ebola cases, a boarding declaration from 3 November. Those are real. But the cost shock arrives on lines a lodge cannot cut. Core inflation of 4.3% says wages and general services have not moved much. The 15.1% non-core figure says the pain sits in fuel and food, which is transfers, game drives, generators, laundry and kitchens.

Now put that beside a flat shilling. When the currency does not weaken, local inflation turns directly into dollar cost for anything you buy in shillings. A property that fixed 2027 dollar rates on spring costs has, in effect, taken a price cut. That is our inference, not a measured margin figure, and its size depends on how much of your cost base is local fuel and food. The slowdown in accommodation and food growth began in the second quarter, before the Level 3 rise, so the demand story is not the whole story either.

Look at the neighbours before assuming Kenya is alone. Rwanda's urban prices rose 17.2% in September, restaurants and hotels 18.9%. Tanzania's headline is 4.3%, but its transport index is 14.0%, so its edge is in the headline, not in fuel. Uganda's central bank rate is 12.0%. These are local-currency indices, and we have not converted them to dollars.

**WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT**

Bush and Mara lodges: re-cost transfers, vehicle running and generator fuel at today's KSh227.76 diesel, then run a second case with VAT at 16%. Our arithmetic puts that at about KSh244.6, a rise of 7.4%. If your 2027 uplift does not cover the gap, change the rate or the inclusions now.

City hotels in Nairobi: food and beverage is the exposure. Milk is up 14.4% and potatoes 34.9% on the year. Review menu pricing and supplier contracts before the festive trade.

Coast and Zanzibar: Zanzibar's August arrivals were 124,481, up 18%, with Europe at 58.1%. The Americas fell 29%. The island sells on Europe, so Kenya's US advisory is a small part of its exposure. Watch Tanzania's October index in the second week of November.

Everyone: write the fuel-VAT date into your pricing calendar and brief your sales team on it.

**THE WATCH**

The US boarding declaration starts on 3 November. EPRA's next review is on 14 November, with new prices from the 15th. Kenya's November inflation print lands around 30 November, and the Central Bank meets in December. The 90-day fuel VAT mark falls in mid-January.

Demand can return in a season. A cost base does not reverse on that schedule, and your rate card is set in this quarter.
