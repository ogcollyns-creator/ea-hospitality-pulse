## LINKEDIN

The half-year arrivals data is in from across Africa, and it quietly overturns the market most East African properties still build their rate card around.

Start with the cleanest number. South Africa recorded 5.58 million international arrivals in the first half of 2026, up 12.3% (Statistics South Africa, released 30 July). But the growth was lopsided: arrivals from the rest of the African continent rose 14.3%, while overseas arrivals grew 5.6%. More than 70% of monthly arrivals came from SADC neighbours. The story is not the long-haul recovery everyone is waiting on — it is the regional traveller who never left.

East Africa shows the same signal without the same attention. In H1 2026, Rwanda's single largest source market was not the UK, Germany or the US. It was the Democratic Republic of Congo — 185,420 visitors, 12.5% of all arrivals — with Kenya and Tanzania close behind (Rwanda Development Board). Cross-border trade, healthcare, education and family travel are generating demand that is structurally more resilient than any long-haul segment, because it does not evaporate when a Western foreign ministry updates an advisory or the dollar strengthens.

Here is what most operators are missing: regional demand is treated as low-yield walk-in — no distinct rate, no product, no campaign — precisely because it arrives without an OTA booking or a European tour operator attached. That is a pricing failure, not a demand ceiling. The regional guest books on a shorter lead, travels by road or regional air, and blends business with leisure. They are underserved, not low-value.

The move is not to abandon long-haul. It is to build a deliberate regional book alongside it: a weekday cross-border corporate and long-stay rate for city hotels; the African-resident and EAC permit tariff packaged into a real shoulder-season offer for lodges; Gulf and Indian charter demand courted for the beach off-season. The destinations already doing this are the ones posting double-digit growth while the rest wait for Europe to come back.
