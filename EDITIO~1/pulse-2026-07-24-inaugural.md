## LINKEDIN (Evening Wrap flagship style)

Nairobi will add roughly 1,500 branded hotel rooms this year. Most operators will respond by cutting rates. That's the wrong move.

The numbers are stark: 20 branded hotels and 3,650 rooms in the pipeline, per W Hospitality Group — in a market where 2,000 new rooms since 2023 already pushed occupancy down nearly 10 percentage points.

But here's what the oversupply headline misses: demand is diversifying faster than at any point in a decade. Middle East and Asian source markets, regional business travel, Nairobi's growing UN and diplomatic footprint. The problem isn't too many rooms — it's too many identical rooms chasing the same corporate guest.

The operators who will win 2027 are doing three things now: locking multi-year corporate and MICE contracts before new entrants buy that business with opening rates; repositioning product toward underserved formats (extended-stay, lifestyle, branded residences); and holding rate discipline — because in a discount war with a new hotel's marketing budget, the incumbent loses.

Supply waves don't sink markets. They sink undifferentiated properties.
