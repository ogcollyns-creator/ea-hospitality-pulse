Republish EA Hospitality Pulse

Free syndication for associations, trade press and member newsletters — no cost, no exclusivity.

The offer

EA Hospitality Pulse is editorially independent and free to read. You're welcome to republish any edition, guide or data snapshot in full or in part — in a newsletter, WhatsApp group, member portal or trade publication — under one condition: credit us and link back. That's the whole arrangement. No fee, no approval process, no exclusivity required.

Why we do this: the audience that matters to us already gathers in associations, agent networks and trade press. We'd rather your members read this in the place they already check daily than ask them to find us separately.

Three ways to take it

1. RSS feedPoint your newsletter tool or CMS at our feed and new editions appear automatically — no manual copying, ever.
2. Copy an editionPaste any edition's text into your own newsletter or bulletin, with the attribution line below.
3. Link onlyJust link to the edition or guide on our site. Simplest option, and it's what helps our search visibility most.

1. Subscribe to the RSS feed

Every new edition — Morning Brief, Midday Pulse, Evening Wrap and the Sunday Foresight — publishes to this feed automatically, usually within minutes of going live.

https://eahospitalitypulse.com/feed.xml

Works with Mailchimp's RSS-to-email, WordPress feed plugins, Feedly, and most newsletter platforms' "auto-import" features.

2. Attribution line

If you're copying text rather than linking, include this line at the top or bottom of the republished item:

Republished from EA Hospitality Pulse (eahospitalitypulse.com), an independent daily intelligence brief for East African hospitality.

What we ask in return

  • Keep the attribution line and link intact — don't republish anonymously.
  • Don't alter the substance of an item. Trimming for length is fine; changing a claim, a date or a source isn't.
  • If you're a trade publication citing our Rate Index, Cost-Side Index or Foresight Ledger, link to the specific page rather than the homepage — it's more useful to your readers and it's how we track which data gets used.
