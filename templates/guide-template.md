Open with the one thing a reader needs to know in the first two sentences — this line renders larger on the page, so make it earn that space.

## Use ## for section headings

Write in plain paragraphs. Short ones. This isn't the daily Pulse format — no emoji headers, no numbered items, no "so what" tags. It's a reference piece: something a reader might come back to and reread months later, so it should hold up without today's date attached to it.

- Bullet lists work
- So do numbered lists
- And > blockquotes for a pulled-out point

A table, if the data needs one:

| Channel | Typical commission |
| --- | --- |
| OTA | 15–25% |
| Direct | 0–3% payment processing |

**Bold** and *italic* both work, and so do [links](https://example.com).

Close with something concrete — a takeaway, a checklist, a "here's what I'd do differently."

## Before you open a pull request

- **Category** should be one of: City, Bush, Beach, Risk, Distribution, Regulatory, Cost, Operations — pick whichever fits best, or ask if none do.
- **Every factual claim needs a named, checkable source** — a report, an official notice, a dated news article. No unverifiable "industry sources say."
- **This is a byline piece.** It runs under your name, not the masthead — write it in your own voice, with your own experience where relevant.
- Keep `slug` short, lowercase, hyphenated, and unique — it becomes the page's permanent URL.
- Delete this "Before you open a pull request" section before submitting; it's guidance, not part of the published piece.
