# East and Central Africa sent 80,763 international passengers into Nairobi's JKIA in September, more than Europe's 61,893, and every route announced this week adds to that side of the ledger

*EA Hospitality Pulse — Weekly Edition, issue 18. 16–22 November 2026. Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Zanzibar and Rwanda.*

---

KNBS's September indicators put East and Central Africa first among JKIA's arrival regions at 80,763, ahead of Europe at 61,893 and the Middle East at 54,246 (KNBS Leading Economic Indicators, via Kenyans.co.ke, 18 Nov). This week's capacity news ran the same way: Kenya Airways' tenth weekly Mumbai flight, Eswatini Air naming Nairobi, Uganda Airlines' daily Kigali service. The one long-haul move was Tanzania waiving park fees to bring UK agents back, in 2027.

**What everyone is missing**

: this regional growth lands in the city, on daily schedules, and books itself. Most bush and beach inventory is still sold through long-haul tour-operator contracts. The demand gap is really a product and channel gap. (Our inference. September is month-on-month after the August peak, and the press report gives no year-on-year figure.)

## This week's Big Reads

**Uganda's shilling fell 16 per cent this year. Rwanda's franc fell 2. The same dollar rate card now means four different things.**

For two decades East Africa's dollar-priced hotels carried an automatic hedge: when local costs rose, the local currency fell, and the gap closed itself. In 2026 that stopped working in half the region, and Rwanda's October inflation print shows what a property is left holding when it does.

Read it: https://eahospitalitypulse.com/guides/east-africa-currency-cushion-dollar-rate-card

**Tanzania doubled the beds inside its national parks. The 160 sites it has just put on the market are in the parks nobody flies to.**

TANAPA's annual results, released on 10 November, show in-park bed capacity up from 5,755 to 12,094 since 2021, a revenue target missed by about 66 billion shillings, and domestic park visits down 7.7 per cent in a single year. The agency's answer is 160 more investment sites, 130 of them camps, concentrated in a circuit whose access project the government itself asked the World Bank to cancel.

Read it: https://eahospitalitypulse.com/guides/tanzania-in-park-beds-160-sites-nobody-flies-there

**Kenya is first on the list for a levy on business class arrivals. The row of numbers behind it does not add up.**

A COP31 declaration launched in Antalya on Tuesday welcomed small levies on polluting sectors, with Kenya among its backers. Four days earlier the task force Kenya co-chairs published the modelling, and Kenya came first of ten countries at $84 million a year. Work the report's own published figures back and the Kenya row implies a revenue base roughly four times the passenger base it uses to count the cost.

Read it: https://eahospitalitypulse.com/guides/premium-flyer-levy-kenya-row-that-does-not-add-up

**Uganda has cut the gorilla permit price in April, May and November. May was its busiest month of the year.**

A short table in Uganda Wildlife Authority's January 2027 tariff knocks 200 dollars off a gorilla permit in three named months. The Ministry of Tourism's own half-year statistics show one of those months was the single busiest for arrivals, while half the permit book went unsold all year. The discount is keyed to the calendar, and the problem is not calendar-shaped.

Read it: https://eahospitalitypulse.com/guides/uganda-low-season-permit-discount-busiest-month

**India got a daily flight to Tanzania this morning. The guest on it is worth $276 a night less.**

IndiGo's first daily Mumbai to Dar es Salaam service was scheduled to land at 06:05 today, and India entered Tanzania's mainland top fifteen source markets for the first time last year. But package visitors spent $479 a night on the mainland in 2025 and non-package visitors spent $203. A seat bought on an airline website arrives without a package, and the constraint East Africa keeps calling connectivity is really conversion.

Read it: https://eahospitalitypulse.com/guides/india-daily-flight-tanzania-package-gap

**Uganda counted 1.6 million visitors last year. Its holiday arrivals grew by 0.4 per cent.**

The World Bank's November tourism brief puts Uganda down 7 per cent on the quarter while Rwanda is up 19. Uganda's own ministry says arrivals rose 3.7 per cent in the same half-year, its civil aviation authority says air traffic fell 10.8 per cent, and its park gates counted 7.9 per cent fewer people. All four numbers are real. Only one of them describes the market a lodge sells into.
