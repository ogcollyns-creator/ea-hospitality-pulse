# Kenya's Ebola-free date is 23 December, but Uganda's 28 July all-clear took 106 days to reopen UAE visas and has not moved the US

*EA Hospitality Pulse — Weekly Edition, issue 17. 9–15 November 2026. Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Zanzibar and Rwanda.*

---

Kenya discharged its last Ebola patient on 11 November. If no case follows, it can be declared Ebola-free on 23 December (Health Ministry, via Africanews, 11 Nov). Owners now have a date. It is the wrong date to plan around.

**What everyone is missing**

: Uganda ran this test first. Its ministry declared the outbreak over on 28 July; WHO confirmed in late August (Al Jazeera). The UAE eased for Ugandans on 26 September and resumed visas on 11 November, 106 days later, and travellers must still be 21 days out of Uganda (The National, 11 Nov). The US reissued Uganda's Level 4 on 9 November and renewed its entry order to 10 December. The health clock is the shortest one. Demand runs on the others. Our inference, from one precedent: Gulf demand for Kenya returns in late Q1 2027, not at Christmas.

**The Mara's bed ceiling is absolute on paper. Nobody has got a court to look at it.**

W Hospitality Group's new pipeline report names the Maasai Mara and the Serengeti among sub-Saharan Africa's luxury development hotspots, and asks whether they can absorb the supply. Kenya's own management plan answered that question in 2023. The answers that have actually counted since then have all been procedural.

**Zanzibar's hotels will argue about the 30 per cent. The expensive word in the order is "contract".**

On 31 October Zanzibar raised its private sector wage floor by about 30 per cent from 1 January and lifted the dollar floor for foreign workers by half. In the same announcement the Labour Commissioner was told to put every private sector worker on a written contract by December. Over the twelve months to October the islands' advertised room rate fell about 18 per cent.

Read it: https://eahospitalitypulse.com/guides/zanzibar-wage-floor-contract-is-the-cost

**Zanzibar's hotels have five weeks to install CCTV. Nobody has produced the order.**

Three accounts published in two days gave the archipelago's hotels a 15 December deadline, a camera specification and a schedule of penalties. None of them cites a circular, a gazette notice or a named official, and they disagree on the one question that decides the price.
