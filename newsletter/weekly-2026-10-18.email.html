The Sunday Foresight

Rwanda won Formula One for the 2030s in the week its prices ran 17.2% above last year's, and East Africa's 2027 cost base now spans 4.3% to 17.2%

This week the region sold the next decade. Formula One signed Rwanda for ten years, circuit due early next decade (13 Oct). EAC ministers adopted a 2027–2036 marketing strategy (15 Oct). The same week's statistics describe 2027: urban inflation of 17.2% in Rwanda (NISR, September), 6.8% in Kenya (KNBS, 30 Sep) and 4.3% in Tanzania (NBS, 8 Oct).

What everyone is missing

: nobody sets the cost bases side by side. Rates are quoted in dollars; payroll, power, fuel and food are paid in local currency. A 13-point inflation gap is a margin gap on the same US$ rate, unless the exchange rate offsets it. We hold no Rwandan franc data to say it does (our inference).

The week's signals
1
RWANDA'S PRICES ARE RISING FOUR TIMES AS FAST AS TANZANIA'S

Rwanda's urban CPI was 17.2% in September, up from 15.7%; national CPI was 20.0%. Restaurants and hotels rose 18.9% on the year and the energy index 45% (NISR, reported 12 Oct). Tanzania held at 4.3% (NBS, 8 Oct). Uganda's latest found is 4.1% for August (4 Sep).

So what — put a cost-review date in 2027 Rwanda contracts; price Tanzania as the low-cost base.

This week's Big Reads
Someone else is selling your lodge under your own name. The defences were built for other countries.

A report published on 9 October put 1.1 million brand-hijacking adverts against 204,000 hotels in 205 countries. East African properties are already on the pages those adverts lead to, and the three remedies working elsewhere are the three that travel worst.

Zanzibar's taxman is moving into the booking system. The levy is not the expensive part.

Zanzibar's revenue authority published an amendment to its Virtual Fiscal Management System regulations on 1 September requiring accommodation providers to run an approved property management system wired into the state's platform. Operators are being told the window closes in weeks, by their software vendors rather than by a public notice. The consequence that matters is not the tax rate, it is that occupancy and achieved rate become a government record before they are a management report.

Kenya is about to re-grade its hotels. The stars being sold today were set in 2018.

The Tourism Regulatory Authority starts its national classification exercise in Nairobi and Kiambu on 26 October, eight years after the last one. Kenya is the only EAC state with more four-star hotels than three-star, and its public register of classified hotels currently returns a single record named Test Entity 1.

The most movable money in travel is shopping for a destination. Kenya has been bidding with a building site.

Nine in ten meeting planners expect to spend more in 2027 and four in ten have already moved an event's destination under cost and geopolitical pressure. Africa holds about 4% of the world's association meetings, and the two East African capitals chasing the segment this week are chasing two very different versions of it.

The Gulf added a third daily flight to Nairobi while its own hub was losing a third of its passengers. That is not confidence, it is surplus.

Dubai International carried 31% fewer passengers in the first half of 2026 and Middle East arrivals fell 22%, yet Emirates put a third daily service into Nairobi on 1 July and signed a marketing agreement with the Kenya Tourism Board in September. Kenya is measuring the deal by Middle Eastern visitors, a market worth 20,480 arrivals. The thing it has actually bought is a transfer desk, and the price is good because the aircraft has nowhere better to be.

The breach happened at the software company. Under three East African laws, it is the hotel that has to report it.

A French hotel software publisher lost roughly 135GB of guest records from about 1,500 establishments between 27 September and 5 October, and the fraud calls began the next day carrying real booking references. The East African exposure is not French, it is structural. Kenya gives a hotel 72 hours from the moment it finds out and gives its software vendor 48 hours to tell it, Tanzania gives no number at all, and Uganda says immediately. Hospitality is on Kenya's register-regardless-of-size list, and the sector's instinctive answer to a data penalty so far has been a sign at the door.

East Africa renewed its brand for another ten years. The visa that would make it one destination is still a study.

EAC tourism ministers adopted a ten-year marketing strategy in Arusha last Thursday under a slogan the region already had. The single tourist visa that would let an operator actually sell the region got two more deadlines and no decision.
