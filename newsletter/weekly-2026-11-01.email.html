The Sunday Foresight

Kenya's inflation hit 7.3% on 17.1% dearer transport, and with the shilling flat, 2027 dollar rates are losing margin before Ebola costs a single booking

The month's argument has been about demand: Level 3, nine Ebola cases, a boarding form. The number that landed on 30 October was about cost. Kenya's annual inflation rose to 7.3% from 6.8%, the highest since July 2023, with transport up 17.1% and diesel up 32.5% on a year earlier (KNBS, 30 Oct). Core inflation is only 4.3%. Non-core, meaning food and fuel, is 15.1%.

What everyone is missing

: that split matters more than the headline. A fuel-and-food shock is not a wage shock, so it lands on lines a lodge cannot trim: transfers, game drives, generators, provisions. The shilling was KSh129.91 to the dollar on 22 October, against 129.81 a week earlier (CBK, 23 Oct). With the currency still, local inflation is dollar cost for anything bought in shillings, so 2027 dollar rates set on spring costs are quietly marked down. That is our inference.

This week's Big Reads
Zanzibar got the seats it asked for. The room rate fell 18 per cent.

Airline capacity into Zanzibar rose 11 per cent in a year and is forecast to rise another 33 per cent by March. August set an arrivals record at an estimated 92.9 per cent bed occupancy. Over the same twelve months the weighted average online room rate fell about 18 per cent, in every category. East Africa has spent three years arguing that seats are the binding constraint. One island has now run the experiment.

East Africa's lodges hedged the room rate. The plate is the open position.

Kenya's maize harvest has fallen to a seven-year low, food inflation is running at 9.5 per cent, and the strongest El Nino since 1950 is landing on the region's granary. Full-board rates for 2027 are already contracted. The cost inside them is not.

Nobody will ask your lodge to change its name. That is what the brands are now selling East Africa.

On one Tuesday last week the world's hotel groups told investors that their growth now comes from buildings they do not own and names they do not change. East Africa's independents are the buildings, and the fine print is about distribution, not identity.

Africa is now 47 per cent of Kenya's arrivals. The rate card is still written for Europe.

Seventy Ugandan hoteliers spent six days on the Kenyan coast this week and three trade associations signed memoranda in Mombasa, but the region's largest source market is the one East Africa's commercial architecture, priced in dollars and sold through overseas intermediaries, is least equipped to serve.

East Africa is counting cruise ships. The number that pays is bed nights.

Mombasa's record 2025/26 cruise season delivered 4,889 passengers across nine calls, under four per cent of a single month's air arrivals into Zanzibar, and almost none of them slept ashore. The published schedule for the season now opening shows four calls and not one turnaround. The region is lobbying for berths when the thing that converts a day call into a bed night is scheduled lift.

Hilton reported its Africa region down 5.6 per cent. It was not measuring Africa.

Three of the four listed hotel majors reported the same overlapping geography this month. Hilton's Middle East and Africa line fell 5.6 per cent and is guided to a low double-digit decline for the year, IHG's wider EMEAA bucket rose 5.0 per cent, and Accor published its numbers twice, once with the Middle East in and once with it out. None of them published a figure for Africa. East Africa is where those same companies are building hardest, and it is the half of a hyphen whose number is set somewhere else.
