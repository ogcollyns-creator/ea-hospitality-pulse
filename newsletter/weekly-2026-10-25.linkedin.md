# Kenya's new US Level 3 will probably outlast its nine Ebola cases: Uganda is still at Level 4, 89 days after declaring its outbreak over

*EA Hospitality Pulse — Weekly Edition, issue 14. 19–25 October 2026. Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Zanzibar and Rwanda.*

---

The US cut Tanzania to Level 2 on 16 Oct and raised Kenya to Level 3 on 19 Oct; Rwanda is at Level 3 and Uganda at Level 4 (State Department, read 25 Oct). The ratings do not follow the cases. Kenya has nine, in one cluster (MoH, 23 Oct). Uganda has none (20 Oct) yet is at Level 4.

**What everyone is missing**

: the argument is whether Kenya's outbreak will grow. The better question is how long the rating outlasts it. Uganda declared its outbreak over on 28 July; the US page still read Level 4 on 25 Oct. Its central bank held an emergency meeting on 14 Oct and raised its rate to 12%, citing weaker tourism receipts among other causes. Kenya following suit is our inference.

**2. UGANDA'S OUTBREAK ENDED, ITS ADVISORY DID NOT, AND ITS CENTRAL BANK CALLED AN EMERGENCY**

The Bank of Uganda raised its rate from 9.75% to 12.0% on 14 Oct, the largest move since 2011, citing energy, the shilling and a US$4.6bn current-account gap, partly from lower tourism receipts (Nairametrics, 14 Oct). The shilling is down over 11% since January (Daily Monitor, 15 Oct).

## This week's Big Reads

**The law that reprices every deposit your lodge takes**

Treasury and the Central Bank spent the first week and a half of October taking public views on a Bill that rewrites the licensing of every company standing between a guest's card and a lodge's bank account. One of the eleven hearings was held in a hotel. The hospitality industry, which has already been taught once this year what a payment rail costs it, was largely somewhere else.

Read it: https://eahospitalitypulse.com/guides/kenya-payments-bill-cost-of-taking-money

**Kenya curated 75 experiences this month. Not one of them can be bought where it is displayed.**

The Magical Kenya Signature Experiences collection grew to 75 entries on 15 October. The public page carries no price, no availability and no booking button, and Kenya's own draft tourism strategy already names the missing piece.

Read it: https://eahospitalitypulse.com/guides/kenya-signature-experiences-no-till

**Nobody at your lodge decided to block the AI agent. Something in your stack did.**

On 18 October an AI agent working for a traveller could not see a rate at a Wyndham hotel. On 19 October it reached the guest details page. Nothing in Wyndham's published policy changed overnight, and that is the point: access to hotel inventory is now decided by bot-management software rather than by anyone's commercial judgement. East African operators have never looked at what their own stack does.

Read it: https://eahospitalitypulse.com/guides/ai-agent-access-east-africa-lodges

**For fifteen years the guest has paid for Kenya's safari land. Maktau just signed up a second payer.**

At a KWS field station in Tsavo West on 21 October, Kenya closed a 160 million dollar Project Finance for Permanence deal covering 17.3 million hectares over ten years. That is about 92 US cents per hectare per year: small against the land, and larger than anything the conservancy model has ever had. The conditions attached matter more than the headline.
