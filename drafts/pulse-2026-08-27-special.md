Parts 1 and 2 corrected the headline and the MICE arithmetic. Part 3 is about three sentences the report published against its own interest.

**1️⃣ KWS SAYS ITS OWN FEE RISE DROVE DOMESTIC VISITORS AWAY**

Park visitation hit **3,543,553**, up **11%** — international +**18%**, domestic +**8%**. A good year, on the face of it.

Then this, verbatim: domestic numbers *"plunged during the last quarter of the year as a result of introduction of the reviewed conservation fees in July 2025."*

The monthly table backs it. **November recorded 191,010 — the lowest month of 2025**, below January (214,558) and February (221,417), in what should be a run-up to peak. It was KWS's **first fee review in 18 years**.

🎯 **So what:** a state agency has documented its own price elasticity in writing. Domestic demand broke at the new gate price within one quarter. Any 2027 model assuming residents absorb the next increase now has a published counter-example. Build resident rates that absorb gate fees rather than stacking on them. 🏷 Bush · KE · Confirmed

**2️⃣ "MULTIPLE MID-TIER HOTELS BEING PLACED UNDER RECEIVERSHIP"**

That sits in the tax chapter, explaining corporate income tax growth of just **4.9%**. The report blames accommodation enterprises facing *"competition from short-term rentals such as Airbnb, with multiple mid-tier hotels being placed under receivership."*

The tax series agrees. Earnings grew **10.55%**; VAT on accommodation and food **6.52%**; CIT **4.87%**. Reported earnings are outrunning remitted tax. Tour operators and travel agencies, meanwhile, grew VAT **26.58%**.

🎯 **So what:** value is migrating from mid-tier hotel P&Ls to intermediaries and unregulated stock. Your competitor is not the hotel down the road — it is a serviced apartment with a lower cost base. Compete on what an apartment cannot supply: F&B, service recovery, corporate contracting, security. 🏷 City/Beach · KE · Confirmed

**3️⃣ CRUISE FELL 36% — AND IS STILL IN THE GROWTH PLAN**

Cruise arrivals: 2,490 (2023) → 6,561 (2024) → **4,197 (2025)**. A **36% fall**. The report states it, then lists cruise among the niches to expand in its recommendations, with no explanation of the decline.

**4️⃣ THE 2030 FORECAST IS A STRAIGHT LINE**

Arrivals run 2,935,804 (2026) to 3,963,586 (2030). The increments: **+256,946 · +256,945 · +256,946 · +256,945.** Receipts add **KSh 45.9bn** a year, to the shilling.

Linear extrapolation, described as a "spreadsheet-forecasting tool" — and the confidence band **narrows** with distance (±2.95% in 2026, ±2.18% in 2030). Real forecasts widen.

🎯 **So what:** Kenya's official 2030 target is an assumption of fixed annual addition, not a demand model. It cannot anticipate a shock, a fee change or a competitor. Do not anchor a 2027–2030 investment case to it — and if a lender cites it at you, this is the page to point at. 🏷 All · KE · Confirmed

## LINKEDIN BIG READ

**Kenya's tourism report contains three sentences written against its own interest. They are the most useful thing in it.**

Most government performance reports are read for their headline and discarded. Kenya's Annual Tourism Sector Performance Report for 2025 rewards the opposite approach. Its candid passages are buried, and they are worth more than its summary.

The first concerns price. In July 2025, Kenya Wildlife Service raised conservation fees for the first time in eighteen years, after a pricing study the report describes as using "elasticity analysis" to produce "data-driven pricing" that "minimized demand shocks". Some pages later, the report records what actually happened: domestic visitor numbers "plunged during the last quarter of the year as a result of introduction of the reviewed conservation fees in July 2025".

The monthly data confirms it. November 2025 recorded 191,010 park visitors — the weakest month of the year, below January and February, during what should be a build toward the festive season. Park visitation still grew 11% overall, carried by international arrivals up 18%. But domestic demand, the segment Kenya has spent a decade cultivating, met the new gate price and stopped.

This is unusual and valuable. A state agency has published its own elasticity result. Any operator being told that Kenyan residents will absorb the next increase now has a documented counter-example, produced by the agency setting the price.

The second sentence sits in the tax chapter, explaining why corporate income tax from tourism enterprises grew only 4.9%. The report attributes it to accommodation businesses facing "competition from short-term rentals such as Airbnb, with multiple mid-tier hotels being placed under receivership."

That is a government acknowledgement of structural distress in the mid-market, and the tax series supports it. Sector earnings grew 10.55% in 2025. VAT from accommodation and food services grew 6.52%. Corporate income tax grew 4.87%. Meanwhile VAT from tour operators and travel agencies grew 26.58%. Reported earnings are outrunning remitted tax, and the intermediaries are capturing the difference. Value is leaving mid-tier hotel P&Ls — toward agents at one end and unregulated stock at the other.

The third is a matter of method rather than candour. The report projects arrivals to 2030: 2,935,804 next year, 3,963,586 by 2030. The annual increments are +256,946, +256,945, +256,946, +256,945. Receipts rise by KSh 45.9 billion each year, to the shilling. This is straight-line extrapolation presented as forecast, and its confidence band narrows with distance — plus or minus 2.95% in 2026, 2.18% by 2030. Real forecasts widen as the horizon lengthens, because the future is less knowable, not more.

None of this is an argument that Kenyan tourism is weak. Arrivals and earnings both grew. It is an argument about what the sector knows about itself. A destination that can document its own price shock and its own receiverships is capable of honest analysis. The same document then forecasts the next five years with a constant and a ruler.

For operators the practical conclusion is narrow. Build resident rates that absorb gate fees rather than stacking on top of them. If you are mid-market, benchmark against serviced apartments rather than hotels. And do not anchor a 2027 investment case to a projection that cannot represent a shock — including the one the same report just described.
