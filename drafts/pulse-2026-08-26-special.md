**📕 SPECIAL PART 2 OF 3 — THE MICE NUMBER THAT COLLAPSES WHEN YOU DIVIDE IT, AND THE 27% NOBODY PRINTED**

Kenya's 2025 tourism report sells two stories hard: business tourism is booming, and accommodation is thriving. Both survive only until you do arithmetic the report doesn't do.

**1️⃣ KENYA HELD 998 "INTERNATIONAL CONFERENCES" IN 2025. THEY AVERAGED 39 DELEGATES EACH.**

The report gives both numbers but never divides them: **998 international conferences** and **38,705 international delegates** (p.61–62). That is **38.8 delegates per conference**. In 2024 it was 37.9 across 987 conferences. Two years, no change.

Local is the same shape: **12,671 conferences, 758,278 delegates — 59.8 per event.**

An international conference averaging 39 delegates is a boardroom booking, not a congress. It fills a meeting room and perhaps twenty bedrooms. It does not compress a city.

**And note the contradiction.** Chapter 2 puts business/conference at **19%** of arrivals. Chapter 5 claims **25.2% (643,068)**. Yet 643,068 ÷ 2,652,540 = **24.24%** — and 25.2% implies a base of 2,551,857, not the report's own total. Three numbers for one variable, in one document.

🎯 **So what:** if your 2027 budget assumes Nairobi MICE compression, size it off 39 delegates a conference, not off the word "conference". Chase the *volume* of small corporate meetings — 12,671 local events is a real and growing market — with meeting-room packages and day-delegate rates, not blanket BAR lifts. 🏷 City · KE · Confirmed

**2️⃣ NATIONAL BED OCCUPANCY WAS 26.8%.**

The report publishes bed capacity — **114,524 beds** — and bed-nights sold — **11,200,390** — in separate chapters, and never puts them together. Divide: 11,200,390 ÷ (114,524 × 365) = **26.8%**.

Roughly three-quarters of Kenya's licensed bed stock sat empty on an average night in 2025.

The mix is moving, though, and in one direction: **international bed-nights +12.2%** (5,344,973 → 5,999,056) against **domestic +5.8%** (4,917,153 → 5,201,334). International now outsells domestic 5.999m to 5.201m.

🎯 **So what:** the constraint is not demand growth, it is *where capacity sits*. Kilifi, Mombasa and Nakuru lead, Kwale and Narok follow — and the report notes Nairobi does **not** surpass the leading coastal counties. In a saturated coastal cluster, a 7% national arrivals rise never reaches you. Compete on segment and season, not the national number. 🏷 All · KE · Confirmed

**3️⃣ WHERE THE REGIONAL GUEST ACTUALLY GOES**

EAC arrivals ran ~732,600. Entry split: **JKIA 248,341 (33.9%)**, **Busia 164,582 (22.5%)**, **Namanga 110,075 (15%)**, then Malaba, Lungalunga, Taveta, Lwakhakha, Isebania. Two-thirds arrive **overland**.

Their purpose is work: EAC business/conference **47%**, VFR **31.7%** — Rwanda 49.1% business, Tanzania 42.1%, Uganda 41.4%. Somalia is the outlier at **60.5% VFR**.

🎯 **So what:** the fastest-growing inbound cohort arrives by road, on business, and never sees an airport lounge. Border-town and corridor properties — Busia, Malaba, Namanga, Taveta — are serving a 730,000-person market that the national marketing spend ignores entirely. Price midweek corporate, not weekend leisure. 🏷 City · KE/EAC · Confirmed

## LINKEDIN

Kenya's 2025 tourism report says the country hosted 998 international conferences. It also says those conferences drew 38,705 international delegates. It never divides one by the other.

38.8 delegates per conference. In 2024 it was 37.9 across 987 events. Two years, essentially no change.

An international conference averaging 39 people is a boardroom booking. It fills a meeting room and maybe twenty bedrooms. It is not the congress business that justifies a convention-centre strategy, and it is not what "998 international conferences" sounds like in a press release.

The same report contains three different figures for business and conference travel: 19% of arrivals in one chapter, 25.2% in another, and an absolute number that works out to 24.24% of the stated total.

There is a second uncomputed ratio. The report publishes bed capacity of 114,524 and bed-nights sold of 11,200,390, in separate chapters. That is 26.8% national bed occupancy. Three-quarters of Kenya's licensed beds were empty on an average night.

Neither finding says the sector is failing. Both say the sector is being described in units that flatter it. If you budget against the description rather than the arithmetic, you will build for congresses that are not coming and assume a market tightness that does not exist.
