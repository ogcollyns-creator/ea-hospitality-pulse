Methodology

How the data behind EA Hospitality Pulse is built, verified and updated.

← Back to the Pulse

Why this page exists

A market-intelligence product that asks you to trust its numbers should show its work. This page explains, plainly, how each dataset is built — what counts as a source, how confidence is graded, and what a stat on the homepage actually means before you act on it.

This page describes the current build. Several of these datasets are genuinely early — coverage figures below are accurate as of this writing and will read differently as they mature. We'd rather show a small, honest number than a rounded-up one.

EA Pulse Rate Index

A fixed basket of 40 properties across six East African markets (Nairobi, the Kenyan coast, Zanzibar, Kigali, Kampala, and the safari circuit), tracked weekly at a constant booking convention so that a change in the index reflects a change in rate — not a change in what was measured.

The convention, fixed for every observation

  • Length of stay: 2 nights
  • Lead time: 30 days ahead of the observation date
  • Occupancy: 2 adults
  • Currency: USD

How a weekly figure is calculated

Each market's figure is the median of that week's observed lead-in rates across its basket, not the mean — a median is far less distorted by one outlier property having a promotional rate that week. A market's week is only shown as confident once at least 3 observations (minN) were collected for it that week; below that, the figure is published but flagged unconfident, because a sample of one or two properties isn't yet a market read.

Where the basket came from

Properties were selected to span each market's actual segment mix — city business hotels, safari lodges and camps across price tiers, and beach resorts — rather than skewing toward the largest or most marketed names. The basket is fixed once set; we don't swap a property out because its rate moved in an inconvenient direction. Basket changes, when they happen, are logged with a reason.

Cost-Side Index

A weekly sweep of the inputs that move a property's margin rather than its top line — fuel, currency, energy and food costs across the five markets. Currently 14 tracked line items. Every figure carries a named source and a date; where a figure is a same-day reported number rather than an official release, that distinction is stated rather than blurred.

Rules, Fees & Levies Tracker

Every entry rule, permit price, park fee, levy and surcharge we track across the five markets is marked verified or needs re-confirmation. As of this writing, 19 of 23 entries are currently verified against a primary source (a gazette, a regulator's own page, or an official fee schedule) within our re-check window; the remainder are marked to flag that they should be re-confirmed with the official portal before being quoted to a client. We would rather show that flag honestly than let a stale fee sit uncorrected on the page.

Confidence grading — every claim, every edition

Every item we publish is graded, and the grade is printed on the item itself:

GradeWhat it means
ConfirmedVerified against a primary or official source — a regulator's own notice, a government gazette, an airline's own schedule filing.
ReportedCarried by credible press but not yet independently confirmed against a primary source.
Early signalAn upstream indicator — a filing, a schedule change, a procurement notice — that suggests something is coming before it's been reported anywhere. Labelled as inference, not fact, until it resolves.

A recency gate runs before anything publishes: every item must carry a publication date, an event date inside the edition's coverage window, and pass a novelty check — if a well-informed operator already knows it, it's catch-up, not intelligence. Evergreen pages, undated aggregators and recycled press releases are treated as stale until proven otherwise, and we chase a claim back to its primary source before trusting the date a search engine shows for it.

Foresight Ledger — how a forecast gets scored

Every forward call we publish is logged with a falsifiable resolution test and a resolve-by date, before we know the outcome. As of this writing: 43 calls logged, 41 still open, 2 resolved (1 correct, 1 partial, 0 incorrect) — a 75% accuracy figure on a genuinely small resolved sample so far. We show that number rather than waiting for a rounder one, because the point of a public ledger is that you can check it at any stage, not just when it looks good.

A call is only logged if its resolution criteria name a specific, checkable source in advance — "confirmed by KNBS's next release" or "resolved by the official gazette notice" — not a vague future vibe check. When a call resolves, the evidence and source are published alongside the verdict, including on calls we got wrong or partially wrong. Nothing is quietly removed or re-worded after the fact; corrections are published as corrections.

Sourcing hierarchy, in order

  1. Primary/official sources — government gazettes, regulator notices, airline schedule filings, statistics bureau releases, park and conservation authorities
  2. Named credible press — regional and international outlets, attributed by name and date
  3. Aggregators and secondary coverage — used only to locate a story, never cited as the source; we chase every claim back to where it originated

Undated content, listicles, evergreen SEO pages and copyright-year-only pages are not treated as sources regardless of how they rank in search.
