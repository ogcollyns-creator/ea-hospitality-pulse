**Kenya's suspected Ebola case in Wajir tested negative and positive for malaria, leaving one confirmed case in the country**
_🌆 Evening Wrap · Fri 9 Oct 2026_

1️⃣ Wajir is cleared, and Kenya's count stays at one

Health CS Duale said the Wajir patient tested negative for Ebola and positive for malaria, and was put on antimalarials on Thursday night. Kenya's only confirmed case is the Kenyan who lived in the DRC for seven years and died at Nairobi Hospital on 6 Oct. Entry-point screening continues; the hotline is 719 *(Capital FM, 9 Oct)*. That report also showed the man passed temperature checks at Entebbe and Nairobi, so the scrutiny now falls on screening, not borders.
🎯 So what: drop the Wajir caveat from agent and guest messaging. Say "one imported case, no second confirmed case published".

2️⃣ Parliament has opened comment on the Tax Laws (Amendment) Bill 2026, which sets VAT on petroleum at 8%

The Clerk's notice of 9 Oct proposes cutting VAT on petroleum products from the prescribed 16% to 8%, amending the VAT and Excise Duty Acts. Comments close Tue 13 Oct, 5pm (cna@parliament.go.ke). Kenyans.co.ke says the 8% rate has applied since 16 April and does not say how the bill relates to it *(Kenyans.co.ke, 9 Oct)*. The earlier cut from 13% to 8% took Sh9.37 off a litre of petrol and Sh10.21 off diesel *(EPRA, as cited)*.
Our inference: this looks like the statutory route for the 8% rate, which makes our earlier "ends 14 Oct" line uncertain. We found no Order or Gazette notice.
🎯 So what: keep Kenya vehicle and generator costs at today's pump prices until EPRA's 14 Oct review, and file comment through the Kenya Tourism Federation by Tuesday if fuel is a top-three cost.

## LINKEDIN

Kenya's fuel tax is back before Parliament, and the comment window is four days.

On 9 October the National Assembly Clerk published notice of the Tax Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026. It proposes VAT on petroleum products at 8%, against the prescribed 16%, and amends the VAT and Excise Duty Acts. Comments close Tuesday 13 October at 5pm. Kenyans.co.ke reports that 8% has applied since 16 April and does not say how the bill relates to it.

The last cut, from 13% to 8%, took Sh9.37 off a litre of petrol and Sh10.21 off diesel, according to EPRA figures the paper cites. For a lodge running daily game drives and a generator, that is a line item, not a rounding error.

The inference is ours: the bill looks like the statutory route for the 8% rate, so the 14 October date we had been watching is now less certain. We found no Order or Gazette notice either way.

For operators, the practical step is to hold Kenya vehicle and generator costs at today's pump prices until EPRA's review on 14 October, then re-cost. If fuel is a top-three cost, a short submission through your tourism association is cheap.
