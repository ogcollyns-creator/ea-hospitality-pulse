Washington Already Writes Uganda in the Past Tense — and Keeps It Restricted Anyway.

The order that keeps Uganda restricted was filed on Friday and publishes tomorrow. We've read it. Uganda's 28 August all-clear is not what releases it.

1️⃣ 🇺🇬 WASHINGTON ALREADY WRITES UGANDA IN THE PAST TENSE — AND KEEPS IT RESTRICTED ANYWAY

The full text of the US entry order signed on 12 August went on public inspection at the Federal Register on 14 August, 08:45 and publishes 17 August (FR Doc 2026-16706, docket CDC-2026-0892). We read it this morning on public inspection; we haven't seen it reported anywhere yet.

Two things in it matter more than the renewal we covered on 13 August.

First, the operative finding. The determination reads that Ebola is "confirmed currently present in DRC and recently present in Uganda." Past tense — in a document that keeps Uganda on the restricted list. The order records Uganda's last confirmed case as 21 June, 20 confirmed cases and two deaths in total, and the Ministry of Health declaration of 28 July. It then keeps Uganda in on a stated ground that has nothing to do with Uganda's epidemiology: "continued overland travel from DRC poses an ongoing risk of cross-border transmission, particularly among healthcare workers and in western Ugandan districts."

What everyone is missing: the release condition is a DRC number, not a Ugandan one. The order names contact tracing at ~78% nationwide against a 95% operational threshold, says the true outbreak size may be "two to four times greater" than reported, and puts DRC at 4,318 confirmed cases and 2,011 deaths across 53 health zones as of 9 August — against 1,873 and 672 across 41 zones when the July order was signed. Uganda's 42-day clock completing around 28 August does not move any of those. Expiry is 4:59pm EDT, Friday 11 September.

Second, there is a door, and nobody has walked through it. Publication on 17 August opens a 15-day comment period on docket CDC-2026-0892 — closing on or about 1 September, ahead of the 11 September call. The order records the comments received so far: three on the original, five in June, and on the July renewal, none. The East African trade has never filed.

🏷 Uganda/Regional · Bush/City · Confirmed (FR Doc 2026-16706, public inspection 14 Aug) / Inference (release-condition read)
🎯 So what: Stop selling 28 August as the turning point — brief agents that the US rule is priced to run to 11 September, and rebook US arrivals accordingly. If you want Uganda decoupled, the comment docket is the only formal channel into the risk assessment that decides it, it costs nothing, and it closes ~1 September.

2️⃣ 🌍 TWO OFFICIAL CASE COUNTS FOR THE SAME DAY — 63 APART

Worth knowing before you quote a number to an agent or an insurer. For 9 August, the US order states DRC at 4,318 confirmed cases across 53 health zones. WHO AFRO's Weekly External Situation Report 13, same reporting date, states 4,381 confirmed cases and 2,011 deaths, a case fatality ratio of 45.9%, with Ituri accounting for 85.8% of cumulative cases (afro.who.int, posted 11 Aug).

Deaths agree exactly. Cases differ by 63. Neither is wrong — the US figure is a snapshot taken for an American risk assessment; WHO AFRO's is the regional surveillance line. But they will be quoted against each other in the coming fortnight, and being caught with the lower one looks like minimising.

🏷 Regional · Bush/City · Confirmed (both documents, dated)
🎯 So what: Standardise on WHO AFRO for any client-facing figure, cite it with the report number and date, and never quote an outbreak number without one.

━━━━━━━━━
✅ STILL TRUE — The order leaves the CDC travel health notice for all of Uganda at Level 2 (practice enhanced precautions), with Level 4 confined to Ituri and North Kivu in DRC (escalated 4 Aug) and Level 3 to South Kivu, Haut-Uélé and Tshopo. Also unchanged since the July order: Nairobi JKIA is named as a transit corridor of concern and Kenya itself is not restricted. Nothing here has moved. Don't discount as though it had — and don't let a risk team read old text as new.

⚡ COST PULSE — Uganda inflation 3.7% annual (UBOS, June 2026), but liquid energy fuels ran +26.2% year-on-year, pushing passenger transport to 11.9%. Ugandan lodge transfer and generator lines are rising faster than the headline suggests.
